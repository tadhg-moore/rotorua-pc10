Parameterising light extinction (Kw) for a GLM-AED climate scenario run

Motivation

Lake Rotorua’s GLM-AED setup needs a light extinction (Kw) parameterisation. The options considered were:

  1. A single static Kw (one value for the whole run).
  2. A monthly Kw climatology (12 calendar-month values, time-varying).
  3. A fully dated time-varying Kw built from the observed record.
  4. A decomposed background Kw (observed Kw minus the fraction attributable to phytoplankton/turbidity), for use when AED’s own biology should generate the biotic part of light extinction dynamically.

This document works through the diagnostics behind that decision for the Rotorua PAR/Kd record, and lands on a recommendation for the climate scenario runs specifically (as opposed to a historical hindcast).

The helper functions used throughout live in R/recommend_light_parameterisation.R.

Static vs. time-varying Kw: the trade-offs

Time-varying Kw (monthly or dated) can capture real seasonal drivers of light attenuation — spring runoff/turbidity pulses, algal self-shading, CDOM inputs, ice-off resuspension — that a single Kw cannot. Where good profile data exist, throwing that signal away by collapsing to one number misrepresents both the clear and the turbid periods.

The trade-offs:

  • More data wrangling and another forcing file to QC.
  • Monthly resolution still misses short-lived bloom/storm spikes.
  • If Kw has been used as a tuning knob to compensate for other model deficiencies (mixing scheme, inflow temperatures), imposing an independently-derived time-varying Kw removes that degree of freedom and can expose calibration problems elsewhere.
  • A poorly-constrained time-varying curve (built on noisy or sparse data) is worse than a well-chosen static value.

Static Kw is defensible when extinction is dominated by relatively stable factors, when supporting data are too sparse/noisy to justify more resolution, or — as turns out to matter most here — when there is no calendar correspondence between the simulated period and the observed record, which is exactly the case for a forward climate scenario.

GLM-AED’s own light feedback

When AED is coupled to GLM with bioshade_feedback = .true., light extinction is not just the prescribed Kw — GLM adds AED’s own simulated biotic/particulate extinction on top, additively (glm_aed.F90, update_light):

par(i) = par(i+1) * EXP( -(Kw + localext_up) * dz(i+1) )
IF (bioshade_feedback) extc(i) = Kw + localext

localext is the sum of whatever AED modules contribute a light-extinction diagnostic — in this setup that includes:

  • aed_phytoplankton — self-shading from simulated chlorophyll.
  • aed_noncohesive — extinction from simulated suspended sediment, Ke_ss(i) * SS_conc(i) summed across size classes. The active configuration:
&aed_noncohesive
   num_ss = 2
   ss_initial = 3, 1
   Ke_ss = 0.06, 0.06
   settling = 1
   w_ss = 0.5, 0.03
   rho_ss = 1800, 1200
   d_ss = 2e-05, 4e-06
   resuspension = 1
   epsilon = 0.02
   tau_0 = 0.04, 0.03
   tau_r = 1
   fs = 1, 1
   Fsed = 0, 0
   decay = 0, 0
   simSedimentMass = .false.
   sed_porosity = 0.3
   diag_level = 10
/

Practical consequence: the prescribed Kw/Kw_file should represent only the background (non-biotic, non-suspended-sediment) extinction — water + CDOM + anything not reproduced by AED’s own state. If a Kw derived from field-measured Kd (which already contains the biotic + SS signal at the time of sampling) is combined with bioshade_feedback and an active aed_noncohesive, the biotic/SS contribution is double-counted, over-attenuating light and biasing production, DO, and thermal structure.

Key finding: interannual variability dominates over seasonality

  • Kd mean 0.71 m⁻¹, CV 0.52, range 0.25–1.92 m⁻¹.
  • Year explains ~47% of total variance — more than the seasonal cycle does. Annual means step from ~0.35–0.43 m⁻¹ (2003–2008) to a noisy 0.32–1.4 m⁻¹ regime from 2009 onward.
  • After removing each year’s own mean, the detrended seasonal ratio is only ~1.6–1.85x, and mostly driven by a handful of bloom/turbidity events rather than a robust recurring monthly pattern.
  • A pooled multi-year monthly climatology would blend a real interannual regime shift with a weak seasonal signal, misrepresenting most individual years — hence the function’s recommendation of a dated time-varying series over a monthly climatology for a hindcast.

Available overlapping chemistry: CHLA (mg/m^3) and Turbidity (NTU) are the two usable predictors of the biotic/particulate extinction component (Secchi is itself just another optical measurement of the same quantity, so it is not used as a predictor).

Interpretation

  • Even after fixing the join (pseudo-replication), R² = 0.067 — Chla and turbidity together explain under 7% of Kd variance. Chla alone is not significant (p = 0.167); only turbidity clears p < 0.05, and its large coefficient combined with an 18% floor-clipping rate suggests the fit is being pulled around by a handful of high-turbidity events rather than reflecting a stable relationship.
  • aed_noncohesive is active in this AED configuration, computing its own suspended-sediment-driven extinction (Ke_ss(i) * SS_conc(i)) dynamically — so, unlike a setup with phytoplankton feedback alone, both the Chla- and turbidity-attributable terms are legitimately things AED will reconstruct, and both are fair to remove from the prescribed background. (Caveat: aed_noncohesive’s simulated SS is a mass concentration state, not the same quantity as measured NTU turbidity — the decision to exclude it is justified, but there’s no guarantee the simulated SS dynamics reproduce the same magnitude/timing the regression attributed to turbidity.)
  • The intercept is the one robust number here: 0.368 m⁻¹ at p = 0.0013, much better constrained than either slope, since intercepts are anchored by the bulk of the data rather than a weak covariate relationship.
  • The Secchi–Kd relationship was also noted as weak, reinforcing that this system’s optical signal is noisy relative to the predictors available.

Recommendation for the climate scenario runs

For a climate scenario parameterisation (not a historical hindcast):

  1. Do not use observed Kd. bioshade_feedback and aed_noncohesive are both active, so a Kw already containing today’s biotic/SS signal would be double-counted against AED’s own dynamic contribution.
  2. Do not use a dated time-varying background. There is no calendar correspondence between a historical sampling date and a future scenario year — the “use the actual dated observations” option only applies to hindcast-style runs over the sampled period.
  3. Do not build a monthly background climatology. The Chla/turbidity regression (R² = 0.067) and the Secchi–Kd relationship are both weak; imposing monthly structure on this background signal risks encoding noise as if it were a genuine recurring seasonal driver.
  4. Use a static background Kw, set from the regression intercept (~0.37 m⁻¹) — the most robust single number the data support. Let bioshade_feedback (phytoplankton) and aed_noncohesive (suspended sediment) supply the dynamic, climate-responsive part of the light environment, rather than freezing it to the historical record. This is also the conceptually correct setup for a scenario run: it lets the lake’s optical response evolve mechanistically under whatever the scenario does to nutrients, temperature, and hydrology, instead of repeating today’s bloom/turbidity patterns indefinitely.

Sensitivity check

Before committing to this, a direct sensitivity comparison is worth running: static background Kw (0.37 m⁻¹) with bioshade_feedback on, vs. the old static observed mean Kw (0.71 m⁻¹) with bioshade_feedback off. If key scenario outputs (surface temperature, stratification onset/breakdown, chlorophyll, hypolimnetic DO) are not materially different between the two, the choice matters less than the analysis above might suggest; if they diverge substantially, it’s worth investing more in constraining the background estimate (e.g. more Chla/TSS sampling, a better SS-turbidity calibration for aed_noncohesive) before running the full scenario suite.

Does AED feedback interact with Kw? Yes — bioshade_feedback (phytoplankton) and aed_noncohesive (suspended sediment) both add their own simulated extinction on top of the prescribed Kw, additively.

-   GLM performs a **daily lookup** (holds the most recent row's value), not sub-daily interpolation — the file needs one row per day you want a distinct value for.
-   `Kw_file` only drives the single scalar `ExtcCoefSW`, so it requires `light_mode = 0`; it has no effect on the per-band `light_extc` array used by `light_mode = 1`.

### Key finding: interannual variability dominates over seasonality

-   Kd mean 0.71 m⁻¹, CV 0.52, range 0.25–1.92 m⁻¹.
-   **Year explains \~47% of total variance** — more than the seasonal cycle does. Annual means step from \~0.35–0.43 m⁻¹ (2003–2008) to a noisy 0.32–1.4 m⁻¹ regime from 2009 onward.
-   After removing each year's own mean, the detrended seasonal ratio is only \~1.6–1.85x, and mostly driven by a handful of bloom/turbidity events rather than a robust recurring monthly pattern.
-   A pooled multi-year **monthly climatology would blend a real interannual regime shift with a weak seasonal signal**, misrepresenting most individual years — hence the function's recommendation of a dated time-varying series over a monthly climatology *for a hindcast*.

## Decomposing observed Kd into background vs. biotic components

Available overlapping chemistry: `CHLA (mg/m^3)` and `Turbidity (NTU)` are the two usable predictors of the biotic/particulate extinction component (Secchi is itself just another optical measurement of the same quantity, so it is not used as a predictor).

### Interpretation

-   Even after fixing the join (pseudo-replication), **R² = 0.067** — Chla and turbidity together explain under 7% of Kd variance. Chla alone is not significant (p = 0.167); only turbidity clears p \< 0.05, and its large coefficient combined with an 18% floor-clipping rate suggests the fit is being pulled around by a handful of high-turbidity events rather than reflecting a stable relationship.
-   `aed_noncohesive` is active in this AED configuration, computing its own suspended-sediment-driven extinction (`Ke_ss(i) * SS_conc(i)`) dynamically — so, unlike a setup with phytoplankton feedback alone, **both** the Chla- and turbidity-attributable terms are legitimately things AED will reconstruct, and both are fair to remove from the prescribed background. (Caveat: `aed_noncohesive`'s simulated SS is a mass concentration state, not the same quantity as measured NTU turbidity — the *decision* to exclude it is justified, but there's no guarantee the simulated SS dynamics reproduce the same magnitude/timing the regression attributed to turbidity.)
-   The **intercept is the one robust number here**: 0.368 m⁻¹ at p = 0.0013, much better constrained than either slope, since intercepts are anchored by the bulk of the data rather than a weak covariate relationship.
-   The Secchi–Kd relationship was also noted as weak, reinforcing that this system's optical signal is noisy relative to the predictors available.

## Recommendation for the climate scenario runs

For a **climate scenario** parameterisation (not a historical hindcast):

1.  **Do not use observed Kd.** `bioshade_feedback` and `aed_noncohesive` are both active, so a Kw already containing today's biotic/SS signal would be double-counted against AED's own dynamic contribution.
2.  **Do not use a dated time-varying background.** There is no calendar correspondence between a historical sampling date and a future scenario year — the "use the actual dated observations" option only applies to hindcast-style runs over the sampled period.
3.  **Do not build a monthly background climatology.** The Chla/turbidity regression (R² = 0.067) and the Secchi–Kd relationship are both weak; imposing monthly structure on this background signal risks encoding noise as if it were a genuine recurring seasonal driver.
4.  **Use a static background Kw**, set from the regression intercept (\~0.37 m⁻¹) — the most robust single number the data support. Let `bioshade_feedback` (phytoplankton) and `aed_noncohesive` (suspended sediment) supply the dynamic, climate-responsive part of the light environment, rather than freezing it to the historical record. This is also the conceptually correct setup for a scenario run: it lets the lake's optical response evolve mechanistically under whatever the scenario does to nutrients, temperature, and hydrology, instead of repeating today's bloom/turbidity patterns indefinitely.

``` fortran
&light
   light_mode       = 0
   Kw               = 0.37   ! static background Kw (regression intercept)
   n_bands          = 2
   light_extc       = 1.0, 0.5
   energy_frac      = 0.5, 0.5
   Benthic_Imin     = 10
   albedo_mode      = 1
   albedo_mean      = 0.08
   albedo_amplitude = 0.02
/
```

## Sensitivity check

Before committing to this, a direct sensitivity comparison is worth running: static background Kw (0.37 m⁻¹) with `bioshade_feedback` on, vs. the old static *observed* mean Kw (0.71 m⁻¹) with `bioshade_feedback` off. If key scenario outputs (surface temperature, stratification onset/breakdown, chlorophyll, hypolimnetic DO) are not materially different between the two, the choice matters less than the analysis above might suggest; if they diverge substantially, it's worth investing more in constraining the background estimate (e.g. more Chla/TSS sampling, a better SS-turbidity calibration for `aed_noncohesive`) before running the full scenario suite.

## Summary

| Question | Answer |
|------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Static vs. time-varying Kw (general)? | Time-varying is better *if* the seasonal signal is real and well-sampled; here, interannual variability (47% of variance) dominates over seasonality, so a pooled monthly climatology would misrepresent most years. |
| Does AED feedback interact with Kw? | Yes — `bioshade_feedback` (phytoplankton) and `aed_noncohesive` (suspended sediment) both add their own simulated extinction on top of the prescribed `Kw`, additively. |
| Should prescribed Kw include the biotic signal? | No, not when both feedbacks are active — it double-counts. |
| Can the biotic component be estimated from data? | Weakly. `Kd ~ Chla + Turbidity` gives R² = 0.067; only the intercept (background Kw ≈ 0.37 m⁻¹) is well-constrained. |
| Is calibrating AED's biotic light coefficients a good idea? | Not as a first move — risks equifinality/circularity with growth and mortality parameters; better to fix it from the empirical estimate and reserve calibration for parameters not otherwise measurable. |
| What should the climate scenario runs use? | A **static background Kw** (\~0.37 m⁻¹), with `bioshade_feedback` and `aed_noncohesive` supplying the dynamic, climate-responsive part of light extinction. |
