Meteorological Data Sources & Forcing Comparison

  • Show All Code
  • Hide All Code
  • View Source

This page compares the meteorological data sources available for Lake Rotorua, describes what each one brings to the model forcing built in climate-scenario-workflow.qmd, and documents the data-quality issues this comparison has caught along the way (a rainfall unit mismatch, a doubled wind-unit conversion on the buoy, ERA5 “drizzle”, and a drifting buoy humidity sensor). See data.qmd for full source/access details on every dataset in the pipeline, including these five.

The airport and PC10 stations were chosen from BOPRC/NIWA’s larger station networks by longest, most complete overlapping record rather than by raw distance to the lake — see the PC10 extraction notes for the full station comparison (pc10_climate_station_summary()); for Lake Rotorua, every realistic candidate station already sits close to the lake, so record length matters more than the last kilometre of proximity.

Comparing the raw sources against the buoy

met_source_comparison aggregates all five sources to daily values and scores the NIWA airport, MetService airport, ERA5 and PC10 against the buoy — the only source with a direct claim to being “true” lake conditions — over whatever window each pair overlaps, for temperature, humidity, rainfall, wind speed, shortwave radiation and station pressure. Since the old buoy data were added (all_buoy_met_data), that window reaches back to mid-2007, so most comparisons rest on up to ~16 years of daily pairs.

Note

Wind units. Buoy wind is in m/s and is extrapolated to 10 m (from 2 m on the new buoy and 1.5 m on the old one). A duplicated knots→m/s conversion was being applied to buoy wind that was already in m/s, scaling it by 0.514; it was removed in buoy_to_aeme_met() (and the old-buoy equivalent) in October 2026. Before the fix the buoy’s mean 10 m wind was ~2.8 m/s; it is now ~5.4 m/s. That change moves ERA5’s apparent wind bias from ~−0.2 m/s to ~−2.8 m/s, so every wind result on this page reflects the corrected series, and earlier versions of this page that reported a small ERA5 wind bias were wrong. Separately, the buoy’s own rain had a unit bug, fixed in rotorua_buoy_met_data: before 2023-10-23 it logged rain in mm/10 min, while later records use mm.

What this shows:

  • Air temperature — every land/reanalysis source reads colder than the buoy: NIWA airport −0.95 °C, MetService airport −0.92 °C, PC10 −1.00 °C and ERA5 −1.48 °C on daily means. The offset is stable year to year (ERA5 − buoy is −1.3 to −1.7 °C in every year 2011–2025), so it is a real and consistent difference between lake-surface air and land/grid-cell air rather than drift. ERA5 tracks the buoy’s day-to-day shape best (r ≈ 0.99); both airports follow at r ≈ 0.98; PC10 is weakest (r ≈ 0.86, MAE ≈ 1.9 °C).
  • Relative humidity — all sources read more humid than the buoy: ERA5 +7.5 %, NIWA airport +6.4 %, MetService +6.1 %, PC10 +3.1 %. This is not simply a lake effect: the two land stations and ERA5 agree with each other (hourly ERA5 − MetService is only +0.6 %, r = 0.88), and the buoy’s offset is not constant — the annual ERA5 − buoy RH bias is +2–3 % in 2022–23, +9.5 % in 2024, +13 % in 2025 and +48 % in the 2026 data (buoy mean RH 47 %). The buoy RH sensor looks drifty or faulty, particularly from 2024 on (see the hourly section). PC10 RH has very weak correlation with the buoy (r ≈ 0.32).
  • Rainfall — mean daily totals agree well. The MetService gauge matches the buoy almost exactly (bias ≈ 0, r ≈ 0.86, the best rain agreement of any source), ERA5 is next (bias +0.0004 m/day, r ≈ 0.77), then the NIWA airport (r ≈ 0.59, biased low because its daily rain is incomplete), and PC10 is essentially uncorrelated day to day (r ≈ 0.06) despite agreeing on long-run totals — consistent with real small-scale spatial variability in rain rather than a PC10 error.
  • Wind speed — the buoy (10 m) averages ~5.4 m/s, well above every other source: MetService airport −1.7 m/s (r ≈ 0.89), NIWA airport −2.1 m/s (r ≈ 0.82, only 679 days), and ERA5 −2.8 m/s (r ≈ 0.89, 5,079 days), i.e. about 52 % below the buoy. Even against the land MetService station ERA5 is 1.2 m/s low on matched hours, so ERA5 under-reads wind generally and the buoy is windier than both land stations (open-water fetch plus the 2 m → 10 m extrapolation). PC10 has no wind station.
  • Shortwave radiation — only the MetService airport (r ≈ 0.97, bias −5 W/m² over 469 days of overlap with the new buoy, Feb 2022–Jun 2023) and ERA5 (r ≈ 0.84, bias −10 W/m² over 1,404 days) can be compared; the NIWA airport has no overlap with the buoy.
  • Station pressure — all sources differ from the buoy by a nearly constant offset (r ≥ 0.996): MetService −0.13 kPa, ERA5 −1.10 kPa and NIWA airport +3.29 kPa. MetService (station height 285 m) is by far the closest.

Hourly comparison: buoys, MetService airport and ERA5

The daily comparison scores sources against a single combined buoy series. This section looks at hourly resolution and brings in four series: the old buoy (2007–22), the new buoy (2022–), ERA5, and the MetService airport record (1993–Jun 2023), which is the only independent, complete hourly station in the set and overlaps the old buoy for 16 years. It answers three questions the daily view cannot: (1) can the two buoy loggers be treated as one record?, (2) what are ERA5’s biases on matched hours, variable by variable, including shortwave, pressure and wind direction? and (3) is the buoy itself a trustworthy reference for each variable? The old and new buoys never overlap in time, so they are compared with each other through their distributions and indirectly through ERA5 and MetService.

All series are floored to the hour (the old buoy logs every 15 minutes, so the :00 record is kept) and duplicate hours are dropped, keeping the first value so wind direction is never averaged arithmetically. MetService rain, wind and radiation are hour-ending values (they describe the hour preceding the stamp); MetService wind is the hourly mean where recorded and the 10-minute mean otherwise.

prep <- function(df) {
  for (v in setdiff(vars, names(df))) df[[v]] <- NA_real_
  out <- df |>
    mutate(Date = lubridate::floor_date(Date, "hour")) |>
    select(Date, all_of(vars)) |>
    arrange(Date)

}

The two buoy loggers are statistically interchangeable for temperature, wind and pressure. Mean air temperature (14.0 °C both), 10 m wind speed (5.45 vs 5.34 m/s) and pressure (98.34 kPa both) match to within about 2 %, so there is no sign of a step change at the 2022 handover, and ERA5’s matched-hour offsets for these variables are nearly identical against each logger (below). Relative humidity is the exception (see the annual-bias table). The land and reanalysis series differ from the buoys in consistent ways: ERA5 is ~1.5 °C cooler, more humid (mean 83 % vs 75 %; median 87 % vs 77 %), has a wider temperature range (SD 4.6 vs 4.0 °C) and a much lower mean wind (2.48 vs ~5.4 m/s). The MetService airport sits in between on wind (3.58 m/s), is cooler than the buoys (12.7 °C, with a much wider range of −5 to 30 °C), is more humid (81 %), and reads 98.20 kPa station pressure (the buoys 98.34 kPa). Shortwave means agree across the three sources that measure it (new buoy 165, ERA5 170, MetService 167 W/m²).

Is the buoy a stable reference? Annual bias by year

Because ERA5 and the MetService airport are independent of the buoy and of each other, year-by-year bias against them shows whether a buoy sensor has drifted. A real, physical offset (such as lake vs. land air temperature) should be steady; sensor drift should not.

Code 
b_all <- bind_rows(dfs_h[["Buoy (old, 2007-22)"]], dfs_h[["Buoy (new, 2022-)"]])
era5_h <- dfs_h[["ERA5"]]
ms_h   <- dfs_h[["MetService airport"]]

yr_bias <- function(ref, test, v, lab) {
  inner_join(select(ref, Date, ref = all_of(v)),
             select(test, Date, test = all_of(v)), by = "Date") |>
    filter(!is.na(ref), !is.na(test)) |>
    mutate(yr = lubridate::year(Date)) |>
    group_by(yr) |>
    summarise(bias = mean(test - ref), .groups = "drop") |>
    mutate(comparison = lab, variable = v)
}

A real bug this comparison caught

Running this comparison surfaced a unit mismatch that a single-source sanity check would have missed: ERA5-Land’s raw MET_pprain column is stored in mm/hour, while every other source in this pipeline (buoy, airport, PC10) is in m/hour/m-day, the AEME convention. Uncorrected, ERA5’s rainfall was reading roughly 1000x too high in the shared units (mean 0.18 vs. the buoy’s 0.0008 m/hr) — and because era5_bias_correction originally excluded MET_pprain from correction (rainfall was assumed to be a poor target due to buoy-gauge reliability, not a units problem), that inflated value would have flowed straight through era5_corrected_hourly into every scenario baseline.

Fixed in two places:

  1. read_era5_hourly_met() now converts MET_pprain mm/hour → m/hour at read time, so the fix applies everywhere the ERA5 series is used, not just here.
  2. MET_pprain is now included in both era5_bias_correction and era5_bias_correction_extended’s vars, using the same method = "scale" day-of-year correction as the other variables — see the comparison above for why this is reasonable (no evidence of a buoy under-catch, just noise), while still treating the buoy’s rain series with more caution than its temperature/RH/wind.

Fixing ERA5 rainfall: quantile mapping vs. scaling

The default method = "scale" rain correction is only a monthly multiplier on totals, so it cannot touch the drizzle problem found above. metscale::fit_met_bias_correction() also offers empirical quantile mapping ("eqm") and quantile delta mapping ("qdm"), which remap the whole ERA5 distribution — zeros included — onto the buoy distribution month by month. Four ERA5 rain variants are compared here, all fit against all_buoy_met_data:

  • raw — uncorrected ERA5.
  • scale — the current era5_bias_correction approach (monthly multiplier).
  • eqm — quantile mapping (100 quantiles), which also adjusts wet-hour frequency and intensity.
  • eqm+scale — eqm, then a second monthly scale fit on the eqm output to put the total back on the buoy’s.

qdm was also fitted and gives numbers identical to eqm on the historical period (it only differs when extrapolating a trend), so it is not shown separately.

How this improves lake model forcing

  1. The buoy reference is now ~19 years, not ~4.5. Adding the old buoy lets the day-of-year corrections in era5_bias_correction and niwa_to_buoy_bias_correction train on ~130,000 hourly pairs instead of ~40,000, and the hourly comparison shows the two loggers agree on temperature, wind and pressure.
  2. Caught a forcing bug before it reached the model. The ERA5 rain unit error above would have meant every ERA5-driven scenario — including every future GCM projection, since deltas are applied to the ERA5 baseline — ran with rainfall roughly 3 orders of magnitude too high. A single-source pipeline has no way to notice this; comparing independent sources does.
  3. Wind is the largest bias in the forcing, and it is now correctly sized. With the doubled unit conversion removed, ERA5 reads ~2.8 m/s (~52 %) below the buoy’s 10 m wind, not ~0.2 m/s. Wind drives mixing and surface heat flux directly, so era5_bias_correction’s MET_wndspd scale correction (cross-validated RMSE 3.52 → 2.06 m/s, KGE 0.19 → 0.72) is one of the most consequential steps in the pipeline. The land stations cannot substitute: the MetService airport also reads 1.7 m/s below the buoy, so the correction really is a land/reanalysis → lake transfer and depends on the buoy’s 2 m/1.5 m → 10 m extrapolation being right.
  4. The buoy → airport extension helps temperature and humidity, less so wind. niwa_to_buoy_bias_correction trains on the full buoy overlap (2007–present) and cuts NIWA-airport-vs-buoy RMSE from 2.02 → 1.74 °C (temperature) while humidity, which uses the 2022–23 buoy only in this fit, shows no cross-validated gain (RMSE 5.7 → 6.6 %; in-sample 5.7 → 5.5 %); wind is corrected for bias (−2.3 → −0.1 m/s) but with only modest gains in RMSE (3.18 → 2.94 m/s) and weak correlation (r ≈ 0.63). era5_bias_correction_extended cuts ERA5 temperature RMSE from 2.08 → 1.45 °C and humidity from 7.3 → 7.0 %.
  5. A second, independent hourly station is now available. The MetService airport record (1993–Jun 2023) has a real rain gauge, shortwave and wind, and agrees with ERA5 on temperature (−0.5 °C), humidity (+0.6 %) and shortwave (−2 W/m²). It validates the rain correction independently of the buoy, exposes the buoy RH drift, and is a candidate for extending reference records back before 2007 (for variables where it agrees well with the buoy: temperature, pressure, rain).
  6. PC10’s 1901–present rainfall record is the longest available context for Rotorua rainfall, over 40 years longer than the airports’. That’s directly useful for summarise_climate_extremes.R / climate-extreme-events.qmd’s historical-extremes framing, and as a long-run independent check on whatever ERA5 rainfall climatology ends up driving the historical baseline and scenario deltas. Its weak daily correlation with the buoy for RH and rain (r ≈ 0.32 and 0.06) means it shouldn’t be used as a fit_met_bias_correction() reference for those variables.
  7. Wind direction and shortwave timing check out. ERA5 reproduces the prevailing WSW–W flow (hourly mean absolute direction error ~39–50°) and the shortwave daily cycle (r = 0.91 against the new buoy and 0.95 against MetService), so ERA5-derived direction and radiation are defensible for the model.

Caveats

  • Buoy relative humidity is not a reliable reference, so ERA5 humidity is corrected against the MetService airport instead. The old buoy (2007–22) reads ~5–9.5 % below ERA5 and the MetService airport, and the new buoy’s offset drifts from +2–3 % in 2022–23 to +9.5 % (2024), +13 % (2025) and +48 % in 2026 (mean RH 47 %). era5_bias_correction therefore no longer corrects MET_humrel; era5_humrel_correction fits it to airport_met_aeme (1993–Jun 2023, so the fit covers 2011–Jun 2023 of ERA5), and the correction is applied before the buoy-based one. buoy_met_fit_ref keeps buoy RH only for 2022-01-01 to 2023-12-31 for the remaining buoy-referenced fits (niwa_to_buoy_bias_correction and, through it, era5_bias_correction_extended, which still corrects humidity against that lake-equivalent series). all_buoy_met_data and the comparisons above still include all buoy RH. The airport station is a land station, so any lake effect on humidity (the buoy is the only lake-surface sensor) is not captured; the airport fit assumes the same land–lake humidity relationship ERA5 already has.
  • Station pressure (MET_prsttn). The buoys log sensible station pressure (~98.3 kPa on both loggers; the MetService airport 98.2 kPa). ERA5 reads a near-constant 1.1 kPa lower (r = 0.997), a grid-cell elevation difference of order 100 m, not a units error; the NIWA airport reads ~3.3 kPa higher. All offsets are removed by the scale corrections (ERA5 RMSE falls to ~60 Pa), but raw ERA5 and NIWA pressures should not be used uncorrected.
  • era5_bias_correction_extended wind and shortwave rest on almost no data. Its cross-validation has only 28 wind and 10 shortwave paired hours (versus ~130,000 for temperature/humidity), so for MET_wndspd and MET_radswd prefer era5_bias_correction. The comment in _targets.R describing these as “uncorrected airport values” predates the old buoy being added to the reference and is worth revisiting.
  • Shortwave is validated on limited data. The old buoy has no shortwave, the NIWA airport has none that overlaps the buoy, and the MetService airport overlaps the new buoy only from Feb 2022 to Jun 2023 (~11,000 hours), so ERA5 shortwave rests on the new buoy (~30,000 hours) plus that MetService overlap.
  • Sub-daily timing. The buoy stamps are offset from ERA5/MetService hour-ending stamps by roughly 30–60 minutes (see the diurnal section). This matters for hourly radiation and temperature, not for daily results.
  • ERA5 rain is wet too often (41 % of hours vs 12–16 %) with weak hourly intensity; fine for totals, less so for event-scale extremes. The current scale rain correction does not address this; eqm followed by scale does (see “Fixing ERA5 rainfall”). era5_bias_correction_extended still uses plain scale for rain.
  • PC10 has no radiation or wind data, so pc10_climate_met is a partial forcing (temp/RH/rain only) — useful for comparison and historical context, not as a standalone forcing source.
  • Hour-stamp de-duplication. prep() in the hourly section drops a large number of duplicated hour stamps (first value kept); the old buoy’s 15-minute rows are reduced to the :00 record. This is harmless for the comparisons here but means sub-hourly information is not used.

## Comparing the raw sources against the buoy

`met_source_comparison` aggregates all five sources to daily values and scores the NIWA airport, MetService airport, ERA5 and PC10 against the buoy — the only source with a direct claim to being "true" lake conditions — over whatever window each pair overlaps, for temperature, humidity, rainfall, wind speed, shortwave radiation and station pressure. Since the old buoy data were added (`all_buoy_met_data`), that window reaches back to **mid-2007**, so most comparisons rest on up to \~16 years of daily pairs.

::: callout-note
**Wind units.** Buoy wind is in m/s and is extrapolated to 10 m (from 2 m on the new buoy and 1.5 m on the old one). A duplicated knots→m/s conversion was being applied to buoy wind that was already in m/s, scaling it by 0.514; it was removed in `buoy_to_aeme_met()` (and the old-buoy equivalent) in October 2026. Before the fix the buoy's mean 10 m wind was \~2.8 m/s; it is now \~5.4 m/s. That change moves ERA5's apparent wind bias from \~−0.2 m/s to \~−2.8 m/s, so **every wind result on this page reflects the corrected series**, and earlier versions of this page that reported a small ERA5 wind bias were wrong. Separately, the buoy's own rain had a unit bug, fixed in `rotorua_buoy_met_data`: before 2023-10-23 it logged rain in mm/10 min, while later records use mm.

## Hourly comparison: buoys, MetService airport and ERA5

The daily comparison scores sources against a single combined buoy series. This section looks at hourly resolution and brings in four series: the old buoy (2007–22), the new buoy (2022–), ERA5, and the MetService airport record (1993–Jun 2023), which is the only independent, complete hourly station in the set and overlaps the old buoy for 16 years. It answers three questions the daily view cannot: **(1) can the two buoy loggers be treated as one record?**, **(2) what are ERA5's biases on matched hours, variable by variable, including shortwave, pressure and wind direction?** and **(3) is the buoy itself a trustworthy reference for each variable?** The old and new buoys never overlap in time, so they are compared with each other through their distributions and indirectly through ERA5 and MetService.

All series are floored to the hour (the old buoy logs every 15 minutes, so the `:00` record is kept) and duplicate hours are dropped, keeping the first value so wind direction is never averaged arithmetically. MetService rain, wind and radiation are hour-*ending* values (they describe the hour preceding the stamp); MetService wind is the hourly mean where recorded and the 10-minute mean otherwise.

### Is the buoy a stable reference? Annual bias by year

Because ERA5 and the MetService airport are independent of the buoy and of each other, year-by-year bias against them shows whether a buoy sensor has drifted. A real, physical offset (such as lake vs. land air temperature) should be steady; sensor drift should not.

```{r}

    summarise(bias = mean(test - ref), .groups = "drop") |>
    mutate(comparison = lab, variable = v)

### Matched-hour comparison: ERA5, MetService and the buoys

To remove sampling differences between periods, series are paired on hours where both have valid data: ERA5 against each buoy, ERA5 against the MetService airport, and MetService against each buoy. Bias is the second-named series minus the first-named reference, i.e. ERA5 − buoy, ERA5 − MetService, and MetService − buoy, so a negative wind bias means the first series reads low.

```{r}
#| label: hourly-paired

paired <- function(ref, test, vars) {
  inner_join(
    ref  |> select(Date, all_of(vars)),
    test |> select(Date, all_of(vars)),
    by = "Date", suffix = c("_ref", "_test")
  ) |>
    tidyr::pivot_longer(-Date, names_to = c("variable", "which"),
                        names_pattern = "(.*)_(ref|test)$", values_to = "value") |>
    tidyr::pivot_wider(names_from = which, values_from = value) |>
    filter(!is.na(ref), !is.na(test))
}

               "MET_radswd", "MET_wnddir")

    "ERA5 vs old buoy" = paired(dfs_h[["Buoy (old, 2007-22)"]], dfs_h[["ERA5"]], pair_vars),

The lag table below shows the effect on hourly skill is small, but it is worth knowing for sub-daily radiation work.

```{r}
#| label: hourly-lag

lag_r <- function(ref, test, v, k) {

## A real bug this comparison caught

Running this comparison surfaced a unit mismatch that a single-source sanity check would have missed: **ERA5-Land's raw `MET_pprain` column is stored in mm/hour, while every other source in this pipeline (buoy, airport, PC10) is in m/hour/m-day**, the AEME convention. Uncorrected, ERA5's rainfall was reading roughly 1000x too high in the shared units (mean 0.18 vs. the buoy's 0.0008 m/hr) — and because `era5_bias_correction` originally excluded `MET_pprain` from correction (rainfall was assumed to be a poor target due to buoy-gauge reliability, not a units problem), that inflated value would have flowed straight through `era5_corrected_hourly` into every scenario baseline.

Fixed in two places:

1.  [**`read_era5_hourly_met()`**](https://github.com/tadhg-moore/rotorua-pc10/blob/main/R/scenario_climate_workflow.R) now converts `MET_pprain` mm/hour → m/hour at read time, so the fix applies everywhere the ERA5 series is used, not just here.
2.  **`MET_pprain` is now included** in both `era5_bias_correction` and `era5_bias_correction_extended`'s `vars`, using the same `method = "scale"` day-of-year correction as the other variables — see the comparison above for why this is reasonable (no evidence of a buoy under-catch, just noise), while still treating the buoy's rain series with more caution than its temperature/RH/wind.

```{r}

*Note: the pipeline now quantile-maps rain before this fit (see "Fixing ERA5 rainfall"), so the `raw` rows for `MET_pprain` here and in the table below are the quantile-mapped series, and the skill shown is the second-stage monthly scale.* With the units fixed, ERA5 rain now agrees with the buoys on mean totals (hourly r ≈ 0.52 overall). The scale correction leaves rain essentially unchanged — cross-validated KGE is marginally *lower* after correction (0.31 vs 0.34) — which is the expected outcome for a variable whose mismatch is in timing and intensity rather than in the mean. Rain correction is harmless but should not be credited with improving the forcing.

### Fixing ERA5 rainfall: quantile mapping vs. scaling

The default `method = "scale"` rain correction is only a monthly multiplier on totals, so it cannot touch the drizzle problem found above. `metscale::fit_met_bias_correction()` also offers empirical quantile mapping (`"eqm"`) and quantile delta mapping (`"qdm"`), which remap the whole ERA5 distribution — zeros included — onto the buoy distribution month by month. Four ERA5 rain variants are compared here, all fit against `all_buoy_met_data`:

-   **raw** — uncorrected ERA5.
-   **scale** — the current `era5_bias_correction` approach (monthly multiplier).
-   **eqm** — quantile mapping (100 quantiles), which also adjusts wet-hour frequency and intensity.
-   **eqm+scale** — `eqm`, then a second monthly `scale` fit on the `eqm` output to put the total back on the buoy's.

`qdm` was also fitted and gives numbers identical to `eqm` on the historical period (it only differs when extrapolating a trend), so it is not shown separately.

## How this improves lake model forcing

1.  **The buoy reference is now \~19 years, not \~4.5.** Adding the old buoy lets the day-of-year corrections in `era5_bias_correction` and `niwa_to_buoy_bias_correction` train on \~130,000 hourly pairs instead of \~40,000, and the hourly comparison shows the two loggers agree on temperature, wind and pressure.
2.  **Caught a forcing bug before it reached the model.** The ERA5 rain unit error above would have meant every ERA5-driven scenario — including every future GCM projection, since deltas are applied to the ERA5 baseline — ran with rainfall roughly 3 orders of magnitude too high. A single-source pipeline has no way to notice this; comparing independent sources does.
3.  **Wind is the largest bias in the forcing, and it is now correctly sized.** With the doubled unit conversion removed, ERA5 reads \~2.8 m/s (\~52 %) below the buoy's 10 m wind, not \~0.2 m/s. Wind drives mixing and surface heat flux directly, so `era5_bias_correction`'s `MET_wndspd` scale correction (cross-validated RMSE 3.52 → 2.06 m/s, KGE 0.19 → 0.72) is one of the most consequential steps in the pipeline. The land stations cannot substitute: the MetService airport also reads 1.7 m/s below the buoy, so the correction really is a land/reanalysis → lake transfer and depends on the buoy's 2 m/1.5 m → 10 m extrapolation being right.
4.  **The buoy → airport extension helps temperature and humidity, less so wind.** `niwa_to_buoy_bias_correction` trains on the full buoy overlap (2007–present) and cuts NIWA-airport-vs-buoy RMSE from 2.02 → 1.74 °C (temperature) while humidity, which uses the 2022–23 buoy only in this fit, shows no cross-validated gain (RMSE 5.7 → 6.6 %; in-sample 5.7 → 5.5 %); wind is corrected for bias (−2.3 → −0.1 m/s) but with only modest gains in RMSE (3.18 → 2.94 m/s) and weak correlation (r ≈ 0.63). `era5_bias_correction_extended` cuts ERA5 temperature RMSE from 2.08 → 1.45 °C and humidity from 7.3 → 7.0 %.
5.  **A second, independent hourly station is now available.** The MetService airport record (1993–Jun 2023) has a real rain gauge, shortwave and wind, and agrees with ERA5 on temperature (−0.5 °C), humidity (+0.6 %) and shortwave (−2 W/m²). It validates the rain correction independently of the buoy, exposes the buoy RH drift, and is a candidate for extending reference records back before 2007 (for variables where it agrees well with the buoy: temperature, pressure, rain).
6.  **PC10's 1901–present rainfall record is the longest available context for Rotorua rainfall**, over 40 years longer than the airports'. That's directly useful for `summarise_climate_extremes.R` / [`climate-extreme-events.qmd`](climate-extreme-events.qmd)'s historical-extremes framing, and as a long-run independent check on whatever ERA5 rainfall climatology ends up driving the historical baseline and scenario deltas. Its weak daily correlation with the buoy for RH and rain (r ≈ 0.32 and 0.06) means it shouldn't be used as a `fit_met_bias_correction()` reference for those variables.
7.  **Wind direction and shortwave timing check out.** ERA5 reproduces the prevailing WSW–W flow (hourly mean absolute direction error \~39–50°) and the shortwave daily cycle (r = 0.91 against the new buoy and 0.95 against MetService), so ERA5-derived direction and radiation are defensible for the model.

## Caveats

-   **Buoy relative humidity is not a reliable reference, so ERA5 humidity is corrected against the MetService airport instead.** The old buoy (2007–22) reads \~5–9.5 % below ERA5 and the MetService airport, and the new buoy's offset drifts from +2–3 % in 2022–23 to +9.5 % (2024), +13 % (2025) and +48 % in 2026 (mean RH 47 %). `era5_bias_correction` therefore no longer corrects `MET_humrel`; `era5_humrel_correction` fits it to `airport_met_aeme` (1993–Jun 2023, so the fit covers 2011–Jun 2023 of ERA5), and the correction is applied before the buoy-based one. `buoy_met_fit_ref` keeps buoy RH only for 2022-01-01 to 2023-12-31 for the remaining buoy-referenced fits (`niwa_to_buoy_bias_correction` and, through it, `era5_bias_correction_extended`, which still corrects humidity against that lake-equivalent series). `all_buoy_met_data` and the comparisons above still include all buoy RH. The airport station is a land station, so any lake effect on humidity (the buoy is the only lake-surface sensor) is not captured; the airport fit assumes the same land–lake humidity relationship ERA5 already has.
-   **Station pressure (`MET_prsttn`).** The buoys log sensible station pressure (\~98.3 kPa on both loggers; the MetService airport 98.2 kPa). ERA5 reads a near-constant **1.1 kPa lower** (r = 0.997), a grid-cell elevation difference of order 100 m, not a units error; the NIWA airport reads \~3.3 kPa *higher*. All offsets are removed by the scale corrections (ERA5 RMSE falls to \~60 Pa), but raw ERA5 and NIWA pressures should not be used uncorrected.
-   **`era5_bias_correction_extended` wind and shortwave rest on almost no data.** Its cross-validation has only 28 wind and 10 shortwave paired hours (versus \~130,000 for temperature/humidity), so for `MET_wndspd` and `MET_radswd` prefer `era5_bias_correction`. The comment in `_targets.R` describing these as "uncorrected airport values" predates the old buoy being added to the reference and is worth revisiting.
-   **Shortwave is validated on limited data.** The old buoy has no shortwave, the NIWA airport has none that overlaps the buoy, and the MetService airport overlaps the new buoy only from Feb 2022 to Jun 2023 (\~11,000 hours), so ERA5 shortwave rests on the new buoy (\~30,000 hours) plus that MetService overlap.
-   **Sub-daily timing.** The buoy stamps are offset from ERA5/MetService hour-ending stamps by roughly 30–60 minutes (see the diurnal section). This matters for hourly radiation and temperature, not for daily results.
-   **ERA5 rain is wet too often** (41 % of hours vs 12–16 %) with weak hourly intensity; fine for totals, less so for event-scale extremes. The current `scale` rain correction does not address this; `eqm` followed by `scale` does (see "Fixing ERA5 rainfall"). `era5_bias_correction_extended` still uses plain `scale` for rain.
-   PC10 has **no radiation or wind data**, so `pc10_climate_met` is a partial forcing (temp/RH/rain only) — useful for comparison and historical context, not as a standalone forcing source.
-   **Hour-stamp de-duplication.** `prep()` in the hourly section drops a large number of duplicated hour stamps (first value kept); the old buoy's 15-minute rows are reduced to the `:00` record. This is harmless for the comparisons here but means sub-hourly information is not used.
