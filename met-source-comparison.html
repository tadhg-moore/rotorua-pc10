Meteorological Data Sources & Forcing Comparison

  • Show All Code
  • Hide All Code
  • View Source

This page compares the meteorological data sources available for Lake Rotorua, describes what each one brings to the model forcing built in climate-scenario-workflow.qmd, and documents a couple of data-quality issues this comparison caught along the way. See data.qmd for full source/access details on every dataset in the pipeline, including these four.

The four sources

The airport and PC10 stations were chosen from BOPRC/NIWA’s larger station networks by longest, most complete overlapping record rather than by raw distance to the lake — see the PC10 extraction notes for the full station comparison (pc10_climate_station_summary()); for Lake Rotorua, every realistic candidate station already sits close to the lake, so record length matters more than the last kilometre of proximity.

Comparing the raw sources against the buoy

met_source_comparison aggregates all four sources to daily values and scores the airport, ERA5 and PC10 against the buoy — the only source with a direct claim to being “true” lake conditions — over whatever window each pair overlaps, for temperature, humidity, rainfall and wind speed. Since the old buoy data were added (all_buoy_met_data), that window now reaches back to mid-2007 rather than 2022, so each comparison rests on up to ~16 years of daily pairs instead of ~4.

Note

The buoy’s own wind speed and rainfall had a unit bug of their own, now fixed in rotorua_buoy_met_data: before 2023-10-23 the buoy logged wind in knots and rain in mm/10 min, while later records use m/s and mm directly. The stats and plot below reflect the corrected series.

What this shows:

  • Air temperature — every land/reanalysis source reads colder than the buoy: airport −0.95 °C, PC10 −1.00 °C and ERA5 −1.48 °C on daily means. That is the expected lake-thermal-inertia effect (water buffers swings that land stations don’t) plus ERA5’s grid-cell offset, not a data error. ERA5 tracks the buoy’s day-to-day shape best (r ≈ 0.99) despite the largest mean offset; the airport is close behind (r ≈ 0.98); PC10 is weakest (r ≈ 0.86, MAE ≈ 1.9 °C).
  • Relative humidity — all sources read more humid than the buoy: ERA5 +7.5 %, airport +6.4 %, PC10 +3.1 %. Correlation is much weaker than for temperature (airport r ≈ 0.84, ERA5 r ≈ 0.78), and PC10’s RH correlation with the buoy is very weak (r ≈ 0.32) — treat PC10 RH as a rough guide, not a precise match to lake conditions.
  • Rainfall — mean daily totals agree well (bias within ~0.002 m/day for every source; airport slightly low at −0.002). Day-to-day skill is best for ERA5 (r ≈ 0.77), then the airport (r ≈ 0.59), and essentially absent for PC10 (r ≈ 0.06) — consistent with real small-scale spatial variability in rainfall (far patchier than temperature) rather than a systematic PC10 error, since its long-run total still agrees.
  • Wind speed — the picture changed once the old buoy was added. The airport now has 679 paired days (up from 2 against the new buoy alone): it reads about 0.7 m/s higher than the buoy (r ≈ 0.82), so it is usable for wind calibration, though the overlap ends in 2023. ERA5 reads only ~0.24 m/s low (buoy mean ~2.8 m/s, i.e. ~9 % low) with r ≈ 0.89 on 5,079 days. The much larger “~45 % low” ERA5 deficit reported in an earlier version of this page was driven by the buoy’s pre-October-2023 knots-vs-m/s issue, not by ERA5, and no longer appears. PC10 has no wind station.

Hourly comparison: old buoy, new buoy and ERA5

The daily comparison scores sources against a single combined buoy series. This section looks at the old (2007–22) and new (2022–) buoys separately, at hourly resolution, alongside ERA5 — the only gap-free source that overlaps both. It answers two questions the daily view cannot: (1) can the two buoy loggers be treated as one record? and (2) what are ERA5’s biases on matched hours, variable by variable, including shortwave, pressure and wind direction? The old and new buoys never overlap in time, so they can only be compared with each other directly through their distributions and indirectly through ERA5.

All three series are floored to the hour (the old buoy logs on :45 stamps) and duplicate hours are dropped, keeping the first value so wind direction is never averaged arithmetically.

prep <- function(df) {
  for (v in setdiff(vars, names(df))) df[[v]] <- NA_real_
  out <- df |>
    mutate(Date = lubridate::floor_date(Date, "hour")) |>
    select(Date, all_of(vars)) |>
    arrange(Date)

}

The two buoy loggers are statistically interchangeable. Mean air temperature (14.0 °C both), wind speed (2.75 vs 2.80 m/s), relative humidity (75.3 vs 74.9 %) and pressure (98.34 kPa both) match to within a few percent, so there is no sign of a step change at the 2022 handover. ERA5’s matched-hour offsets are also nearly identical against each logger (see below), which is independent confirmation. This is what justifies all_buoy_met_data being treated as a single ~19-year lake reference. ERA5 differs from both: it is ~1.5 °C cooler, ~8 % more humid (and saturates — median RH 87 % vs 77 %), has a wider temperature range (SD 4.6 vs 4.0 °C), and a slightly lower mean wind speed (2.48 vs 2.8 m/s). Shortwave is only available on the new buoy, where means agree (165 vs 170 W/m²).

Rainfall

Rainfall is zero-inflated, so it is compared by wet-hour fraction and by the distribution of non-zero hours rather than by mean and SD alone.

All three series are now in the same unit (m/hour — the old buoy’s maximum is 0.046 m/hr, in line with the others), so no rescaling is needed; rain_scale is kept at 1 as a hook in case that changes.

ERA5 gets the rain total right but not its character. Mean hourly rain is similar across sources (≈ 0.00014–0.00018 m/hr), but ERA5 is “wet” in 41 % of hours versus 15–16 % on both buoys, and its wet hours are weaker (mean 0.43 mm/hr vs 0.9–1.1 mm/hr; 95th percentile 2.1 vs 4.5–5.5 mm/hr; maximum 21 vs 28–46 mm/hr). That is the classic reanalysis drizzle pattern — light rain spread over too many hours with extremes smoothed away. The two buoys agree with each other (old buoy slightly heavier in the upper tail). For lake forcing this matters mostly for event-scale inflow and extremes; daily and longer totals are fine.

Matched-hour comparison: ERA5 vs. each buoy

To remove sampling differences between periods, ERA5 is paired with each buoy on hours where both have valid data. Bias is ERA5 minus buoy, so a negative wind bias means ERA5 reads low.

Code 
paired <- function(ref, test, vars) {
  inner_join(
    ref  |> select(Date, all_of(vars)),
    test |> select(Date, all_of(vars)),
    by = "Date", suffix = c("_ref", "_test")
  ) |>
    tidyr::pivot_longer(-Date, names_to = c("variable", "which"),
                        names_pattern = "(.*)_(ref|test)$", values_to = "value") |>
    tidyr::pivot_wider(names_from = which, values_from = value) |>
    filter(!is.na(ref), !is.na(test))
}

pair_vars <- c("MET_tmpair", "MET_prsttn", "MET_wndspd", "MET_humrel",
               "MET_radswd", "MET_wnddir")

pairs_all <- bind_rows(
  list(
    "ERA5 vs new buoy" = paired(dfs_h[["Buoy (new, 2022-)"]],   dfs_h[["ERA5"]], pair_vars),
    "ERA5 vs old buoy" = paired(dfs_h[["Buoy (old, 2007-22)"]], dfs_h[["ERA5"]], pair_vars)

A real bug this comparison caught

Running this comparison surfaced a unit mismatch that a single-source sanity check would have missed: ERA5-Land’s raw MET_pprain column is stored in mm/hour, while every other source in this pipeline (buoy, airport, PC10) is in m/hour/m-day, the AEME convention. Uncorrected, ERA5’s rainfall was reading roughly 1000x too high in the shared units (mean 0.18 vs. the buoy’s 0.0008 m/hr) — and because era5_bias_correction originally excluded MET_pprain from correction (rainfall was assumed to be a poor target due to buoy-gauge reliability, not a units problem), that inflated value would have flowed straight through era5_corrected_hourly into every scenario baseline.

Fixed in two places:

  1. read_era5_hourly_met() now converts MET_pprain mm/hour → m/hour at read time, so the fix applies everywhere the ERA5 series is used, not just here.
  2. MET_pprain is now included in both era5_bias_correction and era5_bias_correction_extended’s vars, using the same method = "scale" day-of-year correction as the other variables — see the comparison above for why this is reasonable (no evidence of a buoy under-catch, just noise), while still treating the buoy’s rain series with more caution than its temperature/RH/wind.

Fixing ERA5 rainfall: quantile mapping vs. scaling

The default method = "scale" rain correction is only a monthly multiplier on totals, so it cannot touch the drizzle problem found above. metscale::fit_met_bias_correction() also offers empirical quantile mapping ("eqm") and quantile delta mapping ("qdm"), which remap the whole ERA5 distribution — zeros included — onto the buoy distribution month by month. Four ERA5 rain variants are compared here, all fit against all_buoy_met_data:

  • raw — uncorrected ERA5.
  • scale — the current era5_bias_correction approach (monthly multiplier).
  • eqm — quantile mapping (100 quantiles), which also adjusts wet-hour frequency and intensity.
  • eqm+scale — eqm, then a second monthly scale fit on the eqm output to put the total back on the buoy’s.

qdm was also fitted and gives numbers identical to eqm on the historical period (it only differs when extrapolating a trend), so it is not shown separately.

How this improves lake model forcing

  1. The buoy reference is now ~19 years, not ~4.5. Adding the old buoy lets the day-of-year corrections in era5_bias_correction and niwa_to_buoy_bias_correction train on ~130,000 hourly pairs instead of ~40,000, and the hourly comparison above shows the two loggers are consistent enough to splice. All the daily statistics at the top of this page are correspondingly more robust than the earlier 2022-only version.
  2. Caught a forcing bug before it reached the model. The ERA5 rain unit error above would have meant every ERA5-driven scenario — including every future GCM projection, since deltas are applied to the ERA5 baseline — ran with rainfall roughly 3 orders of magnitude too high. A single-source pipeline has no way to notice this; comparing independent sources does.
  3. The buoy → airport extension is better supported for temperature and humidity. niwa_to_buoy_bias_correction now trains on the full buoy overlap (2007–present) and cuts airport-vs-buoy RMSE from 2.02 → 1.74 °C (temperature) and 9.2 → 6.5 % (humidity) with the bias removed. The resulting niwa_met_hourly_lake_equiv series is the reference for era5_bias_correction_extended, which on its own cross-validation cuts ERA5 temperature RMSE from 2.08 → 1.45 °C and humidity from 10.2 → 7.0 %. PC10’s independent record over the same window remains a cross-check on that extension (different instrumentation and network — BOPRC, not NIWA).
  4. PC10’s 1901–present rainfall record is the longest available context for Rotorua rainfall, over 40 years longer than the airport’s. That’s directly useful for summarise_climate_extremes.R / climate-extreme-events.qmd’s historical-extremes framing, and as a long-run independent check on whatever ERA5 rainfall climatology ends up driving the historical baseline and scenario deltas.
  5. Flags where to be careful. PC10’s weak correlation with the buoy for RH and rainfall (r ≈ 0.32 and 0.06) means it shouldn’t be used as a fit_met_bias_correction() reference for those variables the way the airport is — its value is historical context and cross-validation, not a second forcing bridge.
  6. Wind now has two usable correction sources, and ERA5 is the safer one. The airport has enough overlap with the buoy (679 daily / ~15,000 hourly pairs) to fit a correction (hourly bias 0.66 → 0 m/s, RMSE 1.83 → 1.40, cross-validated 1.51) — but it reads high and its sensor record ends in 2023. ERA5 has a small bias (−0.24 m/s), full coverage and r ≈ 0.89 daily, so era5_bias_correction’s MET_wndspd correction remains the primary route for future-looking forcing.
  7. Wind direction and shortwave timing check out. ERA5 reproduces the prevailing WSW–W flow and the shortwave daily cycle (r = 0.91 hourly), so using ERA5-derived direction and radiation in the model is defensible, with the caveat that hourly direction errors average ~45–50°.

Caveats

  • Station pressure (MET_prsttn) — earlier “unit issue” is now explained. The buoy logs sensible station pressure (~98.3 kPa on both loggers). ERA5 reads a near-constant 1.1 kPa lower (r = 0.997), which is a grid-cell elevation difference of order 100 m, not a units error; the airport reads ~3.3 kPa higher than the buoy. All offsets are removed by the scale corrections (ERA5 RMSE falls to ~60 Pa), but the raw ERA5 and airport pressures should not be used uncorrected.
  • era5_bias_correction_extended wind and shortwave rest on almost no data. Its fit has only 69 wind and 64 shortwave paired hours (28 and 10 in cross-validation), versus ~120,000 for temperature/humidity — so for MET_wndspd and MET_radswd prefer era5_bias_correction. The comment in _targets.R describing these as “uncorrected airport values” predates the old buoy being added to the reference and is worth revisiting.
  • The airport has no shortwave overlap with the buoy, and the old buoy has no shortwave at all, so shortwave is validated on the new buoy’s ~30,000 hours only.
  • ERA5 rain is wet too often (41 % of hours vs 15–16 %) with weak hourly intensity; fine for totals, less so for event-scale extremes. The current scale rain correction does not address this; eqm followed by scale does (see “Fixing ERA5 rainfall”). The airport’s daily rain series also looks incomplete (~250 mm/yr) and shouldn’t be used as a rain reference yet.
  • PC10 has no radiation or wind data, so pc10_climate_met is a partial forcing (temp/RH/rain only) — useful for comparison and historical context, not as a standalone forcing source.
  • Hour-stamp de-duplication. prep() in the hourly section drops a large number of duplicated hour stamps (first value kept); this is harmless for the comparisons here but means the hourly buoy tables contain many sub-hourly or repeated rows.

| **PC10 / BOPRC stations** | `pc10_climate_met` | Temp/RH: 2006–present (Edmund Rd); Rain: **1901–present** (Whakarewarewa) | Temp, RH, rain only — no radiation or wind | Rain record is by far the longest of any source here; independent of NIWA's instrumentation | No radiation/wind; weaker correlation with the buoy than the airport (see comparison below) |

The airport and PC10 stations were chosen from BOPRC/NIWA's larger station networks by longest, most complete overlapping record rather than by raw distance to the lake — see [the PC10 extraction notes](targets.qmd) for the full station comparison (`pc10_climate_station_summary()`); for Lake Rotorua, every realistic candidate station already sits close to the lake, so record length matters more than the last kilometre of proximity.

## Hourly comparison: old buoy, new buoy and ERA5

The daily comparison scores sources against a single combined buoy series. This section looks at the old (2007–22) and new (2022–) buoys separately, at hourly resolution, alongside ERA5 — the only gap-free source that overlaps both. It answers two questions the daily view cannot: **(1) can the two buoy loggers be treated as one record?** and **(2) what are ERA5's biases on matched hours, variable by variable, including shortwave, pressure and wind direction?** The old and new buoys never overlap in time, so they can only be compared with each other directly through their distributions and indirectly through ERA5.

All three series are floored to the hour (the old buoy logs on `:45` stamps) and duplicate hours are dropped, keeping the first value so wind direction is never averaged arithmetically.

### Matched-hour comparison: ERA5 vs. each buoy

To remove sampling differences between periods, ERA5 is paired with each buoy on hours where both have valid data. Bias is ERA5 minus buoy, so a negative wind bias means ERA5 reads low.

```{r}
#| label: hourly-paired

paired <- function(ref, test, vars) {
  inner_join(
    ref  |> select(Date, all_of(vars)),
    test |> select(Date, all_of(vars)),
    by = "Date", suffix = c("_ref", "_test")
  ) |>
    tidyr::pivot_longer(-Date, names_to = c("variable", "which"),
                        names_pattern = "(.*)_(ref|test)$", values_to = "value") |>
    tidyr::pivot_wider(names_from = which, values_from = value) |>
    filter(!is.na(ref), !is.na(test))
}

pair_vars <- c("MET_tmpair", "MET_prsttn", "MET_wndspd", "MET_humrel",
               "MET_radswd", "MET_wnddir")

pairs_all <- bind_rows(
  list(
    "ERA5 vs new buoy" = paired(dfs_h[["Buoy (new, 2022-)"]],   dfs_h[["ERA5"]], pair_vars),
    "ERA5 vs old buoy" = paired(dfs_h[["Buoy (old, 2007-22)"]], dfs_h[["ERA5"]], pair_vars)
  ),
  .id = "pair"
)

pairs_all |>
  filter(variable != "MET_wnddir") |>
  group_by(pair, variable) |>
  summarise(
    n    = n(),
    bias = mean(test - ref),
    rmse = sqrt(mean((test - ref)^2)),
    r    = cor(ref, test),
    ks_D = unname(suppressWarnings(ks.test(ref, test)$statistic)),
    .groups = "drop"
  ) |>
  knitr::kable(digits = 3,
               caption = "Matched-hour bias (ERA5 − buoy), RMSE, correlation and Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance.")

# Wind direction: signed angular difference in (-180, 180]
pairs_all |>
  filter(variable == "MET_wnddir") |>
  mutate(d = ((test - ref + 180) %% 360) - 180) |>
  group_by(pair) |>
  summarise(n = n(), mean_diff = mean(d), mean_abs_diff = mean(abs(d)), .groups = "drop") |>
  knitr::kable(digits = 1, caption = "Wind direction: signed (ERA5 − buoy) and absolute angular difference (°).")
```

## A real bug this comparison caught

Running this comparison surfaced a unit mismatch that a single-source sanity check would have missed: **ERA5-Land's raw `MET_pprain` column is stored in mm/hour, while every other source in this pipeline (buoy, airport, PC10) is in m/hour/m-day**, the AEME convention. Uncorrected, ERA5's rainfall was reading roughly 1000x too high in the shared units (mean 0.18 vs. the buoy's 0.0008 m/hr) — and because `era5_bias_correction` originally excluded `MET_pprain` from correction (rainfall was assumed to be a poor target due to buoy-gauge reliability, not a units problem), that inflated value would have flowed straight through `era5_corrected_hourly` into every scenario baseline.

Fixed in two places:

1.  [**`read_era5_hourly_met()`**](https://github.com/tadhg-moore/rotorua-pc10/blob/main/R/scenario_climate_workflow.R) now converts `MET_pprain` mm/hour → m/hour at read time, so the fix applies everywhere the ERA5 series is used, not just here.
2.  **`MET_pprain` is now included** in both `era5_bias_correction` and `era5_bias_correction_extended`'s `vars`, using the same `method = "scale"` day-of-year correction as the other variables — see the comparison above for why this is reasonable (no evidence of a buoy under-catch, just noise), while still treating the buoy's rain series with more caution than its temperature/RH/wind.

```{r}
#| label: era5-pprain-skill
#| echo: false

bc <- tar_read(era5_bias_correction)
bc$skill |>
  filter(variable == "MET_pprain", stage %in% c("cv_raw", "cv_corrected")) |>
  mutate(kind = ifelse(grepl("raw", stage), "raw ERA5", "bias-corrected")) |>
  select(variable, kind, n, bias, mae, rmse, r, kge) |>
  knitr::kable(digits = 4,
              caption = "Leave-one-year-out cross-validated skill for MET_pprain specifically, before vs. after the scale correction (post unit-fix).")
```

*Note: the pipeline now quantile-maps rain before this fit (see "Fixing ERA5 rainfall"), so the `raw` rows for `MET_pprain` here and in the table below are the quantile-mapped series, and the skill shown is the second-stage monthly scale.* With the units fixed, ERA5 rain now agrees with the buoys on mean totals (hourly r ≈ 0.52 overall). The scale correction leaves rain essentially unchanged — cross-validated KGE is marginally *lower* after correction (0.31 vs 0.34) — which is the expected outcome for a variable whose mismatch is in timing and intensity rather than in the mean. Rain correction is harmless but should not be credited with improving the forcing.

-   **Temperature, humidity and pressure improve a lot**: temperature bias −1.48 → 0.00 °C and RMSE 2.00 → 1.33 °C; humidity RMSE 11.9 → 9.3 % (KGE 0.58 → 0.61); pressure bias −1,103 Pa → 0 and RMSE 1,104 → 59 Pa.
-   **Wind**: the mean bias is removed but hourly RMSE is unchanged (≈ 1.06 m/s, r ≈ 0.73) — a scale correction fixes the mean, not the hourly timing error visible in the matched-hour scatter.
-   **Shortwave**: already unbiased (+9 W/m²); correction gives only a small gain, and it is fit on the new buoy alone (\~30,000 hours).
-   **Rain**: no gain from `scale` (above). Quantile mapping followed by a monthly scale does fix wet-hour frequency and intensity — see the next subsection.

### Fixing ERA5 rainfall: quantile mapping vs. scaling

The default `method = "scale"` rain correction is only a monthly multiplier on totals, so it cannot touch the drizzle problem found above. `metscale::fit_met_bias_correction()` also offers empirical quantile mapping (`"eqm"`) and quantile delta mapping (`"qdm"`), which remap the whole ERA5 distribution — zeros included — onto the buoy distribution month by month. Four ERA5 rain variants are compared here, all fit against `all_buoy_met_data`:

-   **raw** — uncorrected ERA5.
-   **scale** — the current `era5_bias_correction` approach (monthly multiplier).
-   **eqm** — quantile mapping (100 quantiles), which also adjusts wet-hour frequency and intensity.
-   **eqm+scale** — `eqm`, then a second monthly `scale` fit on the `eqm` output to put the total back on the buoy's.

`qdm` was also fitted and gives numbers identical to `eqm` on the historical period (it only differs when extrapolating a trend), so it is not shown separately.

```{r}
#| label: rain-fit

all_buoy_met_data <- tar_read(all_buoy_met_data)

fit_rain <- \(e, m) metscale::fit_met_bias_correction(
  e, all_buoy_met_data, vars = "MET_pprain", method = m,
  nquantiles = 100, verbose = FALSE)
apply_rain <- \(e, f) metscale::apply_met_bias_correction(e, f, verbose = FALSE)

f_scale <- fit_rain(era5_hourly_met, "scale")
f_eqm   <- fit_rain(era5_hourly_met, "eqm")
e_eqm   <- apply_rain(era5_hourly_met, f_eqm)
f_es    <- fit_rain(e_eqm, "scale")

rain_variants <- list(
  raw         = era5_hourly_met,
  scale       = apply_rain(era5_hourly_met, f_scale),
  eqm         = e_eqm,
  `eqm+scale` = apply_rain(e_eqm, f_es)
)

# Cross-validated (leave-one-year-out) skill. For eqm+scale this is the
# second-stage fit only (scored against the eqm output); the first-stage
# skill is in the "eqm" row.
bind_rows(
  raw         = f_scale$skill |> filter(stage == "cv_raw"),
  scale       = f_scale$skill |> filter(stage == "cv_corrected"),
  eqm         = f_eqm$skill   |> filter(stage == "cv_corrected"),
  `eqm+scale` = f_es$skill    |> filter(stage == "cv_corrected"),
  .id = "method") |>
  select(method, n, rmse, r, kge) |>
  knitr::kable(digits = 4,
               caption = "Cross-validated hourly rain skill vs. the buoy (m/hr). Hourly RMSE and r *worsen* with quantile mapping while KGE improves, because a more realistic intermittent series is penalised for timing errors.")
```

#### Cross-check against independent gauges

Because the corrections are fit to the buoy, the buoy cannot also validate them. The same variants are therefore scored at daily resolution against the buoy, the airport and PC10 gauges (daily totals in mm).

-   **Buoy:** `eqm+scale` matches the buoy's annual total (1,445 vs. 1,446 mm/yr), p95 (21.8 vs. 21.2 mm/day) and maximum (267 vs. 281 mm/day); raw ERA5 has 44 % wet days against the buoy's 27 %, and the `eqm`-based variants 25 %. The upper tail is slightly over-stretched (p99 71 vs. 65 mm/day).
-   **PC10 (Whakarewarewa gauge)** is the most useful independent check on totals: it reads 1,514 mm/yr, between the buoy (1,446) and raw ERA5 (1,576). `eqm+scale` (1,451) is within 4 % of it, so the buoy total is not obviously under-catching, though the true value may be slightly higher than the buoy's. PC10's p95 (22.1 mm/day) is matched by `eqm+scale` (21.9). Its wet-day fraction (35 %) lies between the buoy-corrected series (25 %) and raw ERA5 (44 %), but a once-daily gauge reading probably smears wet days across two calendar days and inflates the count, so this is not a clean target. Its daily correlation with ERA5 is very low in every case (r < 0.1), as in the earlier source comparison.
-   **Airport:** this check is **inconclusive**. The airport's daily rainfall averages only 0.7 mm/day (\~250 mm/yr) on days it is "valid", roughly a fifth of every other gauge, so its rain series appears incomplete (many days probably contain only some of the 3-hourly observations) and cannot be used to judge totals or wet-day frequency. It is excluded from the conclusions below, and its aggregation is worth checking before it is used as a rain reference.
-   **Daily skill:** all `eqm` variants have somewhat higher daily RMSE than `scale` against the buoy (9.5 vs. 8.7 mm/day for `eqm+scale`) with similar r (0.77), consistent with the hourly double penalty.

**Recommendation (now implemented in the pipeline):** use **`MET_pprain = "eqm"` followed by a monthly `scale`** for the historical ERA5 baseline. That fixes drizzle frequency, wet-hour intensity and the tail, keeps the total on the buoy, and is consistent with PC10's independent total to within \~4 %. Because the second stage is fit on the output of the first, it needs two chained calls rather than a single `method =` vector:

In `_targets.R` this is `era5_rain_eqm_correction` → `era5_hourly_met_rainqm` (stage 1), then `era5_bias_correction` fitted on `era5_hourly_met_rainqm` (stage 2, together with the other variables), with `era5_corrected_hourly` and `era5_corrected_daily_baseline` built from `era5_hourly_met_rainqm`. The equivalent standalone code is:

```{r}
#| label: rain-recommended
#| eval: false

# 1. quantile-map rain onto the buoy distribution
bc_rain_eqm <- metscale::fit_met_bias_correction(
  era5_hourly_met, all_buoy_met_data, vars = "MET_pprain",
  method = "eqm", nquantiles = 100, verbose = FALSE)
era5_eqm <- metscale::apply_met_bias_correction(era5_hourly_met, bc_rain_eqm)

# 2. restore the buoy's total with a monthly ratio
bc_rain_scale <- metscale::fit_met_bias_correction(
  era5_eqm, all_buoy_met_data, vars = "MET_pprain",
  method = "scale", by = "doy-loess", verbose = FALSE)
era5_rain_corrected <- metscale::apply_met_bias_correction(era5_eqm, bc_rain_scale)
```

::: callout-note
**`expand_met()` rounding (fixed).** `metscale::expand_met()` previously rounded every column to 3 decimals. Rain is in m/hr, so that quantised it to whole millimetres: in testing it cut the corrected wet-hour fraction from 15.2 % to 4.9 % and erased all the drizzle correction (and coarsened earlier `era5_corrected_hourly` rain). It now rounds to 6 decimals and the pipeline calls `apply_met_bias_correction(expand = TRUE)` directly. `era5_corrected_daily_baseline` rounds daily totals to 6 decimals too, so it is unaffected.
:::

Caveats: hourly timing is unchanged (r stays ≈ 0.5) — this corrects the *distribution*, not when rain falls. The hourly mapping is only as good as the buoy gauge, and the airport could not confirm that. The quantile map caps ERA5 extremes at the buoy's observed maximum.

## How this improves lake model forcing

1.  **The buoy reference is now \~19 years, not \~4.5.** Adding the old buoy lets the day-of-year corrections in `era5_bias_correction` and `niwa_to_buoy_bias_correction` train on \~130,000 hourly pairs instead of \~40,000, and the hourly comparison above shows the two loggers are consistent enough to splice. All the daily statistics at the top of this page are correspondingly more robust than the earlier 2022-only version.
2.  **Caught a forcing bug before it reached the model.** The ERA5 rain unit error above would have meant every ERA5-driven scenario — including every future GCM projection, since deltas are applied to the ERA5 baseline — ran with rainfall roughly 3 orders of magnitude too high. A single-source pipeline has no way to notice this; comparing independent sources does.
3.  **The buoy → airport extension is better supported for temperature and humidity.** `niwa_to_buoy_bias_correction` now trains on the full buoy overlap (2007–present) and cuts airport-vs-buoy RMSE from 2.02 → 1.74 °C (temperature) and 9.2 → 6.5 % (humidity) with the bias removed. The resulting `niwa_met_hourly_lake_equiv` series is the reference for `era5_bias_correction_extended`, which on its own cross-validation cuts ERA5 temperature RMSE from 2.08 → 1.45 °C and humidity from 10.2 → 7.0 %. PC10's independent record over the same window remains a cross-check on that extension (different instrumentation and network — BOPRC, not NIWA).
4.  **PC10's 1901–present rainfall record is the longest available context for Rotorua rainfall**, over 40 years longer than the airport's. That's directly useful for `summarise_climate_extremes.R` / [`climate-extreme-events.qmd`](climate-extreme-events.qmd)'s historical-extremes framing, and as a long-run independent check on whatever ERA5 rainfall climatology ends up driving the historical baseline and scenario deltas.
5.  **Flags where to be careful.** PC10's weak correlation with the buoy for RH and rainfall (r ≈ 0.32 and 0.06) means it shouldn't be used as a `fit_met_bias_correction()` reference for those variables the way the airport is — its value is historical context and cross-validation, not a second forcing bridge.
6.  **Wind now has two usable correction sources, and ERA5 is the safer one.** The airport has enough overlap with the buoy (679 daily / \~15,000 hourly pairs) to fit a correction (hourly bias 0.66 → 0 m/s, RMSE 1.83 → 1.40, cross-validated 1.51) — but it reads *high* and its sensor record ends in 2023. ERA5 has a small bias (−0.24 m/s), full coverage and r ≈ 0.89 daily, so `era5_bias_correction`'s `MET_wndspd` correction remains the primary route for future-looking forcing.
7.  **Wind direction and shortwave timing check out.** ERA5 reproduces the prevailing WSW–W flow and the shortwave daily cycle (r = 0.91 hourly), so using ERA5-derived direction and radiation in the model is defensible, with the caveat that hourly direction errors average \~45–50°.

## Caveats

-   **Station pressure (`MET_prsttn`) — earlier "unit issue" is now explained.** The buoy logs sensible station pressure (\~98.3 kPa on both loggers). ERA5 reads a near-constant **1.1 kPa lower** (r = 0.997), which is a grid-cell elevation difference of order 100 m, not a units error; the airport reads \~3.3 kPa *higher* than the buoy. All offsets are removed by the scale corrections (ERA5 RMSE falls to \~60 Pa), but the raw ERA5 and airport pressures should not be used uncorrected.
-   **`era5_bias_correction_extended` wind and shortwave rest on almost no data.** Its fit has only 69 wind and 64 shortwave paired hours (28 and 10 in cross-validation), versus \~120,000 for temperature/humidity — so for `MET_wndspd` and `MET_radswd` prefer `era5_bias_correction`. The comment in `_targets.R` describing these as "uncorrected airport values" predates the old buoy being added to the reference and is worth revisiting.
-   **The airport has no shortwave overlap with the buoy**, and the old buoy has no shortwave at all, so shortwave is validated on the new buoy's \~30,000 hours only.
-   **ERA5 rain is wet too often** (41 % of hours vs 15–16 %) with weak hourly intensity; fine for totals, less so for event-scale extremes. The current `scale` rain correction does not address this; `eqm` followed by `scale` does (see "Fixing ERA5 rainfall"). The airport's daily rain series also looks incomplete (\~250 mm/yr) and shouldn't be used as a rain reference yet.
-   PC10 has **no radiation or wind data**, so `pc10_climate_met` is a partial forcing (temp/RH/rain only) — useful for comparison and historical context, not as a standalone forcing source.
-   **Hour-stamp de-duplication.** `prep()` in the hourly section drops a large number of duplicated hour stamps (first value kept); this is harmless for the comparisons here but means the hourly buoy tables contain many sub-hourly or repeated rows.
