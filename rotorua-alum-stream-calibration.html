Alum stream step: calibration design from the thesis stoichiometry

Comparing the McIntosh ramp used so far with the Sanila thesis Al:P ratios

Author

Tadhg Moore

Published

November 5, 2026

1 Summary

The alum calibration so far treated the reaction between alum and stream phosphorus (P) before the lake with two rules taken from field data: a ramp to a 90% cap at 23 mol Al per mol DRP for the Puarenga, and a flat 45% removal for the Utuhina (Section 2). Together they used about 30 mol of aluminium per mol of P removed in the streams, and 5.5 in the lake (see “Alum applied per P removed, and the fate of alum and P” on the alum calibration stage 1 page). The thesis (Sanila) gives a different picture, which this page turns into a calibration design.

  • The thesis finding. Alum reduced DRP in all waters, and the Al:P molar ratios ranged from 0.7:1 to 15.1:1. Where DRP stayed measurable after treatment, the ratio was near 1:1 (Lake Rotorua 0.7:1, Puarenga Stream 0.98:1). Where DRP fell below detection, the dose was in surplus and a minimum effective dose could not be determined.
  • What that means for the comparison. Only days where DRP stays measurable carry a ratio. Most of the Al in the model goes out on surplus days (98% of the Puarenga Al at a dose ratio above 10, see the alum calibration stage 1 page), so the earlier comparison with the thesis (ratio of 30 against about 1) mixed both kinds of day. The ramp also spends 26 mol Al per mol P on the days the thesis says 1:1, so the two disagree even on the days that can be compared.
  • The design. Three stream-step hypotheses on the same lake: the previous rule (H0), the thesis stoichiometry in both streams (H1), and the thesis in the Puarenga with the measured Utuhina removal kept (H1b). A calibrated efficiency (H2) follows if H1 does not settle it (Section 4).
  • First results. H1 and H1b have been run on the best member of the joint stage (Section 5). The stream rule was then fitted to the Utuhina data (Section 6): removal is a plateau near 43% and the efficiency ε is not identified, so the thesis value is neither confirmed nor excluded. The thesis lake stoichiometry is applied by scaling the input in R, not by a new module parameter (Section 7).

2 What was used before

The stream step moves a fraction f of the inflow FRP into suspended alum-bound P (ALU_alp) and removes the same amount of capacity from the free pool (ALU_ala) before the inflow enters the lake. The dose ratio is R = (ALU_ala + ALU_alp) / FRP, in mol Al per mol P (the capacity ratio of the module is 1, so a mole of capacity is a mole of Al).

Stream Distance to the lake Rule Source
Puarenga about 680 m f = 0.9 × min(1, R / 23) McIntosh paired up- and downstream samples (Hamilton et al. 2015, Fig 20)
Utuhina about 1360 m f = 0.45 Measured downstream DRP against the reconstructed untreated DRP (Hamilton et al. 2015)

Below R = 23 the Puarenga rule binds 0.9 R / 23 of the FRP, which spends 23 / 0.9 ≈ 26 mol Al per mol P removed. Applied to the dosed days of 2006 to 2022-06 (Table 1), this gives about 30 mol/mol across both streams.

The Puarenga series in the with-alum inflow file is built with the same ramp and cap, so it is not an independent check on this rule. The Utuhina downstream series is a measurement.

3 What the thesis gives

The thesis result, as stated by the author:

Alum reduced DRP across all waters, but the Al:P molar ratios, ranging from 0.7:1 to 15.1:1 and post-treatment pH values ranging from 6.9 to 7.9, indicate that alum dose requirements varied with differing water chemistry. Where post-treatment DRP remained measurable, Al:P molar ratios were near 1:1 (Lake Rotorua 0.7:1; Puarenga Stream 0.98:1). Where DRP fell below detection, results indicated surplus alum (aluminium) dosing, and a minimum effective dose could not be determined.

Three consequences for the model:

  1. The stoichiometric rate is about 1 mol Al per mol P when P is limiting, for both the Puarenga and the lake water. The module’s capacity ratio of 1 matches this. The 26:1 of the stream ramp does not.
  2. Surplus doses cannot give a ratio. If DRP is removed to below detection, the ratio is an upper bound on the dose needed, not a measurement. The model ratio should be compared on days when the model leaves measurable DRP (unsaturated days), and the share of Al applied on saturated days should be reported with it.
  3. The data are from jar tests with hours of contact in mixed samples. The stream reaches are 680 to 1360 m, with minutes of contact, so the thesis gives the efficiency of the chemistry, which the reach can only equal or fall short of. This is the reason to keep an efficiency parameter (H2) and not fix it at the thesis value.

The thesis range also includes surplus-dose ratios up to 15.1:1. The thesis also cites an operational field ratio of about 7:1 for the Utuhina and Puarenga inflows (McDowell and Hawke, 2006), which it attributes to turbulence, short flocculation contact times and hydrodynamic losses in the stream. That is a dose-over-removal figure for real dosing, so it includes surplus days. Its jar-test value for Lake Rotorua (0.7:1, a 1.0 mg Al/L dose removing 1.60 of 1.62 mg DRP/L) is the best-case chemistry.

4 Design

4.1 Hypotheses for the stream step

The stream step binds B mmol P per m³ of inflow, taken from the free capacity.

Run Stream step Free parameters
H0 (previous) Puarenga 0.9·min(1, R/23), Utuhina flat 0.45 none, reference
H1 (thesis stoichiometry) both streams: B = min(f_max · FRP, ε · dose), ε = 1 mol P per mol Al none (f_max = 0.9, as the old cap)
H1b (thesis, Puarenga only) Puarenga as H1, Utuhina as H0 none
H2 (calibrated) as H1 with ε free per stream ε (Puarenga), ε (Utuhina), f_max

H1b is there because H1 contradicts a measurement for the Utuhina (Section 5). Priors for H2: ε between 0.04 (the ramp) and 1 (the thesis), f_max between 0.7 and 1.

4.2 Lake side, the same for all runs

  • Stoichiometry, set in R. The module uses capacity one for one with P (aed_alum.F90), and no parameter is added. The thesis lake ratio of 0.7:1 means 1 mol of Al binds β = 1/0.7 = 1.43 mol P, so the input is scaled instead (Section 7): ALU_ala and ALU_alp become P-binding capacity, β times the Al dose, and aluminium is the capacity divided by β wherever it is reported. H1 and H1b keep β = 1.
  • Rates. Refit w_ala (thesis 0.3 to 1.5 m/d), K_P (about 0.3), k_w, k_s, k_age_w, k_age_s, k_bur, k_res as in stage p4, from the member of the joint stage. The sensitivity screen found the alum parameters carry 6.5% of the objective, and k_age_w is the first driver of the alum in the water.

4.3 Targets

  • Streams. The measured Utuhina downstream DRP, and the McIntosh Puarenga paired up- and downstream samples used directly. The Puarenga series in the inflow file cannot be used because it is built from the ramp.
  • Lake. The existing calibration and held-out targets of the joint stage (TP, FRP, chlorophyll, oxygen), and the ERI 178 aluminium survey once the values are in hand.

4.4 What to compare across runs

  1. The objective on the calibration and held-out windows, and the surface TP, FRP and chlorophyll bias.
  2. Al:P on unsaturated days for the streams, the lake and the whole system, against about 1.
  3. The share of Al applied on saturated (surplus) days for each stream.
  4. The reduction in P export with alum (14.9 t P/yr in the previous setup).
  5. Whether the new parameters narrow under the ensemble, and whether ε and k_w trade off (both set how fast P is removed).

4.5 Stages

  1. H1 and H1b on the best member (this page, Section 5): one run each, no refit. Shows what the thesis rule does before any calibration.
  2. H2 ensemble: ε and f_max free together with the lake alum parameters, ES-MDA as in stage p4.
  3. Compare H0, H1b and H2 on the criteria above, and decide the stream rule.

5 First result: H1 and H1b on the best member

Both runs use member 46 of stage j with the alum parameters unchanged. Only the stream step differs. The base is the previous (flood × 0.5) base with the untreated FRP and the dose rebuilt from it, so every other inflow variable is the same; the rebuild reproduces the previous bound P to machine precision (calibration/alum/60_h1_base.R). Both runs cover 2005-01 to 2022-06 (calibration/historical/run_hist.R, calibration/alum/61_h1_compare.R).

5.1 The stream step

With the thesis rule the 90% cap is reached on 99.6% of the dosed days in both streams, and the dose is in surplus on virtually all of them. The ratio is 1.0 only on the 0.4% of days where the dose is below the FRP. This is the situation the thesis describes for the surplus case: the field doses cannot show what the minimum effective ratio is. Over all dosed days the ratio is 27 (Puarenga) and 13.5 (Utuhina) under H1, close to the previous Puarenga value (33) because the dose is large either way. In that sense the previous ramp and the thesis rule agree on the Puarenga: both remove most of the FRP when the dose is large, and the Al:P over all days is mainly the dose.

The rule changes the removal most at low doses. For the Puarenga the P bound rises from 16.0 to 19.7 t (+23%). For the Utuhina, H1 would double the removal (16.7 to 33.4 t, 90% of the FRP), which contradicts the measured downstream DRP: the observed removal there is 16.4 t, 44% of the FRP (Table 1). H1 is therefore rejected for the Utuhina, and H1b is the version to carry forward.

5.2 The lake and the whole system

The lake takes up what the streams leave. When the stream step binds 1.2 t P/yr more (H1), the lake water binds 0.4 t P/yr less, so the total moves by only 0.8 t P/yr (6%) and the whole-system ratio from 4.8 to 4.5 (H1) or 4.75 (H1b). The modelled export, 10.6 t P/yr in the previous setup, changes by 0.1 to 0.2 t P/yr. The stream step as a whole is therefore a weak control on the lake, in line with the earlier finding that the stream reaction variants are indistinguishable in the lake data.

The fit does not distinguish the rules. H1b is within 0.2 percentage points of the previous bias for TP and chlorophyll. H1 is slightly worse (TP -8.9% against -7.5%, FRP -24% against -21%), because binding more P in the streams leaves less FRP in the lake, and surface FRP is already low in the best member.

5.3 What this shows

  • The thesis and the ramp agree where it matters. Both give near-complete removal at large doses. They differ only at low doses, which are a small part of the Al applied.
  • The thesis 1:1 cannot be tested against the field doses. On 99.6% of the dosed days the thesis rule saturates, so the data cannot show an efficiency. The thesis says this too (“a minimum effective dose could not be determined”).
  • H1 fails for the Utuhina because the measured downstream DRP shows only 44% removal at the same doses. The Utuhina reach therefore does not reach the jar-test efficiency, which supports a calibrated ε (H2) at least for that stream.
  • The lake data do not respond to the stream rule, so the choice should rest on the stream measurements and on physical grounds, not on the lake fit.

This is one member with its parameters unchanged, not a calibration. The lake alum parameters (k_w, K_P, w_ala, ageing and burial) were fitted under the previous rule and the lake already compensates for the difference, so a refit is the proper test of whether the thesis stoichiometry changes the lake parameters.

6 Step 1: the stream rule against the stream observations

This step fits the rule f = min(f_max, ε R) to the stream data alone, with no lake model (calibration/alum/62_stream_fit.R). R is the dose over the untreated DRP, in mol Al per mol P, and ε is the mol of P bound per mol of Al.

The data. The Utuhina inflow was sampled downstream of the dosing point, so on dosed days the removal is f_obs = 1 − DRP(measured) / DRP(untreated), taking the untreated DRP from the reconstructed no-alum series (Hamilton et al. 2015, p. 36). Both series are daily, interpolated from monthly samples, so the 4,980 dosed days of 2007 to 2022-06 are not independent. The intervals below come from a bootstrap over the 178 calendar months.

For the Puarenga there is nothing to fit. The McIntosh spot samples (Hamilton et al. 2015, Fig 20) are not in the repository, and the Puarenga treated DRP in the with-alum file is built from the ramp: its removal by dose-ratio bin follows 0.9·min(1, R/23) (Table 5), so fitting it would only return the ramp. The report text constrains the Puarenga only from below: more than 80% reduction at a mass ratio Al:PO₄-P of about 20 (R = 23 mol/mol) needs ε ≥ 0.8/23 = 0.035, and the ramp (0.9/23 = 0.039) sits at that bound. The thesis value of about 1 is 25 times higher, and the data in hand cannot choose between them.

What the Utuhina data say

  • The removal is a plateau near 43%, not a function of the dose. The fitted f_max is 0.42 (0.40 to 0.45), and the flat rule fits as well as the two-parameter rules (RMSE 0.200 for all three). The previous flat 0.45 is inside the interval and is as good (RMSE 0.202). The previous ramp is worse (0.266) and H1 much worse (0.514), because both over-remove at low and moderate doses.
  • ε is not identified. Its interval runs from 0.14 to 2.1, so the Utuhina data are consistent with the thesis value (1) and with values 5 times lower. The plateau is reached at the lowest doses (a mean of 25 to 45 kg Al/d at R between 1 and 5 already gives 43 to 49%), so the dose never limits the removal, and the data cannot show what limits it.
  • The removal rises only slightly at high doses: 55% at R of 23 to 50 and 65% above 50, from 480 days. This is the only sign of a dose response.
  • Something other than capacity limits the Utuhina removal. A dose-limited reaction at the thesis stoichiometry would remove 100% of the DRP at R above 1, and the Utuhina removes 43%. Either the 1360 m reach is too short for the reaction to finish (a contact time limit), or the reconstructed untreated DRP is biased, because it comes from the pre-dosing DRP:TP ratio and that ratio may have moved for other reasons. The data in hand cannot separate the two.

7 Lake stoichiometry set in R

The module binds one mol of P per mol of capacity, and nothing is added to it. The thesis lake ratio (0.7 mol Al per mol P) is applied in R by scaling the input (calibration/alum/63_stoich_base.R): the dose delivered to the module is β = 1/0.7 = 1.43 times the Al dose, so ALU_ala and ALU_alp are in mmol of P-binding capacity per m³, not mmol of Al. The stream step is applied to this capacity (B = min(f_max · FRP, capacity)), and the Al reported anywhere is the capacity divided by β. The run below uses the Puarenga with the thesis rule (cap 0.9) and the Utuhina with its measured 0.45 (H1b), on member 46.

This changes the model, as intended: each mol of capacity consumed now stands for 1/β mol of Al, so the same Al binds 1.43 times more P. It is not independent of the lake rates, though. Capacity is lost mostly by ageing and settling (57% and 28% of the water column losses in member 46), so a larger β raises P uptake by much less than β, and β trades off with k_w and k_age_w, which set how much capacity binds P before it ages out. In a refit β is therefore fixed (at 1 or 1.43) and the rates are fitted, not both.

With the rates unchanged, β = 1.43 raises the P bound from 12.6 to 14.1 t P/yr (+12%, not +43%), lowers the export from 10.5 to 9.1 t P/yr, and pushes the surface bias to -21% for TP and -48% for FRP, against -8% and -21% before. The whole-system ratio falls to 4.2 mol Al per mol P and the lake ratio to 4.9. The fit is worse because the lake rates were fitted with one-for-one binding, so the lake now removes too much P. A refit of k_w, K_P and the ageing rates under β = 1.43 is needed before the fit can be compared, and it is the test of whether the data prefer the thesis value.

8 Lake refits under each rule

The lake parameters were refitted three times, each with the joint-stage objective (calibration window 2006-06 to 2018-06, validation 2018-07 to 2022-06), 48 members, 4 iterations, started from the stage j best member (calibration/step2/run_step2h1.ps1, run_step2h1_chain.ps1). Fourteen parameters were free: the eight alum parameters and six sediment P parameters (fsed_frp by zone, ksed_frp, theta_sed_frp). H0 is the previous stream rule, so it separates the effect of the stream rule from the effect of refitting. The aluminium target is divided by β, so it compares Al and not capacity. The script is calibration/alum/64_refit_compare.R.

  • The three refits fit equally well. The best member reaches 0.560 (H0), 0.562 (H1b) and 0.586 (β = 1.43) in the calibration window, from 0.73, 0.73 and 0.66 at the start. Validation is 2.72, 3.00 and 2.68, with the starting member at 2.9, 2.9 and 2.6, so the validation fit does not improve in any run. The data do not choose between the previous stream rule and the thesis rule, or between β = 1 and 1.43, on this objective.
  • The rates are not identified. The 10 to 90% range of every alum rate covers 57 to 82% of its allowed log range, so the final ensemble is close to the prior. Best members differ by a factor of seven with similar fit (k_w 2.6, 0.38 and 1.4 per day for H0, H1b and β = 1.43). The ensemble medians differ much less, within a factor of 1.5 for most rates.
  • β trades off with the rates in the expected direction, but within the spread. With β = 1.43 the median k_w is 1.2 per day against 1.7 to 1.8, w_ala is 1.2 against 1.0 and k_age_w is 0.18 against 0.15. This is less binding in the water column and slightly more ageing and settling, as in the discussion above, but each shift is smaller than the ensemble range.
  • The ratios, surplus share and export of the best members are in the next section (Section 8.1).

8.1 Best member of each refit

The lowest-objective member of each refit was run over 2005 to 2022 with its own stream base (calibration/historical/launch_refit_members.ps1; ratios and fit from calibration/alum/65_refit_members.R). Member 46 from the first part of this page is the reference: its run folder was removed, so its ratios and fit are those in the tables above and are not recomputed here.

  • The refits bind more P than member 46. The total is 19.8 (H0), 22.0 (H1b) and 15.5 (β = 1.43) t P/yr, against 12.4 to 14.1 for member 46. Most of it is in the lake water (16.5, 16.8, 12.9 t P/yr) and the bed term is small (0.4 to 3.0). The whole-system Al:P is 3.0, 2.7 and 3.9 mol/mol (4.8 for member 46), and the lake ratio is 3.2, 2.9 and 4.4. Using β = 1.43 gives the highest ratios because it also lowers the P bound (15.5 t P/yr).
  • No ratio reaches the thesis jar-test value (0.7:1 in the lake) or the field value (about 7:1) as a clean match. The lake ratios of 2.9 to 4.4 sit between them, closer to the jar-test end than the 5.5 of member 46, but the rates that set them are not identified (Table 9).
  • Surplus share. With the thesis rule the Puarenga stream step is at its cap on 99.7% (H1b) and 99.8% (β = 1.43) of dosed days, so the surplus-alum days are almost all the days; the ramp (H0) is at its cap on 60%. On the few days below the cap the ratio is 1.15 (H1b) and 0.80 (β = 1.43, which is 1/β = 0.7 plus a little), which is the thesis value by construction: it is set by ε, not tested by the data. The ramp gives 29 on its uncapped days, which is the low-dose end where the ramp removes little.
  • The lower objective was bought with a worse mean fit of TP and FRP. All three best members have surface TP 18 to 26% low and FRP 43 to 51% low, against -8% and -21% for member 46 with H1b. The terms that improved are the wet-year TP, the annual and year-to-year TN terms and the thermocline terms; the TP bias term went from +0.1 to about -1 on the objective scale in H0 and H1b (from -0.7 to -0.6 in the β = 1.43 refit, whose start was already low). The β = 1.43 member is the least biased (TP -18%, FRP -43%, chlorophyll -12%), but all three would need a check against the TP and FRP targets before use. This is an objective-weighting issue, not a stream-rule effect, since H0 shows it too.
  • Export is 8.6 (H0), 8.0 (H1b) and 9.3 (β = 1.43) t P/yr, against 10.6 for member 46. The reduction from alum needs the no-alum partner of each refit member, which has not been run, so no export reduction is reported here.

9 Next steps

  1. Puarenga data. The Puarenga rule is the open question: the ramp (ε 0.04) and the thesis (ε about 1) both fit what is available. The McIntosh paired up- and downstream spot samples (2011 to 2013, Hamilton et al. 2015 Fig 20) or the BoPRC upstream and downstream monitoring would fit ε and f_max directly. They are not in the repository.
  2. Utuhina. Use the plateau (0.43, interval 0.40 to 0.45) as the removal rule, which is the previous 0.45 within error. Decide whether the plateau is a contact-time limit or an artefact of the reconstructed untreated DRP (the pre-dosing DRP:TP ratio).
  3. Lake refits are done (Section 8, Section 8.1): the three fit equally well on the objective, the rates are not identified, and the lower objective came with a worse mean TP and FRP fit. Next: re-weight the TP and FRP bias terms and refit, and run the no-alum partners to report the export reduction.
  4. Report the surplus share with every ratio, so that the comparison with the thesis uses only the unsaturated days.

The code is in calibration/alum/60_h1_base.R (H1 and H1b inflows), 61_h1_compare.R (comparison), 62_stream_fit.R (step 1), 63_stoich_base.R (lake stoichiometry in R) and calibration/historical/run_hist.R (the member run, with HIST_BASE set to the new base).

## What the thesis gives {#sec-thesis}

The thesis result, as stated by the author:

Where post-treatment DRP remained measurable, Al:P molar ratios were near 1:1 (Lake Rotorua 0.7:1; Puarenga Stream 0.98:1). Where DRP fell below detection, results indicated surplus alum (aluminium) dosing, and a minimum effective dose could not be determined.

Three consequences for the model:

1. **The stoichiometric rate is about 1 mol Al per mol P** when P is limiting, for both the Puarenga and the lake water. The module's capacity ratio of 1 matches this. The 26:1 of the stream ramp does not.
2. **Surplus doses cannot give a ratio.** If DRP is removed to below detection, the ratio is an upper bound on the dose needed, not a measurement. The model ratio should be compared on days when the model leaves measurable DRP (unsaturated days), and the share of Al applied on saturated days should be reported with it.
3. **The data are from jar tests** with hours of contact in mixed samples. The stream reaches are 680 to 1360 m, with minutes of contact, so the thesis gives the efficiency of the chemistry, which the reach can only equal or fall short of. This is the reason to keep an efficiency parameter (H2) and not fix it at the thesis value.

The thesis range also includes surplus-dose ratios up to 15.1:1. The thesis also cites an operational field ratio of about 7:1 for the Utuhina and Puarenga inflows (McDowell and Hawke, 2006), which it attributes to turbulence, short flocculation contact times and hydrodynamic losses in the stream. That is a dose-over-removal figure for real dosing, so it includes surplus days. Its jar-test value for Lake Rotorua (0.7:1, a 1.0 mg Al/L dose removing 1.60 of 1.62 mg DRP/L) is the best-case chemistry.

## Design {#sec-design}

### Hypotheses for the stream step {#sec-hypotheses}

The stream step binds `B` mmol P per m³ of inflow, taken from the free capacity.

| Run | Stream step | Free parameters |
|---|---|---|
| H0 (previous) | Puarenga `0.9·min(1, R/23)`, Utuhina flat 0.45 | none, reference |
| H1 (thesis stoichiometry) | both streams: `B = min(f_max · FRP, ε · dose)`, ε = 1 mol P per mol Al | none (`f_max` = 0.9, as the old cap) |
| H1b (thesis, Puarenga only) | Puarenga as H1, Utuhina as H0 | none |
| H2 (calibrated) | as H1 with ε free per stream | ε (Puarenga), ε (Utuhina), `f_max` |

H1b is there because H1 contradicts a measurement for the Utuhina (@sec-h1). Priors for H2: ε between 0.04 (the ramp) and 1 (the thesis), `f_max` between 0.7 and 1.

### Lake side, the same for all runs {#sec-lake}

- **Stoichiometry, set in R.** The module uses capacity one for one with P (`aed_alum.F90`), and no parameter is added. The thesis lake ratio of 0.7:1 means 1 mol of Al binds β = 1/0.7 = 1.43 mol P, so the input is scaled instead (@sec-stoich): `ALU_ala` and `ALU_alp` become P-binding capacity, β times the Al dose, and aluminium is the capacity divided by β wherever it is reported. H1 and H1b keep β = 1.
- **Rates.** Refit `w_ala` (thesis 0.3 to 1.5 m/d), `K_P` (about 0.3), `k_w`, `k_s`, `k_age_w`, `k_age_s`, `k_bur`, `k_res` as in stage `p4`, from the member of the joint stage. The sensitivity screen found the alum parameters carry 6.5% of the objective, and `k_age_w` is the first driver of the alum in the water.

### Targets {#sec-targets}

- **Streams.** The measured Utuhina downstream DRP, and the McIntosh Puarenga paired up- and downstream samples used directly. The Puarenga series in the inflow file cannot be used because it is built from the ramp.
- **Lake.** The existing calibration and held-out targets of the joint stage (TP, FRP, chlorophyll, oxygen), and the ERI 178 aluminium survey once the values are in hand.

### What to compare across runs {#sec-compare}

1. The objective on the calibration and held-out windows, and the surface TP, FRP and chlorophyll bias.
2. Al:P on unsaturated days for the streams, the lake and the whole system, against about 1.
3. The share of Al applied on saturated (surplus) days for each stream.
4. The reduction in P export with alum (14.9 t P/yr in the previous setup).
5. Whether the new parameters narrow under the ensemble, and whether ε and `k_w` trade off (both set how fast P is removed).

### Stages {#sec-stages}

1. **H1 and H1b on the best member** (this page, @sec-h1): one run each, no refit. Shows what the thesis rule does before any calibration.
2. **H2 ensemble**: ε and `f_max` free together with the lake alum parameters, ES-MDA as in stage `p4`.
3. **Compare** H0, H1b and H2 on the criteria above, and decide the stream rule.

The base is the previous (flood × 0.5) base with the untreated FRP and the dose rebuilt from it, so every other inflow variable is the same; the rebuild reproduces the previous bound P to machine precision (`calibration/alum/60_h1_base.R`). ### What this shows

- **The thesis and the ramp agree where it matters.** Both give near-complete removal at large doses. They differ only at low doses, which are a small part of the Al applied.
- **The thesis 1:1 cannot be tested against the field doses.** On 99.6% of the dosed days the thesis rule saturates, so the data cannot show an efficiency. The thesis says this too ("a minimum effective dose could not be determined").
- **H1 fails for the Utuhina** because the measured downstream DRP shows only 44% removal at the same doses. The Utuhina reach therefore does not reach the jar-test efficiency, which supports a calibrated ε (H2) at least for that stream.
- **The lake data do not respond to the stream rule**, so the choice should rest on the stream measurements and on physical grounds, not on the lake fit.

This is one member with its parameters unchanged, not a calibration. The lake alum parameters (`k_w`, `K_P`, `w_ala`, ageing and burial) were fitted under the previous rule and the lake already compensates for the difference, so a refit is the proper test of whether the thesis stoichiometry changes the lake parameters.

## Step 1: the stream rule against the stream observations {#sec-fit}

This step fits the rule `f = min(f_max, ε R)` to the stream data alone, with no lake model (`calibration/alum/62_stream_fit.R`). `R` is the dose over the untreated DRP, in mol Al per mol P, and ε is the mol of P bound per mol of Al.

**The data.** The Utuhina inflow was sampled downstream of the dosing point, so on dosed days the removal is `f_obs = 1 − DRP(measured) / DRP(untreated)`, taking the untreated DRP from the reconstructed no-alum series (Hamilton et al. 2015, p. 36). Both series are daily, interpolated from monthly samples, so the 4,980 dosed days of 2007 to 2022-06 are not independent. The intervals below come from a bootstrap over the 178 calendar months.

For the Puarenga there is nothing to fit. The McIntosh spot samples (Hamilton et al. 2015, Fig 20) are not in the repository, and the Puarenga treated DRP in the with-alum file is built from the ramp: its removal by dose-ratio bin follows `0.9·min(1, R/23)` (@tbl-fit-byR), so fitting it would only return the ramp. The report text constrains the Puarenga only from below: more than 80% reduction at a mass ratio Al:PO₄-P of about 20 (R = 23 mol/mol) needs ε ≥ 0.8/23 = 0.035, and the ramp (0.9/23 = 0.039) sits at that bound. The thesis value of about 1 is 25 times higher, and the data in hand cannot choose between them.


**What the Utuhina data say**

- **The removal is a plateau near 43%, not a function of the dose.** The fitted `f_max` is 0.42 (0.40 to 0.45), and the flat rule fits as well as the two-parameter rules (RMSE 0.200 for all three). The previous flat 0.45 is inside the interval and is as good (RMSE 0.202). The previous ramp is worse (0.266) and H1 much worse (0.514), because both over-remove at low and moderate doses.
- **ε is not identified.** Its interval runs from 0.14 to 2.1, so the Utuhina data are consistent with the thesis value (1) and with values 5 times lower. The plateau is reached at the lowest doses (a mean of 25 to 45 kg Al/d at R between 1 and 5 already gives 43 to 49%), so the dose never limits the removal, and the data cannot show what limits it.
- **The removal rises only slightly at high doses**: 55% at R of 23 to 50 and 65% above 50, from 480 days. This is the only sign of a dose response.
- **Something other than capacity limits the Utuhina removal.** A dose-limited reaction at the thesis stoichiometry would remove 100% of the DRP at R above 1, and the Utuhina removes 43%. Either the 1360 m reach is too short for the reaction to finish (a contact time limit), or the reconstructed untreated DRP is biased, because it comes from the pre-dosing DRP:TP ratio and that ratio may have moved for other reasons. The data in hand cannot separate the two.

## Lake stoichiometry set in R {#sec-stoich}

The module binds one mol of P per mol of capacity, and nothing is added to it. The thesis lake ratio (0.7 mol Al per mol P) is applied in R by scaling the input (`calibration/alum/63_stoich_base.R`): the dose delivered to the module is β = 1/0.7 = 1.43 times the Al dose, so `ALU_ala` and `ALU_alp` are in mmol of P-binding capacity per m³, not mmol of Al. The stream step is applied to this capacity (`B = min(f_max · FRP, capacity)`), and the Al reported anywhere is the capacity divided by β. The run below uses the Puarenga with the thesis rule (cap 0.9) and the Utuhina with its measured 0.45 (H1b), on member 46.

This changes the model, as intended: each mol of capacity consumed now stands for 1/β mol of Al, so the same Al binds 1.43 times more P. It is not independent of the lake rates, though. Capacity is lost mostly by ageing and settling (57% and 28% of the water column losses in member 46), so a larger β raises P uptake by much less than β, and β trades off with `k_w` and `k_age_w`, which set how much capacity binds P before it ages out. In a refit β is therefore fixed (at 1 or 1.43) and the rates are fitted, not both.

```{r}
#| label: tbl-h1-beta
#| tbl-cap: "Member 46 with the lake stoichiometry set to 0.7 mol Al per mol P (β = 1.43) and the lake rates unchanged, against the previous setup and H1b. Al is the capacity divided by β."
h <- read_csv("data/h1/h1_compare.csv", show_col_types = FALSE) |> filter(run %in% c("Previous (ramp)", "H1b (thesis, Puarenga)", "H1b + 0.7:1 lake (beta 1.43)"))
f <- read_csv("data/h1/h1_fit.csv", show_col_types = FALSE) |> filter(run %in% h$run) |> select(run, variable, bias_pct) |> tidyr::pivot_wider(names_from = variable, values_from = bias_pct)
h <- left_join(h, f, by = "run")
kable(data.frame(Run = h$run, `Total P bound (t P/yr)` = sprintf("%.1f", h$P_bound_total_t_yr), `Al:P lake` = sprintf("%.1f", h$AlP_lake), `Al:P whole` = sprintf("%.2f", h$AlP_whole), `Export (t P/yr)` = sprintf("%.1f", h$export_2011_21_t_yr), `TP bias` = sprintf("%.0f%%", h$PHS_tp), `FRP bias` = sprintf("%.0f%%", h$PHS_frp), check.names = FALSE))
```

With the rates unchanged, β = 1.43 raises the P bound from 12.6 to 14.1 t P/yr (+12%, not +43%), lowers the export from 10.5 to 9.1 t P/yr, and pushes the surface bias to -21% for TP and -48% for FRP, against -8% and -21% before. The whole-system ratio falls to 4.2 mol Al per mol P and the lake ratio to 4.9. The fit is worse because the lake rates were fitted with one-for-one binding, so the lake now removes too much P. A refit of `k_w`, `K_P` and the ageing rates under β = 1.43 is needed before the fit can be compared, and it is the test of whether the data prefer the thesis value.

The ramp gives 29 on its uncapped days, which is the low-dose end where the ramp removes little.
- **The lower objective was bought with a worse mean fit of TP and FRP.** All three best members have surface TP 18 to 26% low and FRP 43 to 51% low, against -8% and -21% for member 46 with H1b. The terms that improved are the wet-year TP, the annual and year-to-year TN terms and the thermocline terms; the TP bias term went from +0.1 to about -1 on the objective scale in H0 and H1b (from -0.7 to -0.6 in the β = 1.43 refit, whose start was already low). The β = 1.43 member is the least biased (TP -18%, FRP -43%, chlorophyll -12%), but all three would need a check against the TP and FRP targets before use. This is an objective-weighting issue, not a stream-rule effect, since H0 shows it too.
- **Export** is 8.6 (H0), 8.0 (H1b) and 9.3 (β = 1.43) t P/yr, against 10.6 for member 46. The reduction from alum needs the no-alum partner of each refit member, which has not been run, so no export reduction is reported here.

## Next steps {#sec-next}

The McIntosh paired up- and downstream spot samples (2011 to 2013, Hamilton et al. 2015 Fig 20) or the BoPRC upstream and downstream monitoring would fit ε and `f_max` directly. They are not in the repository.
