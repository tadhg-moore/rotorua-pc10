Climate-Scenario Meteorology Workflow

cmip6-processing.qmd and cmip6-visualisation.qmd extract and plot the raw, absolute CMIP6/CCAM values at the lake point (gcm_point_data_df). Raw GCM output is not suitable for directly driving a sub-daily lake model: it carries its own absolute-level biases (mean offset, distorted seasonal cycle, wrong wind climatology) that would land straight in simulated stratification, ice and evaporation, and it is daily on a coarse grid with no sub-daily information.

This page instead builds the meteorological forcing the lake model actually uses, following the metscale package’s bias-correct → delta-change → disaggregate workflow (vignette("scenario-workflow", package = "metscale"), vignette("climate-extremes") for storm/heatwave-focused alternatives):

  1. Bias-correct hourly ERA5-Land against the buoy record.
  2. Apply each GCM/scenario’s monthly delta-change signal to the corrected baseline (never to the raw reanalysis, and never to the delta itself).
  3. Disaggregate the projected daily series back to hourly.

The corrected reanalysis carries real weather — genuine day-to-day persistence, event structure and a real diurnal cycle, all on actual calendar dates. Warping it with a model’s own future-vs-historical change (which is far more reliable than the model’s absolute output) keeps that physical realism while shifting its statistics onto each scenario. See the vignette for the full reasoning and the calendar-handling details (NorESM2-MM runs a 365_day calendar, NZESM a 360_day calendar – metscale::climate_point_monthly_climatology() decodes each correctly before averaging).

These steps are wired into _targets.R as era5_hourly_met → era5_bias_correction → era5_corrected_hourly / era5_corrected_daily_baseline → gcm_monthly_deltas_df → scenario_daily_met → scenario_hourly_met, added alongside (not replacing) the existing raw gcm_point_data* targets.

Air temperature, wind speed, shortwave radiation and relative humidity all improve out-of-sample after the monthly scale correction (day-of-year loess-smoothed offset). Station pressure (MET_prsttn) is a known data issue, not yet resolved: the raw bias between ERA5 (Pa) and the buoy’s PrBaro field is ~96 kPa, an order of magnitude larger than the ~3 kPa elevation-offset effect the metscale vignette describes for its own site – this looks like a unit mismatch in buoy_to_aeme_met() (PrBaro likely arrives in hPa, not Pa) rather than a real atmospheric difference, and should be checked before MET_prsttn from this workflow is trusted.

Update: rainfall (MET_pprain) is now included in this correction, having previously been left uncorrected on the assumption that the buoy gauge was an unreliable target. A multi-source comparison (buoy vs. airport vs. ERA5 vs. BOPRC/PC10 stations – see met-source-comparison.qmd) found no evidence the buoy under-reads rain relative to the land stations, but did surface a real unit bug: the raw ERA5-Land CSV stores MET_pprain in mm/hour, not the m/hour every other source in this pipeline uses, which was silently inflating ERA5 rainfall ~1000x wherever it passed uncorrected. That’s fixed at the source in read_era5_hourly_met(). The buoy’s rain record is still noisier than its other variables (weak correlation with the long land records), so treat the corrected MET_pprain here with more caution than temperature/humidity/wind – see the comparison page for the numbers.

Further update – found and fixed: comparing the raw buoy PpRain record against its own known climatology surfaced a systematic ~10x inflation from the start of the record (Feb 2022) through October 2023 – mean daily rain of ~47 mm/day (annualising to ~17,000 mm/yr, physically impossible for Rotorua) versus ~4 mm/day (~1,500 mm/yr, matching raw ERA5 and the 125-year Whakarewarewa gauge closely) from late October 2023 onward. The root cause is a recording-unit change at the buoy on 2023-10-23, not an instrument calibration drift: before that date PpRain was logged in mm/10min (not mm) and WndSpd in knots (not m/s). Both are now converted in rotorua_buoy_met_data (_targets.R) – PpRain / 10 and WndSpd * 0.514444 – for the pre-2023-10-23 rows before being combined with the already-correct-unit rows after it, so era5_bias_correction now fits against a consistently-unit’d buoy record across its full span. The plots below reflect whatever era5_bias_correction/era5_corrected_hourly build was current when last rendered – re-render this page after a fresh tar_make() to confirm the corrected distribution’s tail now tracks the buoy/long-record rainfall instead of overshooting it.

3. CMIP6 monthly delta-change factors, and inter-GCM variability

For each of the 7 downscaled GCMs (ACCESS-CM2, AWI-CM-1-1-MR, CNRM-CM6-1, EC-Earth3, GFDL-ESM4, NZESM, NorESM2-MM) and each SSP (ssp126/ssp245/ssp370/ssp585, NZESM has no ssp585 run), compute_gcm_monthly_deltas() reads that GCM’s own already-cropped netCDFs directly at the lake point and computes a monthly change factor – additive for temperature and shortwave, multiplicative (ratio) for the bounded/skewed fields (rainfall, wind, humidity) – for three future windows against a 1995-2014 historical reference.

The dominant source of uncertainty in this workflow, once the downscaling chain and delta-change method are fixed, is which GCM drives the projection. The figure below makes that spread explicit for the end-of-century window:

The spread is not uniform across variables. Annual-mean warming under ssp585 ranges from +2.8 °C (NorESM2-MM) to +4.6 °C (ACCESS-CM2) by 2071-2100 – a ~1.8 °C spread from GCM choice alone, on top of the ~3.5 °C spread already visible between the lowest (ssp126) and highest (ssp585) emissions pathway. Shortwave and rainfall change factors span, and in some months straddle, both sides of no-change (ratio = 1 / delta = 0) across the GCM ensemble, meaning some models project a drier or cloudier Rotorua in a given month under the same scenario that others project the opposite sign for. Practical implication for lake modelling: a single-GCM projection (as the previous scenario_batch_rotorua.R operational script and the metscale vignette both use, NorESM2-MM) understates the true range of plausible future forcing – it sits toward the low end of the warming spread shown here. Any lake-response conclusion sensitive to warming magnitude (stratification strength/duration, hypolimnetic anoxia onset) should be checked against more than one GCM, not just the emissions scenario.

4. Delta-changed baseline and hourly disaggregation

scenario_daily_met applies each GCM/scenario/window’s monthly deltas to the corrected daily baseline (apply_gcm_delta_to_baseline()), and scenario_hourly_met disaggregates the result to hourly with metscale::disaggregate_met_to_hourly(), borrowing the within-day shape of era5_corrected_hourly (method of fragments) and reconstructing shortwave from clear-sky solar geometry – identical to steps 3-4 of vignette("scenario-workflow"), just run once per combination of scenario_delta_grid (7 GCM × 4 SSP × 3 windows, minus NZESM/ssp585 = 83 combinations).

That is enough runs (each disaggregating 20-30 years to hourly) that, like gcm_ts_summary and tutira_cmip6_files elsewhere in this pipeline, both targets are gated with cue = tar_cue(mode = "never") so a routine tar_make() does not trigger them. Build them explicitly with:

targets::tar_make(names = c("scenario_daily_met", "scenario_hourly_met"))

or invalidate just the combinations that changed with tar_invalidate() / tar_cue(mode = "always") per branch.

These time-slice runs are the fixed-climate set: each window is a snapshot of one climate state on the same ERA5 weather, with no trend inside it. They are the right tool for asking “what does the lake look like in a 2071-2100 climate?”, but not for the long-term nutrient-loading question, which is covered next.

5. Transient scenarios for long-term nutrient-loading runs

The lake’s response to a nutrient reduction (e.g. an N cut from 2032) is path-dependent: sediment legacy stores, alum dosing and flushing mean the state in any year depends on the whole history of loads and climate before it. Three disconnected time slices cannot represent that – stitching them together would also put steps in the forcing at the window boundaries. The transient workflow instead builds one continuous 2005-2099 series per GCM/SSP (2005-2014 is the 10-year spin-up before sim_periods$ssp$start), so the climate warms gradually while the loads change. It runs alongside the time-slice set, which stays as the fixed-climate sensitivity.

It follows the same bias-correct → delta-change → disaggregate chain, with three changes, all in R/scenario_climate_workflow.R:

  1. Weather: build_transient_year_map() assigns every simulated year a real, complete ERA5 baseline year (complete_years(); shuffled cycles, so every source year is used about equally often and a weather year is not repeated at a fixed lag the way a simple loop would). Leap-year targets only draw leap-year sources, so no 29 February is invented or dropped. The mapping depends only on transient_weather_seed, so every GCM/SSP sees identical weather and differences between runs are the climate signal alone. build_transient_weather() stitches the years together on the target dates.
  2. Deltas that evolve: compute_gcm_transient_deltas() replaces the three fixed windows with a 30-year window sliding along each GCM’s own historical → SSP run, giving a monthly delta for every year, against the same 1995-2014 reference as the slices. It reads each GCM’s point series once and averages locally, rather than re-reading the netCDFs per year.
  3. A no-delta control: scenario = "control" applies no deltas to the same weather sequence (stationary baseline climate), the reference for separating a nutrient effect from a climate effect.

apply_transient_deltas_to_baseline() then applies the year-and-month delta to each day (the same add/ratio rules, clipping and expand_met() as the slices), and disaggregate_scenario_met() disaggregates to hourly with wind handled as a plain scalar, exactly as in step 4.

Things to keep in mind when reading transient runs:

  • Weather sampling noise is real. Decadal means from a single shuffled 95-year sequence wander by roughly ±0.3 °C and ±15% in rainfall relative to the long-term mean, purely from which ERA5 years land in which decade. Compare each scenario against the control (same weather), never against the observed record.
  • The weather is synthetic, including the spin-up decade. 2005-2014 uses shuffled ERA5 years with small deltas (about +0.1 to +0.5 °C by 2014, from the GCMs’ own historical-to-SSP run), not the actual weather of those years. The transient runs therefore cannot be validated against observations; the calibration/validation period stays on the real ERA5/buoy forcing.
  • The trajectory lags at the end. The sliding window is truncated at 2100 rather than shifted, so the final years are averaged over fewer years (down to ~16 at 2099, see n_years) and lag the underlying trend by up to ~6 years of change. Shifting the window back instead held the last ~15 years flat and under-warmed the end of the run by ~0.6 °C under ssp585, which is why it was changed. Rainfall, wind and shortwave deltas also get noisier over those final years.
  • tas/pr stop at 2099-12-31 while other variables run to 2100-11, so the 2071-2100 end windows average 29 years for temperature and rainfall (extract_climate_point() warns “NA after merge” for those dates; this is expected).
  • Deltas are piecewise-constant by month, as in the slices, so there is a small step at each month boundary.
  • One GCM member per GCM: the 30-year window averages out most, but not all, of each model’s internal variability.

How this feeds into the lake model

For long-term nutrient-loading questions, use scenario_transient_hourly_met, one branch per row of transient_delta_grid. scenario_hourly_met (one branch per GCM/scenario/window) remains the input for fixed-climate sensitivity runs. The intended path from here into a model run is:

  1. AEME::add_met(aeme, met = scenario_transient_hourly_met[[i]]), where i is the row of transient_delta_grid for the GCM/SSP of interest (e.g. which(grid$gcm == "ACCESS-CM2" & grid$scenario == "ssp245")), in place of the placeholder gcm_point_data_std_df-based forcing in setup_aeme().
  2. AEME::set_time() with sim_periods$ssp (2015-07-01 to 2099-06-30, 10-year spin-up – the met starts in 2005 so the spin-up is covered).
  3. AEME::build_aeme() / AEME::run_aeme() as for the calibration runs in aeme_glm4.

What is still missing before a transient simulation is meaningful for nutrients: the met is only half the forcing.

  • Inflows and loads for 2005-2099. setup_aeme() currently removes the inflows, so a run built that way has no catchment loading at all. A transient run needs a flow and nutrient-load series for the whole period, including the N-reduction pathway (e.g. a step from 2032), and a decision on how stream flows respond to the rainfall deltas (for example scaling the historical flow series by the same monthly rainfall ratio, or modelling it explicitly).
  • Alum dosing after the calibration period needs a defined assumption.
  • Lake level / outflow handling over 95 years should be checked in a short run first.

A sensible first set of test simulations is the control plus one GCM under a low and a high SSP (e.g. ssp126 and ssp585), first over a short horizon (say 2015-2040) with loads held at the present baseline, to check the runs are stable and the climate signal comes through in the temperature and stratification output. The 2032 N-reduction step and the long-horizon runs can follow once the inflow/loads series exist. With 28 branches the same pattern = map(transient_delta_grid) branching extends to a per-branch AEME::run_aeme() target, and the GCM-variability figure above is then the basis for interpreting how much of the simulated spread is driven by GCM choice, scenario, or the nutrient pathway versus lake-model internal dynamics.

Caveats

Both the bias-correction and the delta-change step assume stationarity – a transfer function fitted on the ERA5/buoy overlap period is asserted to hold under a future climate, and the scale method used here rescales the observed daily sequence rather than reshaping its day-to-day variability (see ?metscale::scenario_workflow). The buoy overlap (2022-2026) is short relative to the vignette’s recommendation of a long reanalysis record and 20-year climate windows; the ERA5-only record (2011-2026) is longer and used for the daily baseline and hourly donor, but the fit of the bias correction itself is necessarily anchored to the shorter buoy overlap. Treat the across-scenario spread as a lower bound on total uncertainty, and fix the MET_prsttn unit issue noted in step 1 before using corrected station pressure downstream.

[`cmip6-processing.qmd`](cmip6-processing.qmd) and [`cmip6-visualisation.qmd`](cmip6-visualisation.qmd) extract and plot the **raw, absolute** CMIP6/CCAM values at the lake point (`gcm_point_data_df`). This page instead builds the meteorological forcing the lake model actually uses, following the `metscale` package's **bias-correct → delta-change → disaggregate** workflow (`vignette("scenario-workflow", package = "metscale")`, `vignette("climate-extremes")` for storm/heatwave-focused alternatives):

1.  **Bias-correct** hourly ERA5-Land against the buoy record.
2.  Apply each GCM/scenario's **monthly delta-change** signal to the *corrected* baseline (never to the raw reanalysis, and never to the delta itself).
3.  **Disaggregate** the projected daily series back to hourly.

The corrected reanalysis carries real weather --- genuine day-to-day persistence, event structure and a real diurnal cycle, all on actual calendar dates. Warping it with a model's own future-vs-historical *change* (which is far more reliable than the model's absolute output) keeps that physical realism while shifting its statistics onto each scenario. Air temperature, wind speed, shortwave radiation and relative humidity all improve out-of-sample after the monthly `scale` correction (day-of-year loess-smoothed offset). **Station pressure (`MET_prsttn`) is a known data issue, not yet resolved**: the raw bias between ERA5 (Pa) and the buoy's `PrBaro` field is \~96 kPa, an order of magnitude larger than the \~3 kPa elevation-offset effect the `metscale` vignette describes for its own site -- this looks like a unit mismatch in `buoy_to_aeme_met()` (`PrBaro` likely arrives in hPa, not Pa) rather than a real atmospheric difference, and should be checked before `MET_prsttn` from this workflow is trusted.

**Update**: rainfall (`MET_pprain`) is now included in this correction, having previously been left uncorrected on the assumption that the buoy gauge was an unreliable target. A multi-source comparison (buoy vs. airport vs. ERA5 vs. BOPRC/PC10 stations -- see [`met-source-comparison.qmd`](met-source-comparison.qmd)) found no evidence the buoy under-reads rain relative to the land stations, but did surface a real **unit bug**: the raw ERA5-Land CSV stores `MET_pprain` in mm/hour, not the m/hour every other source in this pipeline uses, which was silently inflating ERA5 rainfall \~1000x wherever it passed uncorrected. That's fixed at the source in `read_era5_hourly_met()`. The buoy's rain record is still noisier than its other variables (weak correlation with the long land records), so treat the corrected `MET_pprain` here with more caution than temperature/humidity/wind -- see the comparison page for the numbers.

**Further update -- found and fixed**: comparing the raw buoy `PpRain` record against its own known climatology surfaced a systematic \~10x inflation from the start of the record (Feb 2022) through October 2023 -- mean daily rain of \~47 mm/day (annualising to \~17,000 mm/yr, physically impossible for Rotorua) versus \~4 mm/day (\~1,500 mm/yr, matching raw ERA5 and the 125-year Whakarewarewa gauge closely) from late October 2023 onward. The root cause is a recording-unit change at the buoy on 2023-10-23, not an instrument calibration drift: before that date `PpRain` was logged in mm/10min (not mm) and `WndSpd` in knots (not m/s). Both are now converted in `rotorua_buoy_met_data` (`_targets.R`) -- `PpRain / 10` and `WndSpd * 0.514444` -- for the pre-2023-10-23 rows before being combined with the already-correct-unit rows after it, so `era5_bias_correction` now fits against a consistently-unit'd buoy record across its full span. The plots below reflect whatever `era5_bias_correction`/`era5_corrected_hourly` build was current when last rendered -- re-render this page after a fresh `tar_make()` to confirm the corrected distribution's tail now tracks the buoy/long-record rainfall instead of overshooting it.

## 3. CMIP6 monthly delta-change factors, and inter-GCM variability

For each of the 7 downscaled GCMs (`ACCESS-CM2`, `AWI-CM-1-1-MR`, `CNRM-CM6-1`, `EC-Earth3`, `GFDL-ESM4`, `NZESM`, `NorESM2-MM`) and each SSP (`ssp126`/`ssp245`/`ssp370`/`ssp585`, `NZESM` has no `ssp585` run), `compute_gcm_monthly_deltas()` reads that GCM's own already-cropped netCDFs directly at the lake point and computes a monthly change factor -- **additive** for temperature and shortwave, **multiplicative (ratio)** for the bounded/skewed fields (rainfall, wind, humidity) -- for three future windows against a 1995-2014 historical reference.

The dominant source of uncertainty in this workflow, once the downscaling chain and delta-change method are fixed, is *which GCM* drives the projection. The figure below makes that spread explicit for the end-of-century window:

![Inter-GCM spread in monthly delta-change factors, 2071-2100. Thin lines are individual GCMs, the shaded band spans the GCM min-max range, and the thick line is the GCM mean, for each SSP.](www/plots/gcm_delta_variability_2071-2100.png)

The spread is not uniform across variables. Annual-mean warming under `ssp585` ranges from **+2.8 °C (`NorESM2-MM`) to +4.6 °C (`ACCESS-CM2`)** by 2071-2100 -- a \~1.8 °C spread from GCM choice alone, on top of the \~3.5 °C spread already visible between the lowest (`ssp126`) and highest (`ssp585`) emissions pathway. Shortwave and rainfall change factors span, and in some months straddle, both sides of no-change (ratio = 1 / delta = 0) across the GCM ensemble, meaning some models project a *drier* or *cloudier* Rotorua in a given month under the same scenario that others project the opposite sign for. Wind and humidity changes are comparatively modest and more consistent in sign across GCMs. The mid-century window shows the same pattern at reduced magnitude:

![Inter-GCM spread in monthly delta-change factors, 2041-2070.](www/plots/gcm_delta_variability_2041-2070.png)

Collapsing each GCM's 12 monthly values into a single box per model makes the same GCM-consensus-vs-disagreement question easier to read at a glance, independent of the seasonal cycle:

![GCM consensus in delta-change factors, 2071-2100. Each box summarises one GCM's 12 monthly values; overlapping boxes indicate GCM consensus, separated boxes indicate disagreement between models.](www/plots/gcm_delta_boxplot_2071-2100.png)

![GCM consensus in delta-change factors, 2041-2070.](www/plots/gcm_delta_boxplot_2041-2070.png)

**Practical implication for lake modelling:** a single-GCM projection (as the previous `scenario_batch_rotorua.R` operational script and the `metscale` vignette both use, `NorESM2-MM`) understates the true range of plausible future forcing -- it sits toward the *low* end of the warming spread shown here. Any lake-response conclusion sensitive to warming magnitude (stratification strength/duration, hypolimnetic anoxia onset) should be checked against more than one GCM, not just the emissions scenario.

## 4. Delta-changed baseline and hourly disaggregation

`scenario_daily_met` applies each GCM/scenario/window's monthly deltas to the corrected daily baseline (`apply_gcm_delta_to_baseline()`), and `scenario_hourly_met` disaggregates the result to hourly with `metscale::disaggregate_met_to_hourly()`, borrowing the within-day shape of `era5_corrected_hourly` (method of fragments) and reconstructing shortwave from clear-sky solar geometry -- identical to steps 3-4 of `vignette("scenario-workflow")`, just run once per combination of `scenario_delta_grid` (7 GCM × 4 SSP × 3 windows, minus `NZESM`/`ssp585` = 83 combinations).

That is enough runs (each disaggregating 20-30 years to hourly) that, like `gcm_ts_summary` and `tutira_cmip6_files` elsewhere in this pipeline, both targets are gated with `cue = tar_cue(mode = "never")` so a routine `tar_make()` does not trigger them. Build them explicitly with:

``` r
targets::tar_make(names = c("scenario_daily_met", "scenario_hourly_met"))
```

or invalidate just the combinations that changed with `tar_invalidate()` / `tar_cue(mode = "always")` per branch.

These time-slice runs are the **fixed-climate** set: each window is a snapshot of one climate state on the same ERA5 weather, with no trend inside it. They are the right tool for asking "what does the lake look like in a 2071-2100 climate?", but not for the long-term nutrient-loading question, which is covered next.

## 5. Transient scenarios for long-term nutrient-loading runs

The lake's response to a nutrient reduction (e.g. an N cut from 2032) is **path-dependent**: sediment legacy stores, alum dosing and flushing mean the state in any year depends on the whole history of loads *and* climate before it. Three disconnected time slices cannot represent that -- stitching them together would also put steps in the forcing at the window boundaries. The transient workflow instead builds **one continuous 2005-2099 series per GCM/SSP** (2005-2014 is the 10-year spin-up before `sim_periods$ssp$start`), so the climate warms gradually while the loads change. It runs alongside the time-slice set, which stays as the fixed-climate sensitivity.

It follows the same bias-correct → delta-change → disaggregate chain, with three changes, all in `R/scenario_climate_workflow.R`:

1.  **Weather:** `build_transient_year_map()` assigns every simulated year a real, complete ERA5 baseline year (`complete_years()`; shuffled cycles, so every source year is used about equally often and a weather year is not repeated at a fixed lag the way a simple loop would). Leap-year targets only draw leap-year sources, so no 29 February is invented or dropped. The mapping depends only on `transient_weather_seed`, so **every GCM/SSP sees identical weather** and differences between runs are the climate signal alone. `build_transient_weather()` stitches the years together on the target dates.
2.  **Deltas that evolve:** `compute_gcm_transient_deltas()` replaces the three fixed windows with a 30-year window sliding along each GCM's own `historical` → SSP run, giving a monthly delta for every year, against the same 1995-2014 reference as the slices. It reads each GCM's point series once and averages locally, rather than re-reading the netCDFs per year.
3.  **A no-delta control:** `scenario = "control"` applies no deltas to the same weather sequence (stationary baseline climate), the reference for separating a nutrient effect from a climate effect.

`apply_transient_deltas_to_baseline()` then applies the year-and-month delta to each day (the same add/ratio rules, clipping and `expand_met()` as the slices), and `disaggregate_scenario_met()` disaggregates to hourly with wind handled as a plain scalar, exactly as in step 4.

``` r
targets::tar_make(names = "scenario_transient_hourly_met")
```

**Things to keep in mind when reading transient runs:**

-   **Weather sampling noise is real.** Decadal means from a single shuffled 95-year sequence wander by roughly ±0.3 °C and ±15% in rainfall relative to the long-term mean, purely from which ERA5 years land in which decade. Compare each scenario against the `control` (same weather), never against the observed record.
-   **The weather is synthetic, including the spin-up decade.** 2005-2014 uses shuffled ERA5 years with small deltas (about +0.1 to +0.5 °C by 2014, from the GCMs' own historical-to-SSP run), not the actual weather of those years. The transient runs therefore cannot be validated against observations; the calibration/validation period stays on the real ERA5/buoy forcing.
-   **The trajectory lags at the end.** The sliding window is truncated at 2100 rather than shifted, so the final years are averaged over fewer years (down to \~16 at 2099, see `n_years`) and lag the underlying trend by up to \~6 years of change. Shifting the window back instead held the last \~15 years flat and under-warmed the end of the run by \~0.6 °C under `ssp585`, which is why it was changed. Rainfall, wind and shortwave deltas also get noisier over those final years.
-   **`tas`/`pr` stop at 2099-12-31** while other variables run to 2100-11, so the 2071-2100 end windows average 29 years for temperature and rainfall (`extract_climate_point()` warns "NA after merge" for those dates; this is expected).
-   **Deltas are piecewise-constant by month**, as in the slices, so there is a small step at each month boundary.
-   **One GCM member per GCM:** the 30-year window averages out most, but not all, of each model's internal variability.

## How this feeds into the lake model

For long-term nutrient-loading questions, use `scenario_transient_hourly_met`, one branch per row of `transient_delta_grid`. `scenario_hourly_met` (one branch per GCM/scenario/window) remains the input for fixed-climate sensitivity runs. The intended path from here into a model run is:

1.  `AEME::add_met(aeme, met = scenario_transient_hourly_met[[i]])`, where `i` is the row of `transient_delta_grid` for the GCM/SSP of interest (e.g. `which(grid$gcm == "ACCESS-CM2" & grid$scenario == "ssp245")`), in place of the placeholder `gcm_point_data_std_df`-based forcing in `setup_aeme()`.
2.  `AEME::set_time()` with `sim_periods$ssp` (2015-07-01 to 2099-06-30, 10-year spin-up -- the met starts in 2005 so the spin-up is covered).
3.  `AEME::build_aeme()` / `AEME::run_aeme()` as for the calibration runs in `aeme_glm4`.

**What is still missing before a transient simulation is meaningful for nutrients:** the met is only half the forcing.

-   **Inflows and loads for 2005-2099.** `setup_aeme()` currently removes the inflows, so a run built that way has no catchment loading at all. A transient run needs a flow and nutrient-load series for the whole period, including the N-reduction pathway (e.g. a step from 2032), and a decision on how stream flows respond to the rainfall deltas (for example scaling the historical flow series by the same monthly rainfall ratio, or modelling it explicitly).
-   **Alum dosing** after the calibration period needs a defined assumption.
-   **Lake level / outflow** handling over 95 years should be checked in a short run first.

A sensible first set of **test simulations** is the `control` plus one GCM under a low and a high SSP (e.g. `ssp126` and `ssp585`), first over a short horizon (say 2015-2040) with loads held at the present baseline, to check the runs are stable and the climate signal comes through in the temperature and stratification output. The 2032 N-reduction step and the long-horizon runs can follow once the inflow/loads series exist. With 28 branches the same `pattern = map(transient_delta_grid)` branching extends to a per-branch `AEME::run_aeme()` target, and the GCM-variability figure above is then the basis for interpreting how much of the simulated spread is driven by GCM choice, scenario, or the nutrient pathway versus lake-model internal dynamics.

## Caveats

Both the bias-correction and the delta-change step assume **stationarity** -- a transfer function fitted on the ERA5/buoy overlap period is asserted to hold under a future climate, and the `scale` method used here rescales the *observed* daily sequence rather than reshaping its day-to-day variability (see `?metscale::scenario_workflow`). The buoy overlap (2022-2026) is short relative to the vignette's recommendation of a long reanalysis record and 20-year climate windows; the ERA5-only record (2011-2026) is longer and used for the daily baseline and hourly donor, but the *fit* of the bias correction itself is necessarily anchored to the shorter buoy overlap. Treat the across-scenario spread as a lower bound on total uncertainty, and fix the `MET_prsttn` unit issue noted in step 1 before using corrected station pressure downstream.
