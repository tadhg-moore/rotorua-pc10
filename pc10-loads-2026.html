2026 PC10 river loads: what changed from the current inflow file

Comparison of the Limnotrack/BoPRC 2026 loads with rotorua_inflows_populated_noalum.csv

Author

Tadhg Moore

Published

November 12, 2026

1 Summary

In November 2026 Limnotrack (C. G. McBride) delivered daily GLM-AED boundary-condition files for Lake Rotorua for BoPRC, 1 July 1959 to 30 June 2026. They come from Long-term nutrient loads, retention and water quality for Lake Rotorua: 1965 to 2026. This page compares them with the inflow file the model currently uses (data/raw/flows/rotorua_inflows_populated_noalum.csv, which ends 30 June 2022) and says where they differ.

Key points

  1. Total P is lower in the new files, mostly in wet years. Over 2007 to 2022 the mean TP load is 41.1 t/y against 49.1 t/y in the current file (-16%). TN is 546 against 550 t/y (-1%) and FRP is 25.3 against 27.5 t/y (-8%).
  2. The wet years carry the difference. In 2011, 2012, 2017 and 2018 the new TP loads are 56, 52.4, 51.4, 59.8 t against 69.3, 63.9, 77, 80.9 t. These are the years where the lake TP was overestimated by 66 to 113%, which we worked around by halving flood-day particulates (STR_FLOODSCALE = 0.5). The new files treat flood-day particulates explicitly instead (Section 5).
  3. The record is four years longer (to June 2026). The ERA5 forcing ends on 31 December 2025, so the new targets branch cuts the inflows there (Section 11).
  4. The alum and no-alum sets give the in-stream binding rules an independent check. Dosing lowers FRP in the Utuhina by about 42% and in the Puarenga by about 64%, with TP unchanged (Section 7).
  5. In a forward run of the reference member the new loads change the lake little (TP bias within a few points, wet-year TP overshoot smaller, level unchanged); the new silica changes chlorophyll much more (Section 10).
  6. The lower and upper sets give 5th and 95th percentile load histories and nostorm sets give the PC10 target basis (Section 2).

2 What is in the data

Twelve complete sets, one folder each, named <alum|noalum>_<storm|nostorm>_<central|lower|upper>.

Part Option Meaning
alum alum / noalum P as it entered the lake with alum dosing (Utuhina from June 2006, Puarenga from January 2010) / as it would have without dosing
storm storm / nostorm with / without stormflow particulates (extra particulate N, P and sediment on flood days). nostorm is the basis of the PC10 targets (435 t N/y, 37 t P/y)
bound central, lower, upper best estimate / 5th and 95th percentile of the annual TN and TP load uncertainty

Each folder holds nine stream files, Geothermal, ungauged slow and quick flow, WWTP, inflow_all_combined, outflow_ohau and an optional rain_on_lake file. Concentrations are already in AED variables and units (mmol m-3, flow in m3 s-1), unlike the current file (mg L-1, m3 day-1). The project reader read_pc10_loads() converts them to the current layout (Section 11) and reproduces check_annual_loads.csv to within 0.012% in all twelve sets.

3 Annual loads, old and new

Loads are computed from the species the model reads (ammonium, nitrate, DON, PON for N; FRP, DOP, POP, PIP for P), for both the current and the new inflows. The current file also has its own TN and TP columns, which give higher totals than the sum of its species over 2007 to 2022 (TN 575 against 550 t/y and TP 50.8 against 49.1 t/y); the difference is N and P that the old file does not allocate to a model variable. Against the TP column, the new TP is 19% lower.

The new series used for the comparison is noalum_storm_central, because the current file is a no-alum file with flood-day particulates included.

The percentage differences are small before about 2006 (TP -7% on average over 1990 to 2006) and larger afterwards (-14% over 2007 to 2022). TN differs by -1% and FRP by -8% over 2007 to 2022.

4 Where the differences are: by inflow

The largest percentage changes in TP are in the ungauged slow flow, ungauged quick flow, Ngongotaha, Waiteti and Puarenga (all lower), with a rise in the Waiowhiro. The Hamurana, Awahou, Waingaehe and the geothermal inflow hardly change. The ungauged slow flow also drops from 29.6 to 20.2 Mm3/y and the Waiohewa flow rises by about a quarter.

Flow differs because the new files redo the lake water balance and the stream flow models (Section 8). Concentrations differ because the new loads are rebuilt from the monitoring samples with the storm and speciation treatment described below.

5 Wet years and flood-day particulates

In the current file particulate P is 42% of TP, against 29% in the new files without storm particulates and 34.5% with them (Table 4), and the largest TP differences are in the wet years (Table 2). The new report refits the particulate regressions (concentration against flow, per stream, with a common trend over time) and applies them only on flood days, defined as flow above twice the stream median. The extra material is split into particulate organic N and organic and inorganic P. On average it adds 14 t N/y and 4.0 t P/y over 2007 to 2026.

The storm component is 3.7 t P/y on average (2007 to 2022) and reaches 11.5 t in the wettest year (2011). The model has so far been run with the old particulates scaled by 0.5 on flood days; this scaling has no counterpart in the new files and should not be applied to them.

6 Speciation

The AED state variables need organic and particulate parts that the monitoring does not measure. The new report splits them with fractions held constant over time: DON is 0.50 of non-inorganic N at base flow and 0.25 on flood days; DOP is 0.10 of non-DRP P; particulate P is the rest, split 0.20 organic and 0.80 inorganic; POC is 10 times PON by mass.

7 Alum: with and without dosing

In the alum sets part of the FRP of the Utuhina and Puarenga becomes particulate inorganic P (PHS_frp_ads). TP and all N are identical in the two scenarios. The Utuhina is sampled below its plant, so the alum set uses the measured DRP and the noalum set rebuilds the undosed DRP; the Puarenga is sampled above its plant, so the noalum set uses the measured DRP and the alum set estimates the dosed DRP from the daily dose and the Al:DRP binding ratio of McBride et al. (2022).

The Utuhina reduction is close to the fixed 0.45 binding fraction in the model. The Puarenga reduction (about 63%) is below the 0.9 maximum of the model rule, which depends on the daily Al:FRP ratio; this table compares period means only, and a day-by-day comparison with the model rule has not been made. Because the model applies alum in-stream, the noalum sets are the natural inflows with the alum module on; the alum sets can be used to check the binding rules, not to drive the model.

8 Flows and the 2022 to 2026 extension

The new mean Ōhau outflow over the same years is 17.1 m3 s-1. In the new files the ungauged flow is the residual of the daily lake water balance (Ōhau outflow plus evaporation minus gauged streams, geothermal, rain on the lake and storage change), split into quick and slow flow. The geothermal inflow is a constant 0.6 m3 s-1.

The four new years (2023 to 2026) have mean loads of 641 t N/y, 39.6 t P/y and 24.5 t FRP/y, compared with 546, 41.1 and 25.3 for 2007 to 2022. Year 2026 ends in June 2026 but the forcing ends in December 2025, so a model run on this branch covers the first half of that year only.

9 Uncertainty bounds

The bounds scale concentrations only (flows are unchanged so that the water balance and lake level stay closed), so a run on a bound is a load history that is low or high in every year, not a random draw. The mean band is about 0.87 to 1.16 of central for TP (individual years range from 0.73 to 1.28). The difference between the current file and the new central set over 2007 to 2022 (Section 1) is of the same size as this 5 to 95% band, which is worth keeping in mind when judging calibration against either.

10 Forward run of the reference member

To see what the new loads do to the lake, the reference member of the one-stage calibration (calibration/all_stage/out_main/best.rds, new GLM-AED executable) was run from 1 January 1976 on three sets of inflows, with everything else unchanged (scripts in calibration/pc10_loads/, 02_build_forward.R to 04_compare_forward.R).

Run Inflows Silica in inflows Ends
old the calibrated base (base_Q50, which halves flood-day particulate P) constant 200 mmol m-3 2022-06-29
new noalum_storm_central, GLM-native units constant 200 mmol m-3 (as old) 2025-12-31
newsil noalum_storm_central stream-specific silica of the new files 2025-12-31

For the new runs ALU_ala is rebuilt from the dosing records and the new flows (it reproduces the old dose to 1e-5), and the in-stream binding rules of calibration/alum/stream_variant.R are applied to the new FRP (Utuhina 0.45, Puarenga 0.9 min(1, R/23)). The constant outflow file (6.06 m3 s-1, the rest of the outflow being the calibrated weir) is extended to the end of 2025. Model values are the mean over the top 10 m and are compared with the mean of the integrated samples at sites 2 and 5 (TP includes the refractory DOP pool); bottom oxygen is the lowest 3 m against the deepest profile samples. The forcing ends on 31 December 2025, so that is where the new runs stop.

10.1 Lake bias and skill

What the tables show:

  • The new loads change the lake little. TP bias over 2006 to 2018 goes from -14% to -17% and is -31% against -31% in 2018 to 2022. Chlorophyll, TN, nitrate and ammonium are essentially unchanged. Part of the reason may be that the old base already halves flood-day particulate P, so the unscaled new loads end up in a similar place (not tested separately).
  • The model is still low on P in the later years: TP -31% in 2018 to 2022 and -33% in 2022 to 2025 with the new loads. The shortfall is the same in the old run, so it comes from the model, not from the loads data.
  • Silica is the one input that clearly matters. With the stream silica of the new files the chlorophyll bias changes from -36% to -9% (2006 to 2018) and from -23% to 19% (2018 to 2022), and the positive nitrate and FRP biases of 2006 to 2018 shrink. Most likely silica no longer limits diatom growth, but the diatom and nutrient output were not checked. The reference member was tuned with silica fixed at 200 mmol m-3, so the real silica should go in with a refit of the phytoplankton parameters, not before. Correlations with observed chlorophyll stay near zero in all runs.
  • Bottom oxygen is 22% too high in 2022 to 2025 (less anoxia in the model than observed).

10.2 Wet years and the new years

The 2017 and 2018 TP overshoot is smaller with the new loads and the 2017 FRP overshoot is about halved. 2011 and 2012 hardly change. The observed annual means rest on few samples per year, so single years should be read with care.

10.3 Water balance

The level error is almost the same with the two inflow sets (within a few centimetres in most years), including after 2012, so the weir calibrated on the old inflows still balances the new water budget. The Ōhau series of the new files was not used; the old constant outflow file was extended instead.

11 How the new data enter the pipeline

A parallel set of targets, suffixed _pc10, is in _targets.R under PC10 loads 2026 branch. The existing inflow targets are not touched, and nothing in the old chain depends on the new ones.

Target What it does
pc10_loads_set The set to use (default noalum_storm_central). Change to any of the twelve names
pc10_loads_files The files of that set (a file target, so edits to the data re-run the chain)
pc10_loads_stop Last date of the corrected ERA5 forcing, used to cut the inflows
rotorua_inflow_list_pc10 read_pc10_loads(): the set converted to the current list layout (mg L-1, m3 day-1, AEME names)
rotorua_inflow_list_matrix_pc10 Adds ALU_ala and ALU_alp from the dosing records, as the existing matrix target does
aeme_base_pc10_file, aeme_baseline_pc10 Builds aeme_base_pc10.rds with build_calib_base_aeme(), in calibration/base_build_pc10

This page reads its data from the targets store. pc10_compare (built by pc10_compare_data() from the raw sets synced from OneDrive, so it also works in CI) holds the annual loads of the old file and of three central sets. pc10_fwd_summary, pc10_fwd_annual and pc10_fwd_level are file targets for the result tables of the forward runs (Section 10). Those GLM runs are local only (about 7 GB of output each, calibration/pc10_loads/02_build_forward.R to 04_compare_forward.R), so the three small CSVs in calibration/pc10_loads/compare/ are tracked in git.

Units. read_pc10_loads() converts the mmol m-3 nutrient columns back to mg L-1 with the molar masses used in the report so that the existing AEME conversion applies unchanged. The converted loads equal check_annual_loads.csv to within 0.012% in all twelve sets (script calibration/pc10_loads/01_verify_loads.R).

Points to check before calibrating on the new base:

  • the rain file (rain_on_lake_OPTIONAL.csv) is not used, because rain enters through the meteorology file;
  • the current file holds constants for some variables in every inflow (SiO2 = 1, DIC = 10, and pH values as low as 1), whereas the new files give stream-specific silica (53 to 109 g SiO2 m-3), DIC from alkalinity and pH from measurements. Silica is converted to mg Si L-1 for consistency with rotorua_inflow_key.csv, so check what the AEME inflow conversion expects for SIL_rsi and CAR_dic before comparing lake silica or pH;
  • the previously fitted particulate and phosphorus parameters (w_pom, rpop_hydrol, w_po4ads, sediment P) were tuned against the higher old loads, so a forward run with the final member on the new base should come before any refit.

## Speciation {#sec-spec}

The AED state variables need organic and particulate parts that the monitoring does not measure. The new report splits them with fractions held constant over time: DON is 0.50 of non-inorganic N at base flow and 0.25 on flood days; DOP is 0.10 of non-DRP P; particulate P is the rest, split 0.20 organic and 0.80 inorganic; POC is 10 times PON by mass.

```{r}
#| label: tbl-spec
#| tbl-cap: "Load-weighted composition of TN and TP, 2007 to 2022, all inflows. Shares are of the total in each file."
comp <- function(a, nm) a |> filter(hy %in% 2007:2022) |>
  summarise(across(c(tn, din, tp, frp, dop, pp), sum)) |>
  mutate(src = nm,
         `DIN/TN` = din / tn, `(DON+PON)/TN` = 1 - din / tn,
         `FRP/TP` = frp / tp, `DOP/TP` = dop / tp, `PP/TP` = pp / tp) |>
  select(src, `DIN/TN`, `(DON+PON)/TN`, ends_with("/TP"))
bind_rows(comp(a_old, "old file"), comp(a_new, "new files")) |>
  mutate(across(-src, \(x) 100 * x)) |>
  rename(Source = src) |>
  kable(digits = 1)
```

## Alum: with and without dosing {#sec-alum}

## Forward run of the reference member {#sec-forward}

To see what the new loads do to the lake, the reference member of the one-stage calibration (`calibration/all_stage/out_main/best.rds`, new GLM-AED executable) was run from 1 January 1976 on three sets of inflows, with everything else unchanged (scripts in `calibration/pc10_loads/`, `02_build_forward.R` to `04_compare_forward.R`).

| Run | Inflows | Silica in inflows | Ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| `old` | the calibrated base (`base_Q50`, which halves flood-day particulate P) | constant 200 mmol m^-3^ | 2022-06-29 |
| `new` | `noalum_storm_central`, GLM-native units | constant 200 mmol m^-3^ (as old) | 2025-12-31 |
| `newsil` | `noalum_storm_central` | stream-specific silica of the new files | 2025-12-31 |

For the new runs `ALU_ala` is rebuilt from the dosing records and the new flows (it reproduces the old dose to 1e-5), and the in-stream binding rules of `calibration/alum/stream_variant.R` are applied to the new FRP (Utuhina 0.45, Puarenga 0.9 min(1, R/23)). The constant outflow file (6.06 m^3^ s^-1^, the rest of the outflow being the calibrated weir) is extended to the end of 2025. Model values are the mean over the top 10 m and are compared with the mean of the integrated samples at sites 2 and 5 (TP includes the refractory DOP pool); bottom oxygen is the lowest 3 m against the deepest profile samples. The forcing ends on 31 December 2025, so that is where the new runs stop.


What the tables show:

- **The new loads change the lake little.** TP bias over 2006 to 2018 goes from `r round(g("TP", "2 calibration 2006-18", "old"))`% to `r round(g("TP", "2 calibration 2006-18", "new"))`% and is `r round(g("TP", "3 held out 2018-22", "new"))`% against `r round(g("TP", "3 held out 2018-22", "old"))`% in 2018 to 2022. Chlorophyll, TN, nitrate and ammonium are essentially unchanged. Part of the reason may be that the old base already halves flood-day particulate P, so the unscaled new loads end up in a similar place (not tested separately).
- **The model is still low on P in the later years**: TP `r round(g("TP", "3 held out 2018-22", "new"))`% in 2018 to 2022 and `r round(g("TP", "4 new years 2022-25", "new"))`% in 2022 to 2025 with the new loads. The shortfall is the same in the old run, so it comes from the model, not from the loads data.
- **Silica is the one input that clearly matters.** With the stream silica of the new files the chlorophyll bias changes from `r round(g("chl", "2 calibration 2006-18", "new"))`% to `r round(g("chl", "2 calibration 2006-18", "newsil"))`% (2006 to 2018) and from `r round(g("chl", "3 held out 2018-22", "new"))`% to `r round(g("chl", "3 held out 2018-22", "newsil"))`% (2018 to 2022), and the positive nitrate and FRP biases of 2006 to 2018 shrink. Most likely silica no longer limits diatom growth, but the diatom and nutrient output were not checked. The reference member was tuned with silica fixed at 200 mmol m^-3^, so the real silica should go in with a refit of the phytoplankton parameters, not before. Correlations with observed chlorophyll stay near zero in all runs.
- **Bottom oxygen** is `r round(g("DO_bot", "4 new years 2022-25", "new"))`% too high in 2022 to 2025 (less anoxia in the model than observed).

## How the new data enter the pipeline {#sec-targets}

A parallel set of targets, suffixed `_pc10`, is in `_targets.R` under *PC10 loads 2026 branch*. The existing inflow targets are not touched, and nothing in the old chain depends on the new ones.

| Target | What it does |
|---|---|
| `pc10_loads_set` | The set to use (default `noalum_storm_central`). Change to any of the twelve names |
| `pc10_loads_files` | The files of that set (a file target, so edits to the data re-run the chain) |
| `pc10_loads_stop` | Last date of the corrected ERA5 forcing, used to cut the inflows |
| `rotorua_inflow_list_pc10` | `read_pc10_loads()`: the set converted to the current list layout (mg L^-1^, m^3^ day^-1^, AEME names) |
| `rotorua_inflow_list_matrix_pc10` | Adds `ALU_ala` and `ALU_alp` from the dosing records, as the existing matrix target does |
| `aeme_base_pc10_file`, `aeme_baseline_pc10` | Builds `aeme_base_pc10.rds` with `build_calib_base_aeme()`, in `calibration/base_build_pc10` |

This page reads its data from the targets store. `pc10_compare` (built by `pc10_compare_data()` from the raw sets synced from OneDrive, so it also works in CI) holds the annual loads of the old file and of three central sets. `pc10_fwd_summary`, `pc10_fwd_annual` and `pc10_fwd_level` are file targets for the result tables of the forward runs (@sec-forward). Those GLM runs are local only (about 7 GB of output each, `calibration/pc10_loads/02_build_forward.R` to `04_compare_forward.R`), so the three small CSVs in `calibration/pc10_loads/compare/` are tracked in git.

Units. `read_pc10_loads()` converts the mmol m^-3^ nutrient columns back to mg L^-1^ with the molar masses used in the report so that the existing AEME conversion applies unchanged. The converted loads equal `check_annual_loads.csv` to within 0.012% in all twelve sets (script `calibration/pc10_loads/01_verify_loads.R`).

Points to check before calibrating on the new base:

- the rain file (`rain_on_lake_OPTIONAL.csv`) is not used, because rain enters through the meteorology file;
- the current file holds constants for some variables in every inflow (SiO2 = 1, DIC = 10, and pH values as low as 1), whereas the new files give stream-specific silica (53 to 109 g SiO2 m^-3^), DIC from alkalinity and pH from measurements. Silica is converted to mg Si L^-1^ for consistency with `rotorua_inflow_key.csv`, so check what the AEME inflow conversion expects for `SIL_rsi` and `CAR_dic` before comparing lake silica or pH;
- the previously fitted particulate and phosphorus parameters (`w_pom`, `rpop_hydrol`, `w_po4ads`, sediment P) were tuned against the higher old loads, so a forward run with the final member on the new base should come before any refit.
