Ōhau Channel Weir and GLM Outflow Setup

  • Show All Code
  • Hide All Code
  • View Source

From the physical weir, stoplogs and gauged level/flow to the GLM crest elevation, width and factor

Author

Tadhg Moore

Published

19 October 2026

Note

Lake Rotorua has a single surface outflow, the Ōhau Channel, which is controlled by a weir with removable stoplogs. GLM cannot represent a two-level crest with stoplogs, so we replace it with one equivalent crest (elevation, width, and a dimensionless factor) fitted to the gauged lake level and channel discharge. This page documents the physical weir, the data used, and how the GLM parameters are derived.

1. The physical weir

The weir spans the 26 m wide Ōhau Channel. It has two crest levels:

  • a narrow, deep lower crest (4.6 m wide, 278.166 m) in the centre of the channel, and
  • a higher upper crest (279.316 m) across the remaining 21.4 m.

Up to three stoplogs, each 0.270 m high, can be stacked on the lower crest to raise the low-flow control level. Their installation and removal are tied to lake-level triggers.

3. Physical weir rating curves

For each stoplog count the discharge is the sum of the flow over the lower and the upper sections, each treated as a broad-crested rectangular weir at critical flow:

\[ Q = 1.705\, C_d \Big[\, b_{low}\,(h - z_{low})_+^{3/2} + b_{up}\,(h - z_{up})_+^{3/2} \Big] \]

where \(1.705 = \tfrac{2}{3}\sqrt{2g/3}\) is the critical-flow broad-crested weir coefficient (SI units), \(h\) is lake level, \(z_{low} = 278.166 + n \times 0.270\) is the lower crest with \(n\) stoplogs, \(z_{up} = 279.316\), \(b_{low} = 4.6\) m, \(b_{up} = 21.4\) m, and \(C_d =\) 0.565 is an empirical discharge coefficient. This is rotorua_weir_flow() / generate_rotorua_weir_curves() in R/rotorua_weir_flow.R, as used in compare_rating_curve.R.

4. How GLM represents the outflow

GLM treats the lake outflow as a single rectangular broad-crested weir. When the water level \(h\) is above the crest, outflow is

\[ Q = \tfrac{2}{3}\, f_c \sqrt{2g}\; w_c\, (h - z_c)_+^{3/2} \]

with three parameters in the .nml:

GLM parameter Namelist entry Meaning
Crest elevation \(z_c\) &morphometry crest_elev Height of the single equivalent crest (m a.s.l.)
Crest width \(w_c\) &outflow crest_width Width of the weir (m)
Crest factor \(f_c\) &outflow crest_factor Dimensionless discharge coefficient, a free scaling on flow

Because the physical weir has two crest levels and a stoplog configuration that changes through time, the GLM parameters are not measured, they are fitted so that the single crest reproduces the gauged rating.

Choices

  • Crest width is fixed at the full channel width, 26 m (ohau_weir_width target), so \(w_c\) is not fitted and the factor absorbs the loss in effective width.
  • Crest elevation and crest factor are fitted by least squares on all paired observations (flow >= 0, finite level and flow).
  • For any trial crest elevation \(z_c\) the best factor has a closed form. With \(q_i = \tfrac{2}{3}\sqrt{2g}\; w_c (h_i - z_c)_+^{3/2}\),

\[ f_c(z_c) = \frac{\sum q_i Q_i}{\sum q_i^2} \]

and optimize() then searches \(z_c\) between the lower and upper physical crests (278.166–279.316 m) to minimise the sum of squared errors.

This is implemented in est_glm_weir_params() (R/est_glm_weir_params.R), run by the glm_weir_params target, and written to the model by format_weir_params().

Equivalent crest versus the real weir

The fitted crest (279.147 m) falls between the lower crest and the upper crest. This is expected: it is a flow-weighted compromise between a narrow section that passes water at low heads and a wide, higher section that dominates at high heads.

Code 
patchwork::wrap_plots(
  plot_rotorua_glm_weir_comparison(crest_elev, crest_factor, n_stoplogs = 0),
  plot_rotorua_glm_weir_comparison(crest_elev, crest_factor, n_stoplogs = 2),
  ncol = 1, guides = "collect"
)

The crest factor does not move the drawn crest. It scales flow. Note the GLM equation uses \(\tfrac{2}{3}\sqrt{2g} = 2.953\) as its base coefficient, whereas the physical curves use \(1.705\,C_d\) = 0.963. Per metre of crest width at the same head, the fitted GLM coefficient is \(2.953 \times 0.362 = 1.07\), of similar magnitude to the physical 0.963. The GLM crest sits lower than the upper crest, so it compensates for the physical weir’s higher crest with a slightly higher unit coefficient over the full 26 m.

5. Writing the parameters into GLM

format_weir_params() returns the table that the calibration workflow applies to glm4.nml. The min and max columns are the parameter bounds for calibration: ±0.2 m on the crest elevation, and −0.1 / +0.2 on the factor.

name value min max
morphometry/crest_elev 279.1469 278.9469 279.3469
outflow/crest_width 26.0000 26.0000 26.0000
outflow/crest_factor 0.3623 0.2623 0.5623
outflow/num_outlet 0.0000 0.0000 0.0000

Resulting namelist entries:

&morphometry
   crest_elev   = <crest_elev>      ! fitted
/
&outflow
   num_outlet   = 0                 ! surface weir only; no submerged outlet
   crest_width  = 26.0              ! channel width
   crest_factor = <crest_factor>    ! fitted
/

Caveats

  • One curve for all stoplog states. The fit pools all years and stoplog configurations; stoplog history is not used. The GLM rating is a compromise, so it will overpredict flow in periods when logs are in and underpredict when they are out (the scatter at a given level in the figure above shows this spread).
  • Pooled fit weights high flows. Least squares on flow is driven by the large discharges, so the low-head end of the rating is less well matched.
  • Fitted crest is an effective value. It should not be compared with a surveyed crest level, and the bounds for calibration (±0.2 m) are deliberately narrow around it.
  • Datum. Both level and crest elevations are in Moturiki datum, which must match the datum of the lake hypsograph used in GLM (see the crest_elev row in the config notes).

## 4. How GLM represents the outflow

GLM treats the lake outflow as a single rectangular broad-crested weir. When the water
level $h$ is above the crest, outflow is

$$
Q = \tfrac{2}{3}\, f_c \sqrt{2g}\; w_c\, (h - z_c)_+^{3/2}
$$

with three parameters in the `.nml`:

| GLM parameter | Namelist entry | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Crest elevation $z_c$ | `&morphometry` `crest_elev` | Height of the single equivalent crest (m a.s.l.) |
| Crest width $w_c$ | `&outflow` `crest_width` | Width of the weir (m) |
| Crest factor $f_c$ | `&outflow` `crest_factor` | Dimensionless discharge coefficient, a free scaling on flow |

Because the physical weir has two crest levels and a stoplog configuration that changes
through time, the GLM parameters are **not** measured, they are fitted so that the single
crest reproduces the gauged rating.

### Choices

- **Crest width** is fixed at the full channel width, **26 m** (`ohau_weir_width` target),
  so $w_c$ is not fitted and the factor absorbs the loss in effective width.
- **Crest elevation** and **crest factor** are fitted by least squares on all paired
  observations (`flow >= 0`, finite level and flow).
- For any trial crest elevation $z_c$ the best factor has a closed form. With
  $q_i = \tfrac{2}{3}\sqrt{2g}\; w_c (h_i - z_c)_+^{3/2}$,

$$
f_c(z_c) = \frac{\sum q_i Q_i}{\sum q_i^2}
$$

  and `optimize()` then searches $z_c$ between the lower and upper physical crests
  (278.166–279.316 m) to minimise the sum of squared errors.

This is implemented in `est_glm_weir_params()` ([R/est_glm_weir_params.R](../R/est_glm_weir_params.R)),
run by the `glm_weir_params` target, and written to the model by `format_weir_params()`.

### Equivalent crest versus the real weir

The fitted crest (`r round(crest_elev, 3)` m) falls between the lower crest and the upper
crest. This is expected: it is a flow-weighted compromise between a narrow section that
passes water at low heads and a wide, higher section that dominates at high heads.

```{r glm-vs-actual}
#| fig-height: 7
#| fig-cap: "Actual weir (0 and 2 stoplogs) against the GLM single-crest approximation (dashed)."
patchwork::wrap_plots(
  plot_rotorua_glm_weir_comparison(crest_elev, crest_factor, n_stoplogs = 0),
  plot_rotorua_glm_weir_comparison(crest_elev, crest_factor, n_stoplogs = 2),
  ncol = 1, guides = "collect"
)
```

The crest factor does not move the drawn crest. It scales flow. Note the GLM equation
uses $\tfrac{2}{3}\sqrt{2g} = `r round((2/3)*sqrt(2*9.81), 3)`$ as its base coefficient,
whereas the physical curves use $1.705\,C_d$ = `r round(1.705 * cd, 3)`. Per metre of
crest width at the same head, the fitted GLM coefficient is
$`r round((2/3)*sqrt(2*9.81), 3)` \times `r round(crest_factor, 3)` = `r round((2/3)*sqrt(2*9.81)*crest_factor, 3)`$,
of similar magnitude to the physical `r round(1.705 * cd, 3)`. The GLM crest sits lower
than the upper crest, so it compensates for the physical weir's higher crest with a
slightly higher unit coefficient over the full 26 m.

## 5. Writing the parameters into GLM

`format_weir_params()` returns the table that the calibration workflow applies to
`glm4.nml`. The `min` and `max` columns are the parameter bounds for calibration: ±0.2 m
on the crest elevation, and −0.1 / +0.2 on the factor.

### Caveats

- **One curve for all stoplog states.** The fit pools all years and stoplog
  configurations; stoplog history is not used. The GLM rating is a compromise, so it will
  overpredict flow in periods when logs are in and underpredict when they are out
  (the scatter at a given level in the figure above shows this spread).
- **Pooled fit weights high flows.** Least squares on flow is driven by the large
  discharges, so the low-head end of the rating is less well matched.
- **Fitted crest is an effective value.** It should not be compared with a surveyed crest
  level, and the bounds for calibration (±0.2 m) are deliberately narrow around it.
- **Datum.** Both level and crest elevations are in Moturiki datum, which must match the
  datum of the lake hypsograph used in GLM (see the `crest_elev` row in the config notes).
