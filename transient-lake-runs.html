Transient (Continuous) Lake Runs Under a Changing Climate

climate-scenario-workflow.qmd builds the meteorological forcing. This page describes how that forcing is turned into continuous 2015–2099 lake simulations that carry the lake’s memory (sediment P, alum, thermal inertia) through the whole period, instead of three fixed time slices. It records the design decisions, the pipeline, the first test results, and what is still open before the parameter-ensemble runs.

1. Why continuous runs, and the design decisions

Fixed-window delta-change runs answer “what does the lake look like in a 2071–2100 climate?”. They cannot answer path-dependent questions (a nutrient cut from 2032, alum legacy, sediment flushing), and joining the windows would put steps in the forcing. The continuous runs instead use one uninterrupted simulation per GCM/SSP.

Design decisions for the continuous runs.
Decision Choice Reason
Run structure One run per GCM × SSP plus a control, single spin-up, no restarts Sediment, alum and thermal memory carry through
Reporting Scenario minus control, never absolute values The lake drifts under constant forcing; the control isolates the climate effect
Weather Shuffled real ERA5 years (identical for every branch), plus year-evolving monthly GCM deltas Keeps real storm/wind/diurnal structure; the GCM supplies only its change signal
GCM bias correction None needed A delta is future minus the same model’s reference period, so the model’s mean bias cancels. The only bias correction is ERA5 against the buoy
Historical period Not driven by raw GCM historical weather A free-running GCM historical run is not the real weather and cannot be validated; its information already enters through the deltas
Weather-seed replication Optional Weather is shared across branches, so it cancels in scenario − control

What the control is. The control is the same 2015–2099 run on the same weather sequence with no climate deltas, i.e. a stationary baseline climate. Any trend, drift or step it shows comes from the lake itself (spin-up, legacy stores), not from climate. Each scenario is therefore reported as its difference from the control.

What this design does not do. Delta-change leaves day-to-day variability unchanged, so storm and heatwave frequency does not change. If changing variability is the question, a trend-preserving bias-corrected GCM-direct run (e.g. quantile delta mapping) would be needed as a side test.

2. The pipeline

ERA5-Land ──bias-correct (buoy)──▶ era5_corrected_daily_baseline / _hourly
                                         │
transient_year_map (seeded shuffle) ─────┤  weather: real ERA5 year per simulated year
gcm_transient_deltas_df (30-yr sliding) ─┤  deltas: monthly, evolve year by year
                                         ▼
              scenario_transient_hourly_met   (one branch per transient_delta_grid row)
                                         │
 rotorua_inflow_list_matrix ──build_transient_inflows()──▶ paired inflows
                                         ▼
          run_one_transient.R  ──▶  GLM-AED (step-3 calibrated model)
                                         ▼
        daily_<run>.rds  +  glm_csv_<run>/{lake.csv, mass_balance.csv}

The meteorology half is documented in section 5 of the scenario workflow. The new pieces are the inflows and the run driver.

2.1 Inflows that follow the weather (R/transient_inflows.R)

build_transient_inflows() gives each simulated date the inflow of the same calendar day in the ERA5 source year that supplied its weather, so flow and rainfall stay physically paired. Then, when a scenario’s deltas are supplied (and not for the control):

  • Flow is multiplied by the monthly rainfall ratio (MET_pprain) for that year and month. Concentrations are held, so loads scale with flow.
  • Stream temperature is shifted by temp_damp (default 0.5) × the monthly air-temperature delta, the same damping used in calibration/scenarios30. Streams warm less than air.
  • Fixed inflows (geothermal, wastewater) are not scaled.
  • Alum dose load (ALU_ala × flow) on Puarenga and Utuhina is replaced by its mean by day of year over 2016-07-01 to 2022-06-30, then divided by the new flow. This avoids a pre-dosing weather year receiving zero alum.

Known limitations.

  • The inflow records end 2022-06-30, but 9 of the 95 simulated years take weather from 2022–2025. Those years use a seeded substitute source year with a flow record, so their flow is not paired with that year’s weather.
  • Nutrient concentrations are carried over from the source year, so they reflect that year’s historical loading, not present-day loading.
  • Alum dosing after the calibration period is assumed constant at its recent mean (no future dosing policy yet).
  • No future nutrient-reduction pathway (e.g. an N cut from 2032) is applied; this is a climate-only run.

2.2 The run driver (calibration/transient/run_one_transient.R)

Rscript calibration/transient/run_one_transient.R <gcm|none> <scenario|control> [start] [stop]

It reads the branch’s hourly met from the scenario_transient_hourly_met target, builds the paired inflows, drops both into the step-3 calibrated base model (calibration/step3/aeme_base_2005_alum.rds, parameters step3_best), and runs GLM-AED once with a 540-day spin-up. Defaults are the short 2015-07-01 to 2040-06-30 stability test; the full period is 2015-07-01 to 2099-06-29.

Disk use. The model’s output.nc is about 11.6 GB for the 84-year run (roughly 380 KB per simulated day) because it stores every variable at every depth. After the run the script saves

  • daily_<run>.rds: a daily lake summary (surface and bottom temperature, surface and bottom oxygen, surface chlorophyll-a, phosphorus, FRP, total nitrogen), and
  • glm_csv_<run>/lake.csv and mass_balance.csv: lake level and the water balance (about 45 MB),

then deletes the model folder. A full run therefore leaves about 50 MB. (An earlier version did not clean up and three simultaneous runs filled the disk.)

Posterior members. MEMBER=<k> (environment variable) replaces the step-3 best parameters with joint posterior member k of calibration/posterior/members.rds (40 members, each a full 138-parameter table built from the step-2b and step-3 ensembles). MEMBER=0, the default, is the best-parameter run. Outputs get an _m<k> suffix. calibration/transient/run_ensemble.ps1 queues members x branches with a cap on concurrent jobs and skips jobs whose summary already exists, so it can be restarted.

Running more than one at a time. Each branch writes only to its own folder, so branches can run in parallel, but a branch must never be launched twice: duplicate copies share one folder, corrupt the forcing files and fail with “Day not found”.

3. First test results (ACCESS-CM2, one parameter set)

Three full-length runs: control, ssp126 and ssp585, all with the step-3 best parameters. All values are decadal means.

Stratified and anoxic days

Each run also writes annual_<run>.rds: per water year (July–June), the number of stratified days (surface 0–2 m minus bottom ≥17 m temperature ≥ 1 °C, as in scenarios30), anoxic days (bottom ≥17 m mean oxygen < 2 mg/L, as in calibration/anoxia), and the longest unbroken spell of each (R/transient_metrics.R). The daily files carry the same flags (strat, anoxic, wy).

The run summaries (`calibration/transient/runs/daily_*.rds`) are not present in this build, so the figure and table are omitted. Re-run `run_one_transient.R` for the three branches to regenerate them.

What the first runs show:

  • Control stays flat. Decadal surface temperature is 14.7–15.2 °C and bottom oxygen about 9 mg/L with no trend, so the differences are climate signal and not drift.
  • Temperature. ssp126 warms the surface by about 1.5 °C and plateaus after the 2050s; ssp585 keeps warming to about +4.3 °C by the 2090s. Bottom water follows the surface about 0.3 °C behind.
  • Oxygen. Bottom oxygen falls by about 0.5 mg/L (ssp126) and about 1.7 mg/L (ssp585) by the 2090s.
  • Water balance and level. Lake level stays within 21.2–22.2 m in all three runs, and the water-balance residual is 0.08–0.1% of throughput (a constant bookkeeping offset, not a climate effect; its source, probably ice/snow volume or the runoff term, is not yet identified).
  • Chlorophyll-a and phosphorus rise in the scenarios, but treat this as provisional. The control itself swings between decades (surface chl-a about 7.5 mg/m³ in the 2040s and 4.5 in the 2050s) because of weather sampling and carried-over source-year nutrient concentrations, and only one parameter set has been run.

4. Parameter ensemble (ACCESS-CM2, 8 posterior members)

The control, ssp126 and ssp585 branches were each run with 8 posterior parameter sets. The same member is used for a scenario and its control, and the result is scenario minus control within each member, so differences in the lake’s absolute state between members cancel and only the spread in the climate response remains. Each cell is the median across members with the minimum and maximum in brackets (8 members, so this is a range, not a 5–95% interval).

What the ensemble shows:

  • The physical response is not sensitive to the sampled parameters. The surface-warming response is identical across members to within 0.001 °C, even though absolute daily surface temperature differs between members by up to 0.6 °C (bottom temperature by up to 2.3 °C, bottom oxygen by up to 5.8 mg/L). Bottom oxygen and stratified days vary little in response.
  • Anoxic days are fairly robust. For ssp585 in 2070–2099 the range is 14–18 days a year, and ssp126 is far lower. The best-parameter run (14.3 days) sits inside the range.
  • Chlorophyll-a is the uncertain result. The ssp585 increase in 2070–2099 ranges from +1.4 to +8.7 mg/m³, a factor of about six. It is positive in every member and window, so the direction is robust and the size is not.
  • Compared with the other sources of uncertainty (2070–2099, ssp585 anoxic days): the parameter range is about 3.6 days; the GCM-direct side test differs from delta change by about 3.6 days (see the CMIP approaches page); the difference between ssp126 and ssp585 is about 13 days.

Limits of this ensemble. The posterior members come from the biogeochemical and alum calibration steps, so they vary mostly biogeochemistry. Only about 11 of the 138 parameters that differ between members look physical (mixing, light, meteorological scaling), so physical-parameter uncertainty is barely sampled. The near-identical temperature response should not be read as precise. There are only 8 members, one GCM and two scenarios.

5. What is still open

  1. Parameter uncertainty (done for 8 members). See section 4. Remaining: more members if the chlorophyll range needs tightening, and a physics-parameter ensemble, since the posterior members barely vary the physics.
  2. More GCMs. The three runs use ACCESS-CM2 only (the warmest model). NorESM2-MM sits at the cool end; both are needed to bracket the GCM spread. Extending to all 28 branches is feasible now that each run leaves about 50 MB.
  3. Forcing beyond climate. Future nutrient loads (including an N-reduction pathway), a defined post-calibration alum policy, and lake-level/outflow rules.
  4. Optional hindcast. A 1960-to-present run on real bias-corrected ERA5 (no deltas), followed by the transient period with deltas measured against a recent-observation baseline, would give a validatable historical period. It needs inflows, loads and alum back to 1960.
  5. Variability change. Delta-change keeps variability fixed; a bias-corrected GCM-direct side test is the route if storm or heatwave frequency matters.

## 3. First test results (ACCESS-CM2, one parameter set)

Three full-length runs: `control`, `ssp126` and `ssp585`, all with the step-3 best parameters. All values are decadal means.

```{r}
#| label: results
#| eval: !expr have_runs
#| echo: false
f <- c(control = "none_control", ssp126 = "ACCESS-CM2_ssp126", ssp585 = "ACCESS-CM2_ssp585")
d <- bind_rows(lapply(names(f), function(n) {
  r <- readRDS(file.path(runs_dir, paste0("daily_", f[n], ".rds"))); r$run <- n; r
})) |>
  mutate(decade = 10 * (as.integer(format(Date, "%Y")) %/% 10))

vars <- c(temp_surf = "Surface temperature (°C)", temp_bot = "Bottom temperature (°C)",
          oxy_bot = "Bottom oxygen (mg/L)", chl_surf = "Surface chl-a (mg/m³)",
          tp_surf = "Surface TP (mg/L)")

dec <- d |>
  group_by(run, decade) |>
  summarise(across(all_of(names(vars)), \(x) mean(x, na.rm = TRUE)), .groups = "drop")

diff <- dec |>
  tidyr::pivot_longer(all_of(names(vars)), names_to = "var", values_to = "value") |>
  tidyr::pivot_wider(names_from = run, values_from = value) |>
  mutate(ssp126 = ssp126 - control, ssp585 = ssp585 - control) |>
  tidyr::pivot_longer(c(ssp126, ssp585), names_to = "scenario", values_to = "diff") |>
  mutate(var = factor(vars[var], unname(vars)))

ggplot(diff, aes(decade, diff, colour = scenario)) +
  geom_hline(yintercept = 0, colour = "grey60") +
  geom_line(linewidth = 0.9) + geom_point() +
  facet_wrap(~var, scales = "free_y") +
  labs(x = NULL, y = "Scenario minus control (decadal mean)", colour = NULL) +
  theme_bw() + theme(legend.position = "bottom")
```

What the ensemble shows:

-   **The physical response is not sensitive to the sampled parameters.** The surface-warming response is identical across members to within 0.001 °C, even though absolute daily surface temperature differs between members by up to 0.6 °C (bottom temperature by up to 2.3 °C, bottom oxygen by up to 5.8 mg/L). Bottom oxygen and stratified days vary little in response.
-   **Anoxic days are fairly robust.** For ssp585 in 2070--2099 the range is 14--18 days a year, and ssp126 is far lower. The best-parameter run (14.3 days) sits inside the range.
-   **Chlorophyll-a is the uncertain result.** The ssp585 increase in 2070--2099 ranges from +1.4 to +8.7 mg/m³, a factor of about six. It is positive in every member and window, so the direction is robust and the size is not.
-   **Compared with the other sources of uncertainty** (2070--2099, ssp585 anoxic days): the parameter range is about 3.6 days; the GCM-direct side test differs from delta change by about 3.6 days (see the [CMIP approaches page](cmip-impact-modelling-approaches.qmd)); the difference between ssp126 and ssp585 is about 13 days.

**Limits of this ensemble.** The posterior members come from the biogeochemical and alum calibration steps, so they vary mostly biogeochemistry. Only about 11 of the 138 parameters that differ between members look physical (mixing, light, meteorological scaling), so physical-parameter uncertainty is barely sampled. The near-identical temperature response should not be read as precise. There are only 8 members, one GCM and two scenarios.

## 5. What is still open

1.  **Parameter uncertainty (done for 8 members).** See section 4. Remaining: more members if the chlorophyll range needs tightening, and a physics-parameter ensemble, since the posterior members barely vary the physics.
2.  **More GCMs.** The three runs use ACCESS-CM2 only (the warmest model). `NorESM2-MM` sits at the cool end; both are needed to bracket the GCM spread. Extending to all 28 branches is feasible now that each run leaves about 50 MB.
3. **Forcing beyond climate.** Future nutrient loads (including an N-reduction pathway), a defined post-calibration alum policy, and lake-level/outflow rules.
4.  **Optional hindcast.** A 1960-to-present run on real bias-corrected ERA5 (no deltas), followed by the transient period with deltas measured against a recent-observation baseline, would give a validatable historical period. It needs inflows, loads and alum back to 1960.
5.  **Variability change.** Delta-change keeps variability fixed; a bias-corrected GCM-direct side test is the route if storm or heatwave frequency matters.
