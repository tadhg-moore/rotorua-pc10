Purpose and set-up

This page summarises the full GLM-AED output (output.nc) of the alum scenarios run by scripts/alum/01_run_scenarios.R with the calibrated stage 1–3 parameters (calibration/checkpoints/final/), the real inflow/meteorological forcing and the reconstructed alum dosing history (Puarenga and Utuhina dosing, converted to an inflow ALU_ala concentration by calc_alum_inflow()). It follows the diagnostics used in the aed_alum stress tests, but applied to the lake rather than to idealised boxes.

Scenario What changes
S0 no alum (ALU_ala = 0 in all inflows)
S1 historical dosing, default alum parameters (w_ala = 0.5 m/d, 1 mol P capacity per mol Al)
w_ala 0.3, 1, 1.5, 5 floc settling velocity: thesis central value 0.5 m/d, plausible range 0.3–1.5, 5 as a weakly-mixed upper bound
capacity x1.43 … x0.14 P-binding capacity per Al scaled onto the dosed inflows: 1.43 = jar test (Al:P 0.7:1), 0.14 = operational field value (~7:1)
resuspension simResuspension on (thesis recommendation) with k_res 0.001, 0.01, 0.1 d⁻¹
surface dose no inflow dosing; whole-lake S_ala = 0.10 mmol P/m²/d (the thesis’ mean-rate equivalent)

The settling velocities of 20–100 m/d in the thesis are for individually tracked large flocs (1–3 mm) in still water. The thesis explicitly treats them as upper bounds, because wind mixing keeps flocs suspended longer, and recommends 0.5 m/d (0.3–1.5) for the model. An earlier version of this page swept w_ala up to 100 m/d, which is outside that recommendation; it has been replaced by the thesis-based set above.

Note

Interpret differences, not absolute values. The staged calibration fits lake P poorly (stage 3 FRP KGE < 0, positive FRP/TP bias), and the TP series is still rising through the first years of each run. Everything below is therefore expressed as alum minus no-alum (S1 − S0) wherever possible.

Units: concentrations are mmol P/m³ (1 mmol P/m³ = 31 µg P/L); masses are kg P; benthic pools are summed over the four sediment zones.

The nominal capacity added is a large multiple of the FRP actually delivered. Alum is dosed in streams that carry only part of the P load, so most capacity is expected to remain unused or be aged away (§5).

2.1 Comparison with observations

Observations are the buoy/CTD/survey data held in the Aeme object (observations()$lake), averaged by day over the epilimnion (0–5 m) or hypolimnion (15–21 m; the model lake is 21 m deep). The model is sampled over the same depth bands (volume-weighted). The real lake was dosed, so S1 is the scenario to compare with the data; S0 shows how far the no-alum counterfactual departs from what was measured.

3.3 Internal FRP release and bottom-water oxygen

The sediment FRP input flux (fsed_frp, 0.003–0.010 mmol P/m²/d by zone) is not applied as-is: AED scales it by an oxygen half-saturation term, so the realised exchange PHS_frp_dsf is only a fraction of the potential SDF_Fsed_frp whenever the bottom water is oxygenated. Internal loading therefore depends on whether the bottom water goes anoxic.

5. How much P does alum remove?

For each scenario we integrate, over the analysis window:

  • Capacity dosed – ALU_ala delivered by the dosed inflows (kg P-equivalent).
  • P bound – FRP taken up by suspended alum (ALU_uptake_w × layer volume) plus FRP taken up by settled alum (ALU_uptake_s × zone area). These are direct model fluxes.
  • Where the bound P goes – GLM does not output the floc settling or burial fluxes, so they are inferred by mass closure from the pools:
    • exported: outflow volume × surface-layer ALU_alp;
    • settled to bed = water-column uptake − exported − Δ(suspended ALU_alp);
    • buried = settled to bed + benthic uptake − Δ(ALU_ben_alp).
  • As an independent check the burial implied by k_bur × ALU_ben_alp is also integrated; the ratio to the closure-implied burial should be ≈ 1.

7. What to check next

  • Burial check – the k_bur ratio in §5 should be ≈ 1; if not, inspect the benthic bookkeeping (settling flux is not exported by GLM-AED; add a diagnostic).
  • w_ala – the thesis supports 0.5 m/d (0.3–1.5); the larger floc speeds it measured are quiescent upper bounds. Calibrate within about 0.3–1.5 (log scale), and only against FRP/TP in dosed years once the baseline FRP/TP bias is understood.
  • Capacity per Al – the jar-test (0.7:1) and field (~7:1) Al:P ratios span a factor of 10 in effective dose; this is likely a more influential unknown than w_ala and should be a calibration parameter (it scales the dosed ALU_ala).
  • Resuspension – recommended on by the thesis, k_res unconstrained.
  • pH efficiency – the thesis suggests pH_opt = 7, sigma_pH = 1, but this needs aed_carbon (for CAR_pH), which the lake configuration does not include; not tested here.
  • Identifiability – only lake FRP/TP/chl-a constrain the alum parameters (no Al or floc observations), so calibrate at most 3–4 parameters.
  • Natural experiment – Puarenga was not dosed in WY2020. The S1 − S0 response that year is a useful independent check on the dose–response.

## Purpose and set-up

This page summarises the full GLM-AED output (`output.nc`) of the alum

lake rather than to idealised boxes.

|---|---|
| S0 | no alum (`ALU_ala` = 0 in all inflows) |
| S1 | historical dosing, default alum parameters (`w_ala` = 0.5 m/d, 1 mol P capacity per mol Al) |
| `w_ala` 0.3, 1, 1.5, 5 | floc settling velocity: thesis central value 0.5 m/d, plausible range 0.3–1.5, 5 as a weakly-mixed upper bound |
| capacity x1.43 … x0.14 | P-binding capacity per Al scaled onto the dosed inflows: 1.43 = jar test (Al:P 0.7:1), 0.14 = operational field value (~7:1) |
| resuspension | `simResuspension` on (thesis recommendation) with `k_res` 0.001, 0.01, 0.1 d⁻¹ |
| surface dose | no inflow dosing; whole-lake `S_ala` = 0.10 mmol P/m²/d (the thesis' mean-rate equivalent) |

flocs (1–3 mm) in still water. The thesis explicitly treats them as upper bounds, because
wind mixing keeps flocs suspended longer, and recommends 0.5 m/d (0.3–1.5) for the model.

## 5. How much P does alum remove?

For each scenario we integrate, over the analysis window:

* **Capacity dosed** – ALU_ala delivered by the dosed inflows (kg P-equivalent).
* **P bound** – FRP taken up by suspended alum (`ALU_uptake_w` × layer volume)
  plus FRP taken up by settled alum (`ALU_uptake_s` × zone area). These are
  direct model fluxes.
* **Where the bound P goes** – GLM does not output the floc settling or burial
  fluxes, so they are inferred by mass closure from the pools:
    * *exported*: outflow volume × surface-layer ALU_alp;
    * *settled to bed* = water-column uptake − exported − Δ(suspended ALU_alp);
    * *buried* = settled to bed + benthic uptake − Δ(ALU_ben_alp).
* As an independent check the burial implied by `k_bur` × ALU_ben_alp is also
  integrated; the ratio to the closure-implied burial should be ≈ 1.


```{r}
cap_by <- function(tag) {
  il <- read_alum_inflow_loads(tag, start = WIN_START, stop = WIN_STOP)
  cap <- sum(il$ala_kgd)
  if (tag == "SURF") {                       # S_ala (mmol P/m2/d) x surface area x days
    d <- lake |> filter(scenario == labels[["SURF"]], Date >= WIN_START, Date <= WIN_STOP)
    cap <- sum(0.10 * d$surface_area_m2) * MMOL_TO_KG
  }
  cap
}
bud <- lw |> group_by(scenario) |>
  arrange(Date) |>
  summarise(
    uptake_w = sum(uptake_w_kgd), uptake_s = sum(uptake_s_kgd),
    d_alp_w = last(alp_kg) - first(alp_kg),
    d_alp_s = last(ben_alp_kg) - first(ben_alp_kg),
    burial_kbur = sum(K_BUR * ben_alp_kg),
    exported = sum(overflow_m3 * alp_top) * MMOL_TO_KG,
    ala_end = last(ala_kg) + last(ben_ala_kg),
    .groups = "drop") |>
  mutate(tag = tags[match(as.character(scenario), labels[tags])]) |>
  rowwise() |> mutate(capacity = cap_by(tag)) |> ungroup() |>
  mutate(removed = uptake_w + uptake_s,
         settled = uptake_w - exported - d_alp_w,
         buried  = settled + uptake_s - d_alp_s,
         kbur_ratio = burial_kbur / buried,
         eff = removed / capacity)

bt <- bud |> filter(scenario != S0) |>
  transmute(Scenario = as.character(scenario),

            `Total bound (t P)` = removed / 1e3,
            `Efficiency (bound / capacity)` = eff,
            `Exported (t P)` = exported / 1e3,
            `Settled to bed (t P)` = settled / 1e3,
            `Buried (t P)` = buried / 1e3,
            `Pool change: water (t P)` = d_alp_w / 1e3,
```

## 7. What to check next

* **Burial check** – the `k_bur` ratio in §5 should be ≈ 1; if not, inspect the
  benthic bookkeeping (settling flux is not exported by GLM-AED; add a diagnostic).
* **`w_ala`** – the thesis supports 0.5 m/d (0.3–1.5); the larger floc speeds it
  measured are quiescent upper bounds. Calibrate within about 0.3–1.5 (log scale),
  and only against FRP/TP in dosed years once the baseline FRP/TP bias is understood.
* **Capacity per Al** – the jar-test (0.7:1) and field (~7:1) Al:P ratios span a
  factor of 10 in effective dose; this is likely a more influential unknown than
  `w_ala` and should be a calibration parameter (it scales the dosed `ALU_ala`).
* **Resuspension** – recommended on by the thesis, `k_res` unconstrained.
* **pH efficiency** – the thesis suggests `pH_opt` = 7, `sigma_pH` = 1, but this
  needs `aed_carbon` (for `CAR_pH`), which the lake configuration does not include;
  not tested here.
* **Identifiability** – only lake FRP/TP/chl-a constrain the alum parameters
  (no Al or floc observations), so calibrate at most 3–4 parameters.
* **Natural experiment** – Puarenga was not dosed in WY2020. The S1 − S0 response
  that year is a useful independent check on the dose–response.
