Benthic chamber fluxes (2026 and 2003–04), the 5-zone sediment layout, and how the data constrain GLM-AED

ImportantUnpublished data

The 2026 benthic chamber data are unpublished results from a student project, used here with permission: Campbell (pers. comm., 2026). They are shown only as summaries (medians and interquartile ranges by depth). Do not reuse, copy or redistribute them until the results are published.

Summary

  • Benthic chambers were deployed in Lake Rotorua (Campbell, pers. comm., 2026) on 17–20 March 2026 at 10, 15 and 20 m, in light and dark chambers (17 chambers in all). They measured NH4, NOx and PO4 release and sediment oxygen demand (SOD).
  • The fluxes are much lower than in the 2003–04 survey by Burger et al. (2007). At 20 m, PO4 release is about 10× lower (median 4.6 vs 43 mg P/m²/d), NH4 about 6× lower, and SOD about 4× lower.
  • A chamber flux is the release that actually happened at the chamber’s temperature and oxygen. GLM-AED’s fsed_* parameters are maximum rates that the model scales down by temperature and oxygen. The two can only be compared through those scaling terms (below).
  • The model’s 4-zone layout puts the 15 and 20 m chambers in one zone (13–25 m). A 5-zone layout gives each chamber depth its own zone. It is set up as a target (sed_zones) and a calibration base. A new calibration target (ALL_CHAMBER=1) scores each run against the chamber fluxes.

The data

Source: Campbell (pers. comm., 2026), unpublished. The workbook is kept in the project’s restricted OneDrive data folder and read by read_sediment_chamber() into the target sediment_chamber. This page shows only summaries of it.

  • Method. Each rate is the linear-regression slope of chamber concentration over the deployment, times the chamber volume divided by the sediment area. SOD uses only the readings from the start until DO first reached 1 mg/L.
  • Units. Nutrient fluxes are taken as mg N or mg P per m² per day; the workbook header says “mg/m/day”. SOD is in g O2/m²/d.
  • Burger et al. (2007). Chambers at 7, 14 and 20 m in four periods of 2003–04, with the bottom-water DO and temperature on each date (website/data/burger2007_*.csv). Their secondary NH4 rate (12–48 h) is used, which the authors take as the natural release.
  • Sites. Coordinates are in the target sediment_chamber_site (table, map).
  • Overlying water. For 2026 it comes from the mid-day buoy casts during the deployment, interpolated to each chamber depth. DO was 7.3, 7.0 and 6.6 mg/L at 10, 15 and 20 m, at about 19.7 °C.

Sediment zones on the bathymetry

GLM-AED gives each sediment zone one set of release rates. In AED, each zone’s release also uses the average water conditions over that zone’s depth band. The current model has 4 zones. Its deepest zone covers 13–25 m (29 km²) and so mixes water at 15 m, which stays oxygenated, with the anoxic bottom.

The 2026 sites lie on a west–east line from the western shelf to just west of Mokoia Island, about 3.4 km end to end. On the bathymetry their depths are 9.3, 15.6 and 22.2 m. The 20 m site is therefore a little deeper than its name, but it stays in the deepest zone in both layouts.

The 5-zone layout splits that zone at 17.5 m. This is about where the hypolimnion stays below the thermocline (see the zone release section of the synthesis page), and it gives each chamber depth its own zone.

How a chamber flux relates to AED’s fsed

The chamber method (as in Burger et al. 2007) measures a net flux under the chamber conditions: concentration change × chamber volume / sediment area. AED’s Constant2d model applies a maximum rate fsed, scaled by temperature and by the oxygen in the water above the sediment (mmol/m²/d, O2 in mmol/m³):

Flux AED rate
SOD fsed_oxy × O2/(ksed_oxy + O2) × θ_oxy^(T−20)
NH4 fsed_amm × ksed_amm/(ksed_amm + O2) × θ_amm^(T−20)
FRP fsed_frp × ksed_frp/(ksed_frp + O2) × θ_frp^(T−20)

So a chamber flux equals the model’s actual flux at the chamber’s DO and temperature. It equals fsed only in anoxic water at 20 °C. To get fsed from a chamber flux, divide by the oxygen and temperature factor, which depends on the half-saturation constant ksed. ksed is itself a calibration parameter.

Code 
cm <- summ |> filter(source == "chamber_2026", variable %in% c("NH4", "PO4"))
ks <- 10^seq(0, 2.7, length.out = 60)
K <- tidyr::crossing(cm, ksed = ks) |> rowwise() |>
  mutate(k = list(modifyList(SED_K_SODP, if (variable == "NH4") list(ksed_amm = ksed) else list(ksed_frp = ksed))),
         fsed_mg = median / aed_sed_factor(variable, do_mg_l, temp_c, k)) |> ungroup()
ggplot(K, aes(ksed, fsed_mg, colour = factor(depth_m))) + geom_line(linewidth = 0.8) +
  geom_vline(data = data.frame(variable = c("NH4", "PO4"), v = c(SED_K_SODP$ksed_amm, SED_K_SODP$ksed_frp)), aes(xintercept = v), linetype = 2, colour = "grey40") +
  scale_x_log10() + scale_y_log10() + facet_wrap(~ variable, scales = "free_y", labeller = as_labeller(c(NH4 = "fsed_amm (mg N/m²/d)", PO4 = "fsed_frp (mg P/m²/d)"))) +
  scale_colour_manual(values = c(`10` = "#6da7ec", `15` = "#2a78d6", `20` = "#104281"), name = "depth (m)") +
  labs(x = "ksed (mmol O2/m³)", y = "implied fsed")
  • Oxygen sensitivity. The out_gns_sodp calibration chose a very low ksed_frp (2.6), so P release is about 80× faster without oxygen than at 7 mg/L. Burger et al. found SRP release “largely independent of DO”. In the 2026 data, dark and light chambers release similar PO4 even though DO fell during the deployment. Both point to a weaker oxygen dependence. With ksed_frp near 45 (the default), the chamber medians imply fsed_frp of 15–30 mg P/m²/d rather than 200–400.
  • Earlier starting values. These were taken directly from the measured fluxes as fsed (meas_param, fsed_data_start()), with an oxygen correction for SOD only. If those measurements were made in oxygenated water, the NH4 and P starting values were too low by this factor.

Comparison with the calibrated model

calibration/hyps_gns/14_chamber_flux_compare.R puts each member of the out_gns_sodp ensemble through these equations at the 2026 chamber conditions (4-zone model).

  • 15–20 m (zone 1): posterior NH4 (~16 mg N/m²/d) and PO4 (~2 mg P/m²/d) are close to the chambers, through very high anoxic rates damped by oxygen.
  • 10 m (zone 2, 8–13 m): the calibration turned release off (NH4 ~1, PO4 ~0.0001 vs 14 and 3.8 measured).
  • SOD: the model gives 2.5–3.7 g/m²/d at every depth vs 0.4–1.0 measured. The bulk hypolimnetic oxygen demand (AHOD, about 1.5 g/m²/d), which also includes water-column respiration, is being supplied almost entirely by the sediment.

The chamber terms. They need only the parameters, not the model output. For each chamber, the member’s AED flux at that chamber’s DO and temperature is compared with the measured flux in log space. There is one term per variable and zone: the mean log ratio divided by a tolerance of √(0.3² + sd²/n), where 0.3 (about 35%) covers how well a few chambers represent a whole zone. Each term scores the actual release, so ksed, θ and fsed stay free together and are tied to the data at the oxygen and temperature it was measured at. ALL_CHAMBER_SRC=chamber_2026,burger_2007 adds the 2003–04 survey. Its fluxes are pre-alum and several times higher, so it suits a run that is scored on the pre-alum period.

A 5-zone recalibration run (the out_gns_sodp settings plus the chamber target):

SED_NZONE=5 ALL_CHAMBER=1 ALL_FSED=1 ALL_V2=1 ALL_FSED_START=data \
  PT_BASE=calibration/hyps_gns/base_long_gns_sed_5z.rds \
  ALL_TAB_OVERRIDE=calibration/hyps_gns/sed_temp/override_est_5z.csv \
  ALL_MIXHYP_LIM=1.5,3 ALL_FSEDFRP_LIM=1e-4,20 ALL_KSEDFRP_LIM=1,500 \
  ALL_OUT=calibration/all_stage/out_gns_5z Rscript calibration/all_stage/coord_all.R
  • The fsed_frp upper bound is raised to 20 here because the zone 1–3 starting values (7–13 mmol/m²/d at the sodp ksed_frp) sit above the earlier limit of 5.
  • ALL_FSEDOXY_ZLIM refers to zones by number, so limits written for 4 zones do not carry over.

What the check run shows:

  • Splitting the zone changes the model even with the same release rates. In AED each zone uses the average water conditions over its depth band. Total squared error: cal 71.0 → 69.4, val 92.0 → 75.0. Modelled AHOD fell from 1.71 to 1.41 g/m²/d (observed 1.50). Minimum bottom DO got worse (z 0.64 → 1.51). A 5-zone recalibration therefore starts from a different point, not just a finer one.
  • The chamber terms are far from zero for the 4-zone best member. The 10 m zone (zone 3) has almost no NH4 or PO4 release (z −14 and −28), and SOD is 3.5–6.3 tolerances too high at every depth. Together these terms (sum of squares about 1000) would dominate the objective at the start. ALL_CHAMBER_W of about 0.3 would keep them in proportion while the rates move toward the data.

Forward runs with chamber-based release

These runs show how the model responds to sediment release set from the chamber data. Each starts from the out_gns_sodp best member; only the sediment parameters change, and nothing is recalibrated. They are full runs from 2002 to 2022 on the 5-zone base, with one 4-zone reference run.

  • Chamber values. The fsed values come from target sed_fsed_anchor_2026. They are set so that each zone’s release at the chamber DO and temperature equals the chamber median.
  • Shallow zones. Zones 4–5 (0–8 m) have no 2026 data. They take the 10 m values unless the run says “zones 1–3”.
  • Scripts. calibration/hyps_gns/16_forward_5z_pars.R builds the parameter sets, run_forward_5z.sh runs them, and 18_forward_compare.R compares them.

What the runs show:

  • The chamber SOD removes the anoxia. With the chamber values, sediment O2 demand is 0.3–1.1 g/m²/d. AHOD falls from 1.4 to 0.5 g/m²/d (observed 1.6), there are no anoxic summer days (observed 9%), and bottom DO stays above 3 mg/L. Hypolimnetic NH4 and DRP accumulation collapse with it. The chambers cover at most about a third of the observed AHOD, so the rest must come from water-column respiration below the thermocline. In the calibrated model, the sediment supplies almost all of it. Raising water-column demand (organic matter mineralisation, rdom_minerl, rpoc_hydrol, θ) is a precondition for using the chamber SOD.
  • Chamber nutrient release fits the hypolimnion when the oxygen dependence is weak. With the calibrated SOD (anoxia intact), the chamber NH4 and P release gives hypolimnetic NH4 accumulation of 20–28 mg/m²/d (observed 24). The P response depends on ksed_frp. With the calibrated 2.6, hypolimnetic DRP accumulation is 12.5 mg/m²/d, 5× the observed 2.4, and the summer bottom DRP maximum is 132 mg/m³. With ksed_frp 45 they are 3.3–3.6 and 50–83. This supports the weak oxygen dependence that Burger et al. (2007) reported.
  • Release at 10 m raises surface TP. The calibration set zone 3 (8–13 m) release to almost zero. At the chamber rate (about 2.4 mg P/m²/d averaged over the year), P released in the mostly mixed 8–13 m band reaches the surface. Surface TP rises from 40 to 64 mg/m³ before alum and from 16 to 25 under alum, even with zones 4–5 unchanged (z +5.4 and +3.7). Filling 0–8 m with the 10 m rate as well raises TP to 82 and 39. So either the 10 m chamber rate is a late-summer high that overstates the annual mean (2026 is one deployment), or the model lacks a P sink near the sediment, such as sorption, burial or alum binding, that offsets it in the real lake.
  • The TP response to the dosing pause appears only with chamber P release. Neither calibrated run reproduces it (−1.6 vs +13.6 mg/m³). The runs with chamber P release give +2 to +32, so sediment P release, rather than inflow load alone, may be the missing part of that response.
  • Chlorophyll does not respond. Surface chl-a under alum is 13.1–13.3 mg/m³ in every run, although surface TP nearly triples. The model’s phytoplankton are not limited by P at these concentrations, which needs checking separately.
  • Splitting the zone alone helps the fit. On the same release rates, the 5-zone base lowers the total error (val 92 → 75, WQ 184 → 172).

What this means for calibration: putting all the chamber values in at once makes the fit much worse (WQ 172 → 760–1520), because the model’s oxygen and P budgets were tuned around a sediment that supplies most of the oxygen demand and little shallow P. The chamber terms (ALL_CHAMBER=1) should be combined with freeing the water-column oxygen demand, the P sinks and ksed_frp (bounds widened up to about 100). Expect the calibration to trade surface TP against the 10 m release.

Calibration setup and smoke tests

The calibration is set up in calibration/all_stage/run_gns_5z.sh. bash … dry writes the ranges and starting values without running the model; bash … run launches it, which has not been done.

  • Base and objective: 5 zones, plus the chamber terms with weight 0.3.
  • Zones 1–3 (the chamber depths): fsed limits are multiples of the chamber-implied value (ALL_FSED_ANCHOR), computed with ksed_frp 45. SOD ×0.5–3; NH4 and FRP ×0.33–3.
  • Zones 4–5: keep the wide absolute ranges.
  • ksed_frp: 5–200, starting at 45.
  • Start point: the out_gns_sodp best member, with zones 1–3 set to the chamber values (prev_gnssodp_5z_k45.rds).
  • Water-column oxygen demand (nm_dom, rpoc_hydrol, theta_minerl) and the P sinks: stay free.

The smoke runs (calibration/hyps_gns/19_smoke_5z_pars.R, 20_smoke_compare.R) set zones 1–3 SOD at the start value and at both ends of its range, with and without more water-column oxygen demand (rdom_minerl at its upper bound ×4, rpoc_hydrol ×5).

  • Stratification does not respond. Share of stratified days, longest stratified spell, thermocline depth and surface temperature stay within ±0.6 tolerances in every run, and the surface − bottom temperature difference is the same in all runs. The sediment ranges act on oxygen and nutrients only.
  • Anoxia cannot be reached within the range as set. At the start value and the low bound there are no anoxic summer days, minimum bottom DO is 5–6 mg/L, and AHOD is 0.4–0.6 g/m²/d (observed 1.6). At the high bound (×3), bottom DO falls to 0.3–1 mg/L in the strongest stratified periods (2015, 2016, 2019) but does not reach the anoxia threshold, and AHOD is about 1.0. The calibrated sodp values sit at about 6.6× the chamber value in zones 1–2.
  • More water-column demand adds little. Raising rdom_minerl and rpoc_hydrol changes AHOD by only 0.03 g/m²/d, so oxygen demand in the hypolimnion seems limited by the supply of organic matter rather than its decay rate. That points to settling and hydrolysis of particulate matter (w_pom) or a sediment organic-matter flux, rather than the mineralisation rates.
  • TP is too high from the start (57–60 mg/m³ before alum, observed 41), because the 10 m release adds P (see Forward runs).

Caveats

  • Data status. The 2026 chamber data are unpublished (Campbell, pers. comm., 2026). Keep the page to summaries until they are published, and check the published values when they appear.
  • Chamber DO. Each nutrient flux is a regression over the whole deployment, while the DO in the chamber was falling. Using the lake DO as the chamber condition overstates the oxygen. The raw chamber time series (concentration and DO over time) would allow the oxygen dependence (ksed) to be fitted directly.
  • One deployment. The 2026 data cover one late-summer deployment. Seasonal and year-to-year variation (a factor of about 5 between periods in Burger et al.) is not sampled. The 0.3 log tolerance is a judgement and could be widened.
  • No data for 0–3.5 m. Zone 5 has no measurements, and zone 4 (3.5–8 m) has only Burger’s 2003–04 data.

## Sediment zones on the bathymetry {#sec-zones}

Its deepest zone covers 13–25 m (29 km²) and so mixes water at 15 m, which stays oxygenated, with the anoxic bottom.

The 2026 sites lie on a west–east line from the western shelf to just west of Mokoia Island, about 3.4 km end to end. On the bathymetry their depths are 9.3, 15.6 and 22.2 m. The 20 m site is therefore a little deeper than its name, but it stays in the deepest zone in both layouts.

## How a chamber flux relates to AED's `fsed` {#sec-aed}

The chamber method (as in Burger et al. 2007) measures a **net flux under the chamber conditions**: concentration change × chamber volume / sediment area. AED's Constant2d model applies a **maximum rate** `fsed`, scaled by temperature and by the oxygen in the water above the sediment (mmol/m²/d, O2 in mmol/m³):

| Flux | AED rate |
|---|---|
| SOD | `fsed_oxy × O2/(ksed_oxy + O2) × θ_oxy^(T−20)` |
| NH4 | `fsed_amm × ksed_amm/(ksed_amm + O2) × θ_amm^(T−20)` |
| FRP | `fsed_frp × ksed_frp/(ksed_frp + O2) × θ_frp^(T−20)` |

So a chamber flux equals the model's *actual* flux at the chamber's DO and temperature. It equals `fsed` only in anoxic water at 20 °C. To get `fsed` from a chamber flux, divide by the oxygen and temperature factor, which depends on the half-saturation constant `ksed`. `ksed` is itself a calibration parameter.

```{r}
#| label: fig-ksed
#| fig-cap: "AED maximum rate implied by the 2026 chamber medians, against the half-saturation constant, at the chamber DO and temperature. Lines are zones 1–3 (20, 15, 10 m). Dashed: the out_gns_sodp best value (ksed_frp 2.6, ksed_amm 29). The implied fsed_frp ranges over two orders of magnitude depending on ksed_frp."
#| fig-width: 9
#| fig-height: 3.8
cm <- summ |> filter(source == "chamber_2026", variable %in% c("NH4", "PO4"))
ks <- 10^seq(0, 2.7, length.out = 60)
K <- tidyr::crossing(cm, ksed = ks) |> rowwise() |>
  mutate(k = list(modifyList(SED_K_SODP, if (variable == "NH4") list(ksed_amm = ksed) else list(ksed_frp = ksed))),
         fsed_mg = median / aed_sed_factor(variable, do_mg_l, temp_c, k)) |> ungroup()
ggplot(K, aes(ksed, fsed_mg, colour = factor(depth_m))) + geom_line(linewidth = 0.8) +
  geom_vline(data = data.frame(variable = c("NH4", "PO4"), v = c(SED_K_SODP$ksed_amm, SED_K_SODP$ksed_frp)), aes(xintercept = v), linetype = 2, colour = "grey40") +
  scale_x_log10() + scale_y_log10() + facet_wrap(~ variable, scales = "free_y", labeller = as_labeller(c(NH4 = "fsed_amm (mg N/m²/d)", PO4 = "fsed_frp (mg P/m²/d)"))) +
  scale_colour_manual(values = c(`10` = "#6da7ec", `15` = "#2a78d6", `20` = "#104281"), name = "depth (m)") +
  labs(x = "ksed (mmol O2/m³)", y = "implied fsed")
```

- **Oxygen sensitivity.** The out_gns_sodp calibration chose a very low `ksed_frp` (2.6), so P release is about 80× faster without oxygen than at 7 mg/L. Burger et al. found SRP release "largely independent of DO". In the 2026 data, dark and light chambers release similar PO4 even though DO fell during the deployment. Both point to a weaker oxygen dependence. With `ksed_frp` near 45 (the default), the chamber medians imply `fsed_frp` of 15–30 mg P/m²/d rather than 200–400.
- **Earlier starting values.** These were taken directly from the measured fluxes as `fsed` (`meas_param`, `fsed_data_start()`), with an oxygen correction for SOD only. If those measurements were made in oxygenated water, the NH4 and P starting values were too low by this factor.

## Using the data in calibration {#sec-calibration}

| Piece | Where |
|---|---|
| Chamber data | target `sediment_chamber` (`R/read_sediment_chamber.R`) |
| All flux observations with overlying DO and temperature | target `sed_flux_obs_all` (2026 + Burger), summary `sed_flux_obs_summary` |
| Zone layout and GLM rows | targets `sed_zones`, `sed_zone_glm_param` (`R/sed_zones.R`) |
| Implied `fsed` per zone (start values) | target `sed_fsed_anchor_2026` (out_gns_sodp `ksed`/θ; zones 4–5 take zone 3) |
| 5-zone calibration base | `calibration/hyps_gns/15_make_base_5zone.R` → `base_long_gns_sed_5z.rds`, `sed_temp/override_est_5z.csv` |
| Zone count | `SED_NZONE=5` (`calibration/00_config.R`; read by `sa_params.R`, `sa_common.R`, `lib_all.R`) |
| Chamber objective terms | `ALL_CHAMBER=1` in `lib_all.R`: `c_<SOD/NH4/PO4>_z<k>` |

**The chamber terms.** They need only the parameters, not the model output. For each chamber, the member's AED flux at that chamber's DO and temperature is compared with the measured flux in log space. There is one term per variable and zone: the mean log ratio divided by a tolerance of √(0.3² + sd²/n), where 0.3 (about 35%) covers how well a few chambers represent a whole zone. Each term scores the actual release, so `ksed`, θ and `fsed` stay free together and are tied to the data at the oxygen and temperature it was measured at. `ALL_CHAMBER_SRC=chamber_2026,burger_2007` adds the 2003–04 survey. Its fluxes are pre-alum and several times higher, so it suits a run that is scored on the pre-alum period.

A 5-zone recalibration run (the out_gns_sodp settings plus the chamber target):

```bash
SED_NZONE=5 ALL_CHAMBER=1 ALL_FSED=1 ALL_V2=1 ALL_FSED_START=data \
  PT_BASE=calibration/hyps_gns/base_long_gns_sed_5z.rds \
  ALL_TAB_OVERRIDE=calibration/hyps_gns/sed_temp/override_est_5z.csv \
  ALL_MIXHYP_LIM=1.5,3 ALL_FSEDFRP_LIM=1e-4,20 ALL_KSEDFRP_LIM=1,500 \
  ALL_OUT=calibration/all_stage/out_gns_5z Rscript calibration/all_stage/coord_all.R
```

- The fsed_frp upper bound is raised to 20 here because the zone 1–3 starting values (7–13 mmol/m²/d at the sodp `ksed_frp`) sit above the earlier limit of 5.
- `ALL_FSEDOXY_ZLIM` refers to zones by number, so limits written for 4 zones do not carry over.


What the check run shows:

- **Splitting the zone changes the model even with the same release rates.** In AED each zone uses the average water conditions over its depth band. ## Forward runs with chamber-based release {#sec-forward}

These runs show how the model responds to sediment release set from the chamber data. Each starts from the out_gns_sodp best member; only the sediment parameters change, and nothing is recalibrated. They are full runs from 2002 to 2022 on the 5-zone base, with one 4-zone reference run.

- **Chamber values.** The `fsed` values come from target `sed_fsed_anchor_2026`. They are set so that each zone's release at the chamber DO and temperature equals the chamber median.
- **Shallow zones.** Zones 4–5 (0–8 m) have no 2026 data. They take the 10 m values unless the run says "zones 1–3".
- **Scripts.** `calibration/hyps_gns/16_forward_5z_pars.R` builds the parameter sets, `run_forward_5z.sh` runs them, and `18_forward_compare.R` compares them.

```{r}
#| label: tbl-fwd
#| tbl-cap: "Forward runs: model value and z score (model − observed, in tolerance units) for the oxygen and nutrient targets, and total squared error (cal and val without the chamber terms; WQ = weighted target sum)."
fz <- here::here("calibration", "hyps_gns", "fwd_zone")
if (file.exists(file.path(fz, "fwd_scores.csv"))) {
  w <- read.csv(file.path(fz, "fwd_scores.csv"), check.names = FALSE)
  tt <- read.csv(file.path(fz, "fwd_totals.csv"))
  keep <- c("ahod", "anox_frac_summer", "do_bot_min", "hyp_flux_nh4", "hyp_flux_drp", "bot_drp_max", "tp_int_prealum", "tp_int_alum", "tp_pause_response", "chl_int_alum")
  w <- w[match(keep, w$metric), ]
  tot <- c("Total squared error: cal / val / WQ", "", sprintf("%.0f / %.0f / %.0f", tt$cal, tt$val, tt$wq_weighted))
  out <- rbind(as.matrix(w[, -1]), tot); out[, "obs"] <- c(signif(as.numeric(w$obs), 3), "")
  colnames(out)[1:2] <- c("Target", "Observed")
  knitr::kable(out, row.names = FALSE)
}
```

![Modelled March mean sediment release by zone (2008–2022) for four of the runs, against the 2026 chamber median and interquartile range (Campbell, pers. comm., 2026; unpublished).](figures/fwd_zoneflux.png)

![Annual means (July–June) for the same four runs against the monitoring record.](figures/fwd_annual.png)

What the runs show:

- **The chamber SOD removes the anoxia.** With the chamber values, sediment O2 demand is 0.3–1.1 g/m²/d. AHOD falls from 1.4 to 0.5 g/m²/d (observed 1.6), there are no anoxic summer days (observed 9%), and bottom DO stays above 3 mg/L. Hypolimnetic NH4 and DRP accumulation collapse with it. The chambers cover at most about a third of the observed AHOD, so the rest must come from water-column respiration below the thermocline. In the calibrated model, the sediment supplies almost all of it. Raising water-column demand (organic matter mineralisation, `rdom_minerl`, `rpoc_hydrol`, θ) is a precondition for using the chamber SOD.
- **Chamber nutrient release fits the hypolimnion when the oxygen dependence is weak.** With the calibrated SOD (anoxia intact), the chamber NH4 and P release gives hypolimnetic NH4 accumulation of 20–28 mg/m²/d (observed 24). The P response depends on `ksed_frp`. With the calibrated 2.6, hypolimnetic DRP accumulation is 12.5 mg/m²/d, 5× the observed 2.4, and the summer bottom DRP maximum is 132 mg/m³. With `ksed_frp` 45 they are 3.3–3.6 and 50–83. This supports the weak oxygen dependence that Burger et al. (2007) reported.
- **Release at 10 m raises surface TP.** The calibration set zone 3 (8–13 m) release to almost zero. At the chamber rate (about 2.4 mg P/m²/d averaged over the year), P released in the mostly mixed 8–13 m band reaches the surface. Surface TP rises from 40 to 64 mg/m³ before alum and from 16 to 25 under alum, even with zones 4–5 unchanged (z +5.4 and +3.7). Filling 0–8 m with the 10 m rate as well raises TP to 82 and 39. So either the 10 m chamber rate is a late-summer high that overstates the annual mean (2026 is one deployment), or the model lacks a P sink near the sediment, such as sorption, burial or alum binding, that offsets it in the real lake.
- **The TP response to the dosing pause appears only with chamber P release.** Neither calibrated run reproduces it (−1.6 vs +13.6 mg/m³). The runs with chamber P release give +2 to +32, so sediment P release, rather than inflow load alone, may be the missing part of that response.
- **Chlorophyll does not respond.** Surface chl-a under alum is 13.1–13.3 mg/m³ in every run, although surface TP nearly triples. The model's phytoplankton are not limited by P at these concentrations, which needs checking separately.
- **Splitting the zone alone helps the fit.** On the same release rates, the 5-zone base lowers the total error (val 92 → 75, WQ 184 → 172).

What this means for calibration: putting all the chamber values in at once makes the fit much worse (WQ 172 → 760–1520), because the model's oxygen and P budgets were tuned around a sediment that supplies most of the oxygen demand and little shallow P. The chamber terms (`ALL_CHAMBER=1`) should be combined with freeing the water-column oxygen demand, the P sinks and `ksed_frp` (bounds widened up to about 100). Expect the calibration to trade surface TP against the 10 m release.

## Calibration setup and smoke tests {#sec-cal-setup}

The calibration is set up in `calibration/all_stage/run_gns_5z.sh`. `bash … dry` writes the ranges and starting values without running the model; `bash … run` launches it, which has not been done.

- **Base and objective:** 5 zones, plus the chamber terms with weight 0.3.
- **Zones 1–3 (the chamber depths):** `fsed` limits are multiples of the chamber-implied value (`ALL_FSED_ANCHOR`), computed with `ksed_frp` 45. SOD ×0.5–3; NH4 and FRP ×0.33–3.
- **Zones 4–5:** keep the wide absolute ranges.
- **`ksed_frp`:** 5–200, starting at 45.
- **Start point:** the out_gns_sodp best member, with zones 1–3 set to the chamber values (`prev_gnssodp_5z_k45.rds`).
- **Water-column oxygen demand (`nm_dom`, `rpoc_hydrol`, `theta_minerl`) and the P sinks:** stay free.

The smoke runs (`calibration/hyps_gns/19_smoke_5z_pars.R`, `20_smoke_compare.R`) set zones 1–3 SOD at the start value and at both ends of its range, with and without more water-column oxygen demand (`rdom_minerl` at its upper bound ×4, `rpoc_hydrol` ×5).

![Target z scores for the smoke runs; grey band: within one tolerance.](figures/smoke_z.png)

![Bottom DO (19–23 m) and surface − bottom temperature difference, 2014–2019.](figures/smoke_bottom_do.png)

- **Stratification does not respond.** Share of stratified days, longest stratified spell, thermocline depth and surface temperature stay within ±0.6 tolerances in every run, and the surface − bottom temperature difference is the same in all runs. The sediment ranges act on oxygen and nutrients only.
- **Anoxia cannot be reached within the range as set.** At the start value and the low bound there are no anoxic summer days, minimum bottom DO is 5–6 mg/L, and AHOD is 0.4–0.6 g/m²/d (observed 1.6). At the high bound (×3), bottom DO falls to 0.3–1 mg/L in the strongest stratified periods (2015, 2016, 2019) but does not reach the anoxia threshold, and AHOD is about 1.0. The calibrated sodp values sit at about 6.6× the chamber value in zones 1–2.
- **More water-column demand adds little.** Raising `rdom_minerl` and `rpoc_hydrol` changes AHOD by only 0.03 g/m²/d, so oxygen demand in the hypolimnion seems limited by the supply of organic matter rather than its decay rate. That points to settling and hydrolysis of particulate matter (`w_pom`) or a sediment organic-matter flux, rather than the mineralisation rates.
- **TP is too high from the start** (57–60 mg/m³ before alum, observed 41), because the 10 m release adds P (see [Forward runs](#sec-forward)).

## Caveats

- **Data status.** The 2026 chamber data are unpublished (Campbell, pers. comm., 2026). Keep the page to summaries until they are published, and check the published values when they appear.
- **Chamber DO.** Each nutrient flux is a regression over the whole deployment, while the DO in the chamber was falling. Using the lake DO as the chamber condition overstates the oxygen. The raw chamber time series (concentration and DO over time) would allow the oxygen dependence (`ksed`) to be fitted directly.
- **One deployment.** The 2026 data cover one late-summer deployment. Seasonal and year-to-year variation (a factor of about 5 between periods in Burger et al.) is not sampled. The 0.3 log tolerance is a judgement and could be widened.
- **No data for 0–3.5 m.** Zone 5 has no measurements, and zone 4 (3.5–8 m) has only Burger's 2003–04 data.
