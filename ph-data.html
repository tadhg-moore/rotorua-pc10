Lake pH time series

Buoy pH record (2014 to 2026): summaries, seasonality, depth structure and download

Author

Tadhg Moore

Published

November 11, 2026

1 The data

pH is measured by the Lake Rotorua monitoring buoy and enters the model workflow as CHM_ph (see R/format_buoy_for_aeme.R). It is used to check the pH response (fpH) of the alum module, and is an input to the alum calibration. The record is two buoy deployments with different sensor set-ups:

  • Old buoy (target old_buoy_wq): one value per day at 1 m, 25 Feb 2014 to 7 Jan 2018 (only 4 days in 2018).
  • New buoy (target aeme_buoy_data): a daily profile at 12:00 UTC from 21 Feb 2022 to 17 Sep 2026, with values every 0.5 m between 1 and 21 m (41 depths).
  • There is no pH record between January 2018 and February 2022.

new buoy 59,313 1508 2022-02-21 2026-10-30 1–21 (41) 6.84 6.1–7.78 5.32 12.71 1

Quality control. Only one value is outside the plausible range of 4 to 11 (12.7 at 14.5 m on 2024-05-28), and it is flagged qc_in_range = FALSE in the download rather than removed. All figures and tables on this page exclude it. The old buoy record has a minimum of 5.2 and the new buoy of 5.3 (at 20 m in February 2025); these are plausible for the anoxic hypolimnion but have not been checked against grab samples. The two sensors have not been cross-calibrated.

5 Relationship with other profiler variables

The profiler measures temperature, fluorescence chlorophyll, dissolved oxygen, ORP, turbidity and specific conductance alongside pH (target buoy_profile_daily: daily means in 0.5 m bins, 2022 to 2026, so the new buoy only). The old buoy has no chlorophyll record. Chlorophyll here is in-situ fluorescence (FlChlr), which is not calibrated against lab chlorophyll-a, so it is a relative index of phytoplankton.

Spearman rank correlation of daily mean pH (0.5 m bins) with the other profiler variables: all depths, by depth zone, and for anomalies from each depth’s long-term mean.
Variable All 0-5 m 5-10 m 10-17 m 17-22 m Anomaly from depth mean
Temperature -0.26 0.02 -0.21 -0.44 -0.50 -0.28
Chlorophyll (fluorescence) 0.38 0.27 0.37 0.46 0.52 0.41
DO (mg/L) 0.55 0.29 0.46 0.64 0.68 0.54
DO saturation (%) 0.63 0.51 0.55 0.66 0.70 0.60
ORP 0.27 0.05 0.26 0.40 0.41 0.29
Turbidity 0.00 -0.07 -0.17 0.00 0.12 -0.04
Specific conductance 0.00 -0.06 -0.01 0.04 0.05 0.01
Depth -0.28 0.01 -0.11 -0.10 0.03 NA
  • Oxygen is the strongest correlate (Spearman 0.63 with saturation and 0.55 with concentration over all depths; 0.51 for saturation in the top 5 m and 0.7 at 17 to 22 m). Both pH and oxygen respond to the balance of photosynthesis and respiration, so this is expected and not independent evidence.
  • Chlorophyll is positively correlated with pH (0.38 overall, from 0.27 in the top 5 m to 0.52 at 17 to 22 m). It stays at 0.41 after removing each depth’s mean, so days with more chlorophyll have higher pH at a given depth.
  • Temperature is negatively correlated overall (-0.26), but the relationship changes with depth: 0.02 in the top 5 m and -0.5 at 17 to 22 m, where colder water has the higher pH.
  • Turbidity and specific conductance have correlations of 0 and 0 with pH over all depths, and ORP (0.27) largely follows oxygen.
  • These are rank correlations of autocorrelated daily data from one site. They show association only, and any significance test would be overstated.

6 Alkalinity, hardness and dissolved organic carbon

Discrete lake samples from Sites 2 and 5 (target alk_data, from the BOPRC file alkalinity, tot hardness & diss org Carbon.xlsx) give the buffering context for the pH record. Alkalinity and total hardness are in mg/L as CaCO₃; DOC is in g/m³ (the same as mg/L).

  • The lake is poorly buffered. Integrated-sample alkalinity has a median of 6.2 mg/L as CaCO₃ (range 3.3 to 13) over 57 monthly samples (both sites combined) between 2015-10-21 and 2018-02-20. Such low alkalinity lets pH move easily with photosynthesis, respiration and alum addition.
  • Alkalinity varies a lot from month to month, with no regular seasonal cycle visible in about two years of monthly data.
  • Hardness and DOC are measured about once a year (July or August, 2011 to 2017). DOC is low (0.9 to 2.5 g/m³) and hardness is steady (12.5 to 15 mg/L as CaCO₃).
  • No clear same-day link with pH. Matching the 46 alkalinity samples to the old buoy’s 1 to 2 m pH on the same date gives a Spearman correlation of -0.15. The sample is small, and alkalinity is only available while the old buoy was running (2015 to 2018), so it says nothing about the 2022 to 2026 pH decline.

6.1 Do pH values exist on the sampling dates?

Samples with a value for each variable, and how many have a buoy pH measurement on the same date.
Variable Samples With pH on the same date Without pH
Alkalinity 66 46 20
Hardness 13 5 8
DOC 13 5 8

Mostly, yes. 46 of the 66 alkalinity samples have a buoy pH value on the same date, all from the old buoy (the alkalinity file ends in February 2018 and the new buoy starts in February 2022). Of the 20 without pH, 6 were taken before the old buoy started on 25 Feb 2014, 4 after it stopped in January 2018, and the other 10 fall on days within the old buoy period when it has no pH record. Only 5 of the 13 hardness and DOC samples have a matching pH value. The old buoy measures at 1 m only, so each match is to surface pH, while the integrated samples cover a depth range.

## Relationship with other profiler variables

The profiler measures temperature, fluorescence chlorophyll, dissolved oxygen, ORP, turbidity and specific conductance alongside pH (target `buoy_profile_daily`: daily means in 0.5 m bins, 2022 to 2026, so the new buoy only). The old buoy has no chlorophyll record. Chlorophyll here is in-situ fluorescence (`FlChlr`), which is not calibrated against lab chlorophyll-a, so it is a relative index of phytoplankton.

```{r}
#| include: false
vars <- c(`Temperature` = "temp_c", `Chlorophyll (fluorescence)` = "chl_fl", `DO (mg/L)` = "do_mgl", `DO saturation (%)` = "do_sat",
          `ORP` = "orp_mv", `Turbidity` = "turb", `Specific conductance` = "spcond", `Depth` = "depth_m")
sp <- \(x, y) suppressWarnings(cor(x, y, method = "spearman", use = "pairwise.complete.obs"))
prof <- prof |> mutate(zone = cut(depth_m, c(0, 5, 10, 17, 22), labels = c("0-5 m", "5-10 m", "10-17 m", "17-22 m")))
# anomalies from the long-term mean at each depth (removes the average depth gradient)
anom <- prof |> group_by(depth_m) |> mutate(across(c(ph, temp_c, chl_fl, do_mgl, do_sat, orp_mv, turb, spcond), \(x) x - mean(x, na.rm = TRUE))) |> ungroup()
cr <- data.frame(Variable = names(vars),
  All = sapply(vars, \(v) sp(prof$ph, prof[[v]])),
  sapply(levels(prof$zone), \(z) sapply(vars, \(v) sp(prof$ph[prof$zone == z], prof[[v]][prof$zone == z]))),
  `Anomaly from depth mean` = sapply(vars, \(v) if (v == "depth_m") NA else sp(anom$ph, anom[[v]])), check.names = FALSE)
cr[-1] <- lapply(cr[-1], r2)
rownames(cr) <- NULL
g <- \(v, z) cr[cr$Variable == v, z]
```

```{r}
#| include: false
int <- alk |> filter(sample == "Integrated", !is.na(alkalinity_mgl_caco3))
ph_d <- ph |> filter(depth_m <= 2) |> group_by(date) |> summarise(ph = mean(ph))
jj <- inner_join(filter(alk, !is.na(alkalinity_mgl_caco3)), ph_d, by = "date")
r_pa <- cor(jj$ph, jj$alkalinity_mgl_caco3, method = "spearman")
```

- **The lake is poorly buffered.** Integrated-sample alkalinity has a median of `r r2(median(int$alkalinity_mgl_caco3))` mg/L as CaCO₃ (range `r r2(min(int$alkalinity_mgl_caco3))` to `r r2(max(int$alkalinity_mgl_caco3))`) over `r nrow(int)` monthly samples (both sites combined) between `r min(int$date)` and `r max(int$date)`. Such low alkalinity lets pH move easily with photosynthesis, respiration and alum addition.
- **Alkalinity varies a lot from month to month**, with no regular seasonal cycle visible in about two years of monthly data.
- **Hardness and DOC are measured about once a year** (July or August, 2011 to 2017). DOC is low (`r r2(min(alk$doc_gm3, na.rm = TRUE))` to `r r2(max(alk$doc_gm3, na.rm = TRUE))` g/m³) and hardness is steady (`r r2(min(alk$hardness_mgl_caco3, na.rm = TRUE))` to `r r2(max(alk$hardness_mgl_caco3, na.rm = TRUE))` mg/L as CaCO₃).
- **No clear same-day link with pH.** Matching the `r nrow(jj)` alkalinity samples to the old buoy's 1 to 2 m pH on the same date gives a Spearman correlation of `r r2(r_pa)`. The sample is small, and alkalinity is only available while the old buoy was running (2015 to 2018), so it says nothing about the 2022 to 2026 pH decline.

### Do pH values exist on the sampling dates?

```{r}
#| include: false
chk <- alk |> mutate(has_ph = date %in% ph$date)
cnt <- \(v) { x <- chk[!is.na(chk[[v]]), ]; c(n = nrow(x), same_day = sum(x$has_ph)) }
cc <- sapply(c(Alkalinity = "alkalinity_mgl_caco3", Hardness = "hardness_mgl_caco3", DOC = "doc_gm3"), cnt)
no_ph <- chk |> filter(!is.na(alkalinity_mgl_caco3), !has_ph)
```

```{r}
#| tbl-cap: "Samples with a value for each variable, and how many have a buoy pH measurement on the same date."
knitr::kable(data.frame(Variable = colnames(cc), Samples = cc["n", ], `With pH on the same date` = cc["same_day", ], `Without pH` = cc["n", ] - cc["same_day", ], check.names = FALSE), row.names = FALSE)
```

Mostly, yes. `r cc["same_day", "Alkalinity"]` of the `r cc["n", "Alkalinity"]` alkalinity samples have a buoy pH value on the same date, all from the old buoy (the alkalinity file ends in February 2018 and the new buoy starts in February 2022). Of the `r nrow(no_ph)` without pH, `r sum(no_ph$date < as.Date("2014-02-25"))` were taken before the old buoy started on 25 Feb 2014, `r sum(no_ph$date > as.Date("2018-01-07"))` after it stopped in January 2018, and the other `r sum(no_ph$date >= as.Date("2014-02-25") & no_ph$date <= as.Date("2018-01-07"))` fall on days within the old buoy period when it has no pH record. Only `r cc["same_day", "Hardness"]` of the `r cc["n", "Hardness"]` hardness and DOC samples have a matching pH value. The old buoy measures at 1 m only, so each match is to surface pH, while the integrated samples cover a depth range.
