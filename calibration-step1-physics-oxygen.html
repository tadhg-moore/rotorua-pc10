Calibration Step 1: Physics and Oxygen

Polymixis and bottom-water anoxia, hourly forcing, wind unscaled, alum off

Author

Tadhg Moore

Published

October 20, 2026

1 Purpose

This is the first step of the staged plan that follows the sensitivity analysis: calibrate the physics and the oxygen dynamics together, using the process signatures of the CSPS framework as well as state errors, with the alum module off, because alum does not change physics or oxygen. The target behaviour is a polymictic lake that stratifies for days at a time, mixes again, and develops short bottom-water anoxia.

2 Set-up

  • Forcing: the hourly bias-corrected ERA5 series. The sensitivity analysis showed that daily forcing has less mixing energy, so mixing parameters fitted on it would not transfer.
  • Wind is not scaled. The wind is measured on the lake, so the wind scaling factor is fixed at 1 (earlier calibrations settled on 1.64, which was compensating for other errors). Heat and mixing errors are absorbed by the heat-flux coefficients and mixing coefficients instead.
  • Alum off: the dosed inflow concentration is set to zero. Phytoplankton and the other biogeochemical parameters are held at the earlier calibrated values (the phytoplankton parameters are calibrated in the next step).
  • Period: 2012-07 to 2022-06, the span with both hourly forcing and the inflow series, plus a 540-day spin-up. Calibration window 2012-07 to 2018-12; the years 2019-01 to 2022-06 are held out and are only used to check the result.
  • Free parameters (27): the radiation, air-temperature and humidity scalings; the heat-flux coefficients ce, ch, cd; the light extinction Kw; five mixing coefficients (coef_mix_shear is inert and fixed); the sediment oxygen demand in four zones with ksed_oxy and theta_sed_oxy; and eight organic-matter parameters (mineralisation and hydrolysis rates, their temperature coefficients and half-saturations, f_an, particle settling w_pom).
  • What is fitted (28 residuals, each in units of the lake’s tolerance or a fixed scale, so every metric counts equally):
    • 16 process signatures: stratified fraction (all year, summer, winter), temperature difference statistics, spells per year, spell length, bottom temperature, anoxic fraction (below 2 and 1 mg/L), bottom oxygen statistics, the share of years with anoxia, and the oxygen decline rate in stratified spells.
    • Bias and RMSE of temperature and oxygen in the surface, middle and bottom layers.
  • Method: an ensemble smoother with multiple data assimilation (ES-MDA), 80 members, 6 assimilation steps, run in parallel (560 model runs). It gives an ensemble rather than one parameter set.

  • Stratification: the stratified fraction and the temperature difference between surface and bottom now sit close to the observed values (stratified days 0.8 tolerances over in the calibration years and 0.3 in the held-out years), where the baseline was up to two tolerances too stratified. Surface oxygen is about 0.65 mg/L too low in both windows, which this step did not target: surface oxygen is set by air-water exchange and phytoplankton production, and will be revisited with the phytoplankton step.

5 Second pass with wider bounds

To check whether those limits were holding the fit back, I ran a second pass of 4 assimilation steps starting from half of the first posterior, with coef_mix_hyp up to 5, cd and ch up to 0.003, ce down to 0.0004, coef_mix_KH down to 0.02, and wider organic-matter ranges.

Best member of each pass.
Pass Calibration phi (best) Held-out phi (best)
1: original bounds 0.64 0.5
2: widened bounds 0.63 0.6
  • No improvement in the fit (0.63 against 0.64 on the calibration years) and a worse score on the held-out years (0.60 against 0.50), so widening the bounds is not worthwhile and pass 1 is kept as the step 1 result. The pinned parameters are not what limits the stratification.
  • The oxygen and organic-matter parameters are not unique. The second pass reached the same fit with very different values: for example rdom_minerl went from 0.045 to 0.006, kpom_hydrol from 6 to 95, w_pom from -0.40 to -0.02, the zone-4 oxygen demand from -134 to -38 and ksed_oxy from 97 to 35. The data constrain a combination of these (the total oxygen demand reaching the bottom water), not each one. The physics parameters were more stable (coef_wind_stir 0.56 in both passes, cd about 0.002, coef_mix_hyp 1.7 to 2). The next steps should treat these as an ensemble, not a single set.

6 Limitations

  • The residual stratification excess (summer fraction, spell frequency) is not removed by any of the bounded parameters, so it is probably structural (for example the missing deep pit). This was not tested here.
  • Surface oxygen is biased low; this is an air-water exchange and production issue.
  • Phytoplankton, nitrogen and phosphorus parameters were held at their earlier values, so the oxygen parameters may be compensating for phytoplankton oxygen production and respiration. They should be re-checked after the phytoplankton step.
  • Three and a half years are held out, which is short for the signatures that depend on rare events (anoxic years).

7 Next

  1. The parameter set (calibration/checkpoints/step1_final_params.csv) becomes the physics and oxygen baseline for the next step.
  2. Calibrate the sediment and phytoplankton parameters on the 1990 to 2006 nutrient and chlorophyll record with the alum module off. This needs the hourly forcing back to 1987.
  3. Calibrate the alum parameters on the dosed years with everything else fixed.
