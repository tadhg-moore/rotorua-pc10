Phytoplankton groups and zooplankton: investigation

How zooplankton grazing, group traits and nutrient stoichiometry shape succession, chlorophyll-a and nutrients in the GLM-AED model

Author

Tadhg Moore

Published

November 9, 2026

1 Summary

The calibrated Lake Rotorua model has no zooplankton (aed_zooplankton is commented out of the model list), so nothing grazes the phytoplankton and every phytoplankton parameter absorbed that missing loss. The result is a lake of diatoms and cyanobacteria only, with the greens gone. This investigation switches the zooplankton on and asks which settings give three coexisting groups with a plausible seasonal order (diatoms in the cool seasons, greens through the year, cyanobacteria in summer), total chlorophyll-a near the observations, and nutrients that stay close to the calibrated model.

Method. A 2-year window (2018-07 to 2020-06, plus the 540 day spin-up) of the stage j best member is run in about 20 s to 90 s per configuration. A one-at-a-time screen of 41 parameters (75 runs) was followed by an adaptive search of 11 generations of 90 runs (990 runs) over all 41 parameters at once. Each run is scored on chlorophyll-a, five nutrients, the seasonal group shares, zooplankton biomass and whether cyanobacteria favour calm days. No data exist for the individual groups or for zooplankton, so the group split and zooplankton biomass are set only to plausible ranges.

What was found

  • Zooplankton in this build do not conserve phosphorus or nitrogen. Switching them on with their default phosphorus content raised surface TP from 0.02 to 0.11 g/m³ and FRP from 0.003 to 0.08 g/m³. In aed_zooplankton.F90 the phosphorus and nitrogen eaten from phytoplankton are divided by 31 and 14 (a mg-to-mmol conversion), while the phytoplankton lose the full amount, so the zooplankton phosphorus content (IPC_zoo) and nitrogen content (INC_zoo) act as the closure terms of the budget. Every good configuration settles at an IPC_zoo between 0.003 and 0.008 mol P per mol C.
  • Grazing is what opens the niche for three groups. With the zooplankton on, the greens and diatoms are held back and the cyanobacteria, which the zooplankton hardly eat, get the warm season.
  • The best configuration scores 2.7 against 3.6 for the hand-tuned case and 18.8 for the zooplankton-off model (lower is better). It gives mean chlorophyll-a of 9.0 mg/m³ (8.3 observed in the window), diatoms 70% of biomass in September to November and cyanobacteria 44% in January to March.
  • The result holds on a six-year window (2016 to 2022). The best configuration scores 2.2 there, against 4.0 for the hand-tuned case and 18.1 for the zooplankton-off model, with the same seasonal order (diatoms 68% in September to November, cyanobacteria 43% in January to March) and chlorophyll-a of 9.2 mg/m³ against 8.2 observed. The search never saw most of these years, but it was scored on the 2-year window inside this one.
  • Calm-water blooms are not reproduced. In every configuration the cyanobacteria are no more abundant on stratified than on mixed summer days, and constant positive buoyancy or the motile option changes little, because the top 5 m of this polymictic lake is well mixed.
  • Total nitrogen is too low in all of the good configurations (about 0.15 to 0.25 g/m³ against 0.31 observed).

2 Method

2.1 Parameter space

Forty-one parameters (Table 1) cover the traits of the three phytoplankton groups, the grazing and mortality of three zooplankton groups, how much of each prey they take, and three switches (nitrogen fixation, motile cyanobacteria, copepods that eat other zooplankton). The temperature function is built from T_opt with fixed offsets for T_std and T_max, and combinations that the AED temperature solver cannot solve (which would stop GLM) are rejected before the run.

Table 2 describes what each parameter does in AED and why it was included. Parameters that exist once per group are shown as one row with the prefix cy_ (cyanobacteria), gr_ (greens) or di_ (diatoms); the zooplankton rates with zr_/zm_ come in three versions (rot, cla, cop for rotifers, cladocerans and copepods).

2.2 Score

The score adds four parts (all are about 1 when the model is within a stated tolerance):

  1. Chlorophyll-a: log bias of the mean, the 90th percentile and the monthly pattern against the surface observations in the window.
  2. Nutrients: mean absolute scaled log bias of TP, FRP, total nitrogen, nitrate and ammonium.
  3. Succession: penalties when the diatom share in September to November and May to July averages below 0.5, the cyanobacteria share in January to March is below 0.5, the green share is below 0.35 over the year, and a large penalty for any group below 5% of biomass.
  4. Zooplankton: penalty outside 3 to 30 mmol C/m³ in total, and for any group below 0.05.

The weighting is a judgement, not a result, so the ranking among near-equal scores should not be over-read.

3 One-at-a-time effects

Figure 1 moves each parameter to 10% and 90% of its range around the h1 base case.

  • The zooplankton refuge Cmin_grz_zoo is the most powerful single control. At the low end the grazing removes nearly all the algae (mean chlorophyll about 1 mg/m³); at the high end chlorophyll-a rises by about 7 mg/m³.
  • Copepod and rotifer grazing rates at the high end remove the cyanobacteria (even at a preference of 0.02) and so wreck the succession.
  • IPC_zoo at the high end raises TP about seven-fold, and at the low end TP falls, so it must be tuned together with grazing.
  • Diatom respiration at the high end wipes out the diatoms.
  • Green growth, light and nutrient half-saturation decide whether the greens survive, but none of them alone brings the cyanobacteria to the target summer share.

4 Adaptive search

Each generation samples 90 configurations around the average of the best 20% of the last, with a floor on the spread.

The best score fell from 5.5 to 2.7 over 11 generations and levelled off at about 2.7 to 3.0, so more generations with this score would add little. Figure 3 shows how tightly the best 5% of runs pin each parameter.

  • Well constrained: the zooplankton phosphorus content, the refuge, the nitrogen content, the cladoceran grazing rate and the cyanobacteria respiration.
  • Poorly constrained: whether copepods eat other zooplankton, copepod mortality, the cyanobacteria preference by zooplankton, and cyanobacteria buoyancy. Different values work about equally well, so these are not identifiable from total chlorophyll-a and nutrients.

5 Best configurations

Observed means over the same pairs are chlorophyll-a 8.3 mg/m³, TP 0.018, FRP 0.002, TN 0.31, nitrate 0.034 and ammonium 0.008 g/m³.

In the best configuration the spring diatom bloom (about 70 mmol C/m³ in October to November) is probably too sharp, and the summer is nearly flat, with biomass held at about 43 mmol C/m³ from December to April. The observed chlorophyll-a has more year to year variation than this.

5.1 Zooplankton in the best configurations

  • Zooplankton almost disappear in winter. In every configuration biomass is close to zero from about June to September and comes back in October to December, peaking at 10 to 30 mmol C/m³ in summer. Real zooplankton in a lake of this temperature persist through winter at low levels, so the winter collapse is probably a model artefact (temperature limits on growth and the minimum biomass floor), and it lets the diatoms bloom unchecked each spring.
  • Rotifers or cladocerans dominate; copepods are marginal in all but one configuration (g010_042, where copepods reach 20 mmol C/m³ in spring). The search had no reason to favour copepods because no data say which group should dominate.
  • Grazers are 20 to 60% of phytoplankton biomass in summer and well under 5% in winter (Figure 9), which is strong top-down pressure. There are no zooplankton data to say whether that is realistic for Rotorua.

5.2 What was and was not varied

The search varied, for each of the three zooplankton groups, the maximum grazing rate and the mortality rate; it varied the half-saturation (Kgrz_zoo), the refuge (Cmin_grz_zoo) and the assimilation efficiency in common for all three; it varied how strongly all three prefer greens, diatoms and cyanobacteria (one preference each, shared by the three groups); and whether copepods eat the other zooplankton. Not varied: which groups are present (all three are always on, so there is no run with, for example, rotifers only or cladocerans only), a separate food preference for each group, the zooplankton temperature limits (Tstd_zoo, Topt_zoo, Tmax_zoo), respiration, the split of excretion and faeces, the oxygen threshold, and predation on the zooplankton. The model has no fish or other predator, so predation pressure on the zooplankton enters only through the linear mortality rate, and that is the only lever on top-down control of the grazers. The winter collapse and the unconstrained copepod diet suggest that the community composition and the temperature limits are the next things to test.

7 Fit to the observed group data, 2003 to 2011

The BOPRC phytoplankton counts (50 samples at sites 2 and 5, October 2003 to January 2011, mostly sampled in January, April, July and October) give the first observed group shares. They were used as follows.

  • Window: one run from 2003-07 to 2011-06 per configuration. Calibration scores use 2003-07 to 2007-06 (17 sample dates) and hold-out scores use 2007-07 to 2011-06 (14 sample dates); both are recorded for every run, and only the calibration score steered the search.
  • Observed curves: a first-harmonic seasonal curve per group, refit on the calibration samples only (so the hold-out years do not leak into the target), with a bootstrap spread as the tolerance (at least 0.07 in share). The model is scored on how far its monthly mean shares fall from these curves, in units of that tolerance (z3 for the three-group scheme, z4 for the four-group scheme with the cyanobacteria split).
  • Four model structures, all with zooplankton on and the 15 to 21 parameters of the earlier best configuration free: s3 (cyanobacteria, greens, diatoms); s4c (cyanobacteria split into a nitrogen-fixing and a non-fixing group); s4g (an added group of other greens, the AED cryptophyte column with a lower light requirement); s5 (both).
  • Budget: 240 runs (about 60 per structure). The first three rounds were scored against a diatom curve that was wrongly set to zero (a missing-value bug in my script). The model runs themselves were fine, so all 144 saved runs were re-scored offline and the search was resumed with two more rounds under the corrected score.
  • Selection: structures are compared on the terms they share (z3, chlorophyll-a, nutrients and zooplankton, summed as the “common score”), because the search score adds a four-group term for the split structures.

What the comparison shows

  • The seasonal pattern can be matched. In all four structures the model’s monthly shares sit mostly inside the observed 95% band: diatoms dominate July to October, cyanobacteria peak in summer (January to March, about 20 to 50% in the model depending on the structure, against about 37% observed), and greens and other taxa are highest in summer to autumn. The earlier assumption that cyanobacteria should reach 50% in summer was too high, since the samples show them at about 25 to 35% of biovolume.
  • Hold-out years are about as well fitted as the calibration years for the three-group, cyano-split and other-greens structures (share error on the sample dates about 0.20 to 0.23 against 0.22 to 0.28), so the seasonal order found in 2003 to 2007 holds in 2007 to 2011. Mean chlorophyll-a is within about 25% of the observed mean in both periods (9.0 to 12.3 against 11.3 mg/m³ in the calibration years, 8.9 to 11.0 against 12.0 in the hold-out years).
  • No structure is clearly better. The common scores of the best runs are 4.1 (three groups), 4.3 (cyano split), 4.6 (other greens) and 4.5 (both), and the differences are small next to the run-to-run spread. With 60 runs per structure and 15 to 21 free parameters this is inside the noise of the search, so these results do not support the added complexity and also do not rule it out.
  • Other greens help the summer greens, but not the hold-out. The s4g run reproduces the observed summer rise in greens (peak near 0.7 in December) that the three-group and cyano-split runs miss (about 0.35), but it overestimates greens by 0.21 in the hold-out years. The five-group run has the largest hold-out errors (greens +0.28, diatoms -0.34 on the sample dates).
  • A persistent diatom excess in summer. Every structure keeps diatoms at about 25% of biomass from December to May, against about 5 to 15% observed.
  • Cyanobacteria split: the split run overestimates the summer cyanobacteria (about 50%, at the top of the observed band), and the four-group score term (z4 of 1.7 to 2.0) shows the nitrogen-fixing and non-fixing groups are not separated cleanly by the model (the observed peaks are about two months apart).

7.1 Timing: time series and depth contours

The depth contours (Figure 15 to Figure 18) show the daily profiles of each group, the total chlorophyll-a and the temperature in the best run of each structure. Triangles at the top mark the sample dates.

  • The timing repeats almost exactly every year. In the three-group run the diatoms form a sharp peak in September to October, the cyanobacteria bloom from about December to April, and the greens are spread over the warm half of the year. The model has little year-to-year variation in timing, while the observed shares scatter widely between years (Figure 14).
  • The groups are nearly uniform with depth. The temperature panel shows weak stratification in summer, so the blooms extend through the whole 21 m column, including the bottom layers where light is low. Cyanobacteria are somewhat stronger near the surface, with streaks of high values through the column that follow mixing events. There is no surface scum layer, which is consistent with the earlier result that buoyancy changes little in this lake.
  • Timing differs between structures. The five-group run (Figure 18) has a long period of greens in the hold-out years and almost no spring diatom peak in 2008 to 2010, which is the source of its large hold-out errors.
  • Which sample dates would constrain timing? The sample dates fall mostly in January, April, July and October, so they pin the shares in those months but say little about when each bloom starts or ends (for example the diatom peak between July and October).

8 Calibration with PEST++ and a nutrient-extended search

The random search tunes only the phytoplankton and zooplankton settings. Three further attempts tested whether a formal calibration, or freeing the nutrient processes, gives a better match to the nutrients as well as the succession. All three use the three-group structure (s3) with the zooplankton on, calibrate on 2003-07 to 2007-06 and hold out 2007-07 to 2011-06.

  • PEST++ run 1 (phytoplankton and zooplankton only). pestpp-ies through aemetools, 39 free parameters (growth, light and nutrient half-saturation, respiration and settling for the three groups, and seven grazing settings for each of three zooplankton groups), 40 realisations, 5 iterations, 5 agents (333 min). Targets were the surface (0 to 5 m) total chlorophyll-a, the three group chlorophyll-a series from the BOPRC counts (25 samples per group in the calibration years, log1p), and FRP, TP, nitrate, ammonium and TN. The zooplankton parameters were switched on through the AEME configuration and calibrated through the same parameter table as the rest.
  • PEST++ run 2 (narrowed priors, nutrients freed). The same set-up, started from the best nutrient-weighted member of the random search (r5_s3_11). The prior ranges of the phytoplankton and zooplankton settings were narrowed to the span of the 12 best search members (padded by 1.3; ±40% for settings the search never varied), and nine nutrient processes were freed within a factor of 4 (sediment ammonium, nitrate and FRP fluxes, nitrification, denitrification, DOM mineralisation, PON and POP hydrolysis, PO4 sorption). That gave 57 free parameters, with larger weights on TP, TN, FRP, nitrate and ammonium (357 min).
  • Nutrient-extended search. The cross-entropy search of Section 4, restarted from the 8 best earlier members with nine multipliers (0.25 to 4) on the same nutrient processes as extra search dimensions. 60 rounds of 12 runs (720 runs, 3 workers). It is scored by the same metrics as the earlier searches, so succession (z3), chlorophyll-a, nutrients and zooplankton all count.

What the comparison shows

  • The PEST++ route works, including for the zooplankton parameters. The zooplankton module is switched on through the model configuration, its parameters sit in the same table as the rest, and group-level observations can be added to the AEME observations. The runs finish and the objective falls (run 1: 20.5 to 12.4 on its own objective).
  • But the PEST++ members score worse on the search metrics than the member they started from. Run 1 gives 7.8 (calibration) and 8.0 (hold-out) against 4.1 for the starting member, run 2 gives 6.4 and 6.1. Run 1 cut total chlorophyll-a to about 6.4 mg/m³ (observed 11 to 12 mg/m³) and surface TP to 0.024 (calibration) and 0.014 (hold-out), while raising TN from 0.20 to 0.52 (0.31 observed). Run 2 improved little in its own objective after the first iteration (best phi about 15.3), and its nutrient penalty rose from 0.74 to 1.39.
  • Why: the two objectives differ. PEST++ fits the surface residuals of each variable. The search score adds the seasonal succession shares, a chlorophyll-a high-percentile and seasonality term, and a zooplankton biomass range. Optimising one does not improve the other, and only 25 group samples per group constrain the succession in the PEST++ runs.
  • The nutrient-extended search improved on all of the other approaches. Its best member (r45_s3_02) scores 3.38 on the calibration years and 3.37 on the hold-out years, against 4.06 for the earlier best, with a nutrient penalty of 0.70 against 1.17 (re-scored; 1.32 in the original search) and 0.61 on hold-out. The hold-out matches the calibration score, so there is no sign of overfitting in the final member, although the hold-out score of the best-so-far member rose to 4.0 for a while in the middle of the search.
  • Succession did not improve. z3 is 1.65 against 1.57 for the earlier best, with biases of +0.11 (cyanobacteria), -0.17 (greens) and +0.06 (diatoms) on the sample dates, so the greens are still underestimated. The nutrient gain was not paid for in the shares, but it did not help them either.
  • What the nutrient parameters did. The best member raises the sediment ammonium flux and denitrification by about 2.9 times and the nitrate flux by 1.1, and lowers the sediment FRP flux (0.30), nitrification (0.40), PON hydrolysis (0.41), POP hydrolysis (0.63) and PO4 sorption (0.56). Surface TN is 0.33 g/m³ (0.31 observed), TP 0.035, FRP 0.013 and nitrate 0.024. Several of these multipliers moved far from 1, and the nutrient parameters are not identified separately from each other, so they should be read as a combined adjustment, not as separate estimates.
  • Remaining gap. The search plateaued: the last 14 rounds improved the score by about 0.04. Simulated ammonium rose to 0.025 g/m³ (0.004 to 0.017 in the other members), and I have not compared nitrate, ammonium, TP or FRP with the observed means. The persistent summer diatom excess also remains.

9 CSPS-based scoring: nutrient and alum dynamics

The CSPS framework (calibration/csps_metrics.R) scores a model by signatures: summary statistics computed in the same way on the observations and on the model, on the days that were sampled, with the interannual spread of the observed signature as the tolerance. Until now it covered mixing, anoxia, internal loading, chlorophyll-a and trophic state. Two sets were added, switched on with extended = TRUE so the sensitivity analysis and the step 1 to 3 calibrations are unchanged.

  • Nutrient dynamics (nut_*, 23 signatures). For TP, TN, FRP, nitrate and ammonium in the surface 2 m: median, 90th percentile, coefficient of variation and the ratio of summer (December to April) to winter (June to August) medians, which catches a wrong seasonal cycle such as a winter nitrate pile-up that is not drawn down in summer. Also the TN:TP mass ratio, FRP as a fraction of TP and the dissolved inorganic N to FRP ratio.
  • Alum dynamics (alum_*, 7 signatures). The response to the alum programme: surface TP, surface chlorophyll-a and summer bottom-water FRP after dosing (from 2008) over before dosing (before June 2006), the trend in annual mean TP after dosing, the change in surface pH, and the aluminium in the water (needs the 2011 to 2015 Al record, read from the GLM output with csps_pair_al()). The observed ratios are 0.44 for TP, 0.88 for chlorophyll-a and 0.19 for bottom FRP, so the observed TP fell by 56% and the summer bottom FRP by 81%.

The tolerances and observed values are in calibration/csps_targets_ext.csv (65 signatures, built by csps_build_targets_ext.R). Several tolerances are wide because the observed series are short (FRP has only 5 usable years; nitrate and ammonium are mostly near the detection limit).

In the phytoplankton scoring (calibration/phyto_groups/csps_phyto.R), each run is now also scored on the CSPS signatures of its calibration and hold-out windows. A term is the mean of min(|z|, 3) over its signatures, where z is the model minus observed signature, in tolerance units, on the paired days of that window: csps_chl (7 chlorophyll-a signatures), csps_nut (23), csps_load (9 bottom against surface signatures), csps_sys (trophic state) and csps_alum (the alum response across 2003 to 2011). The new score2 keeps the succession, zooplankton and extinction terms of the earlier score and replaces the chlorophyll-a and nutrient terms by the CSPS terms. The earlier score is still recorded, so earlier runs stay comparable, and the nutrient-extended search can be steered by score2 by setting INV_SCORE=score2; that search has not been re-run with it.

What the CSPS scoring shows

  • The nutrient-extended member is better than the earlier best on the CSPS terms too. Chlorophyll-a signatures 1.54 to 0.99 and nutrient signatures 1.51 to 1.05 in the calibration years, with the pass fraction rising from 0.39 to 0.59. In the hold-out years the gains are smaller (chlorophyll-a 1.53 to 1.18, nutrients 1.02 to 0.99, pass fraction 0.52 to 0.50), and internal loading is worse (1.07 to 1.30), so the improvement is concentrated in the calibration years.
  • The model reproduces the alum response. The model’s post-dose over pre-dose ratios of TP and chlorophyll-a are within 0.15 tolerances of the observed ratios in both members, and the bottom FRP ratio within 0.8 tolerances. This is expected because the alum dosing is in the forcing and the alum parameters were calibrated earlier; it is a check that the phytoplankton and nutrient changes have not broken it.
  • The signatures show what the earlier terms hide. The nutrient-extended member still has chlorophyll-a that is too seasonal (summer to winter ratio about 4 tolerances too high), TN that is too low (median about 2.7 and 90th percentile 2.6 tolerances low), ammonium and TP in the bottom water that are too low (90th percentiles about 1.9 and 1.8 tolerances low), and a nitrate variability that is too low (3.2 tolerances). The nutrient-extended search did reduce the TN shortfall, from about 4.9 to 2.7 tolerances.
  • Limits of this check. Only two members were scored, so this is a demonstration of the terms and not a new calibration. The FRP, nitrate and ammonium tolerances are wide or based on few years, and several signatures overlap (median, 90th percentile and variability of the same series), so the mean over signatures weights nutrients with many signatures more heavily than the others. sys_chl_tp_slope is left out of the terms because its value for a window of a few years is not stable.

10 Interpretation and limits

  • Zooplankton are a closure device here, not a measured pool. There are no zooplankton data, and the phosphorus and nitrogen content that make the budget work are far from textbook values (a C:P of about 130 for the best run, close to the algae themselves rather than a zooplankton value), because the module’s stoichiometry does not conserve mass in this build. A fix in the source (removing the divisions by 31 and 14 if the phytoplankton internal stores are already in mmol) would change these settings, and should be checked with the AED developers before the zooplankton parameters are taken as physical.
  • Group shares in the first search were not constrained by data. Only total chlorophyll-a was used, and the shares were set to the ordering described in the brief. The later search (Section 7) uses the BOPRC counts, which are 31 quarterly dates from 2003 to 2011.
  • Calm-water cyanobacteria blooms are missing. Whatever the buoyancy setting, the surface layer is too well mixed for floating to matter. A bloom that follows calm days would need either a stability-dependent growth or loss term, a lower mixing of the top metres (a model change in GLM), or a vertical resolution at the surface that this configuration does not have.
  • Total nitrogen stays 20 to 50% low, and nitrate is 30 to 60% high.
  • The window is short (2 years from two strong cyanobacteria summers), so a configuration may be tuned to them. The six-year check helps but is not independent of the search.
  • The first search was scored on succession targets I assumed (cyanobacteria at least 50% in summer, greens at least 35% all year). The observed data (Section 7) put cyanobacteria at about 37% in summer and greens at about 60% in summer to autumn, so the configurations of the first search should be treated as a first pass, not a calibration.
  • Model shares are shares of carbon biomass and the observations are shares of biovolume. Generic cell volumes are within a factor of two of Rotorua populations at best, and diatoms hold less carbon per unit volume than other groups, which may account for part of the diatom excess.
  • The observed series is short and quarterly (31 dates), so the curves and the hold-out check have wide uncertainty, and year-to-year differences are as large as the seasonal ones for diatoms and nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria.

11 Suggested next steps

  1. Take the best configurations into a full ES-MDA refit with the zooplankton on and the group-share targets as soft constraints, starting from member 46, because the nutrient budget and the sediment and alum parameters were calibrated without grazing.
  2. Ask the AED developers about the /31 and /14 factors in the grazing budget, and rebuild a binary with them corrected if they are confirmed as unit conversions that no longer apply.
  3. Try a calm-weather cyanobacteria mechanism (a wind- or stability-dependent growth multiplier or a buoyancy that depends on light and nutrient state) and test it on the 2-year window with the same score.
  4. Run a second search on zooplankton community and predation (held until this page has been reviewed). Add as free parameters or switches: which of the three groups are present (including rotifers only and cladocerans only), a separate food preference for each group, and the zooplankton temperature limits (Tstd_zoo, Topt_zoo, Tmax_zoo) so they can persist through winter. Also vary respiration and the excretion/faeces split, and treat the mortality rate as the stand-in for predation by fish since the model has no predator. Score zooplankton on a winter floor (not near zero from June to September) as well as the existing summer range, and keep the same nutrient, chlorophyll-a and succession terms.
  5. Extend the observation-based search (Section 7) before choosing a structure: several hundred more runs per structure, fewer free parameters (fix the well-constrained zooplankton settings), an extra term for the summer diatom excess, and a repeat of the comparison with the hold-out and calibration periods swapped, to see whether the ranking of the structures holds.
  6. Make PEST++ see the succession. calibration/phyto_groups/pest/calibrate_curve.R replaces the 25 raw group samples by the observed seasonal curve (monthly shares scaled by the observed monthly chlorophyll-a), so the succession is part of the PEST++ objective. It has not been run (about 5.5 h). Alternatively add the search score terms as a penalty.
  7. Add zooplankton or group-level data if any exist (even a few years of cell counts or pigment data would constrain the group shares and the zooplankton biomass).

## Summary {#sec-summary}

The calibrated Lake Rotorua model has no zooplankton (`aed_zooplankton` is commented out of the model list), so nothing grazes the phytoplankton and every phytoplankton parameter absorbed that missing loss. The result is a lake of diatoms and cyanobacteria only, with the greens gone. This investigation switches the zooplankton on and asks which settings give three coexisting groups with a plausible seasonal order (diatoms in the cool seasons, greens through the year, cyanobacteria in summer), total chlorophyll-a near the observations, and nutrients that stay close to the calibrated model.

**Method.** A 2-year window (2018-07 to 2020-06, plus the 540 day spin-up) of the stage j best member is run in about 20 s to 90 s per configuration. A one-at-a-time screen of 41 parameters (75 runs) was followed by an adaptive search of 11 generations of 90 runs (990 runs) over all 41 parameters at once. Each run is scored on chlorophyll-a, five nutrients, the seasonal group shares, zooplankton biomass and whether cyanobacteria favour calm days. No data exist for the individual groups or for zooplankton, so the group split and zooplankton biomass are set only to plausible ranges.

**What was found**

- **Zooplankton in this build do not conserve phosphorus or nitrogen.** Switching them on with their default phosphorus content raised surface TP from 0.02 to 0.11 g/m³ and FRP from 0.003 to 0.08 g/m³. In `aed_zooplankton.F90` the phosphorus and nitrogen eaten from phytoplankton are divided by 31 and 14 (a mg-to-mmol conversion), while the phytoplankton lose the full amount, so the zooplankton phosphorus content (`IPC_zoo`) and nitrogen content (`INC_zoo`) act as the closure terms of the budget. Every good configuration settles at an `IPC_zoo` between 0.003 and 0.008 mol P per mol C.
- **Grazing is what opens the niche for three groups.** With the zooplankton on, the greens and diatoms are held back and the cyanobacteria, which the zooplankton hardly eat, get the warm season.
- **The best configuration scores 2.7 against 3.6 for the hand-tuned case and 18.8 for the zooplankton-off model** (lower is better). It gives mean chlorophyll-a of 9.0 mg/m³ (8.3 observed in the window), diatoms 70% of biomass in September to November and cyanobacteria 44% in January to March.
- **The result holds on a six-year window (2016 to 2022).** The best configuration scores 2.2 there, against 4.0 for the hand-tuned case and 18.1 for the zooplankton-off model, with the same seasonal order (diatoms 68% in September to November, cyanobacteria 43% in January to March) and chlorophyll-a of 9.2 mg/m³ against 8.2 observed. The search never saw most of these years, but it was scored on the 2-year window inside this one.
- **Calm-water blooms are not reproduced.** In every configuration the cyanobacteria are no more abundant on stratified than on mixed summer days, and constant positive buoyancy or the motile option changes little, because the top 5 m of this polymictic lake is well mixed.
- **Total nitrogen is too low** in all of the good configurations (about 0.15 to 0.25 g/m³ against 0.31 observed).

## Method {#sec-method}

### Parameter space

Forty-one parameters (@tbl-space) cover the traits of the three phytoplankton groups, the grazing and mortality of three zooplankton groups, how much of each prey they take, and three switches (nitrogen fixation, motile cyanobacteria, copepods that eat other zooplankton). The temperature function is built from `T_opt` with fixed offsets for `T_std` and `T_max`, and combinations that the AED temperature solver cannot solve (which would stop GLM) are rejected before the run.

```{r}
#| label: tbl-space

knitr::kable(data.frame(Parameter = cons$param, Group = cons$grp, Low = signif(cons$lo_val, 3), High = signif(cons$hi_val, 3), Base = signif(cons$base_val, 3)), digits = 4)
```

@tbl-desc describes what each parameter does in AED and why it was included. Parameters that exist once per group are shown as one row with the prefix `cy_` (cyanobacteria), `gr_` (greens) or `di_` (diatoms); the zooplankton rates with `zr_`/`zm_` come in three versions (`rot`, `cla`, `cop` for rotifers, cladocerans and copepods).

| Parameter | Description | Why it is included |
|:-----------------|:---------------------------------|:---------------------------------|
| `cy_Rg`, `gr_Rg`, `di_Rg` | Maximum growth rate at 20 °C (1/d) of each phytoplankton group (`R_growth`). | Sets how fast each group can respond to favourable conditions, so it controls how quickly one group can overtake another. |
| `cy_To`, `gr_To`, `di_To` | Optimum temperature (°C) of the growth temperature function (`T_opt`). `T_std` and `T_max` are fixed offsets from it. | Temperature is the main driver of seasonal succession: diatoms in cool months, greens and cyanobacteria in warm months. |
| `cy_KP`, `gr_KP`, `di_KP` | Half-saturation concentration for phosphorus uptake (g/m³; `K_P`). | Lets the groups differ in competitive ability for P, the nutrient most linked to alum treatment and bloom control. |
| `cy_KN`, `gr_KN`, `di_KN` | Half-saturation concentration for nitrogen uptake (`K_N`). | Same role for N; total N is under-predicted, so N limitation may be mis-set. |
| `cy_IK`, `gr_IK`, `di_IK` | Light intensity at which photosynthesis saturates (W/m²; `I_K`). | Low-light tolerance separates diatoms (low value) from surface-adapted groups and shapes the depth distribution. |
| `cy_Rr`, `gr_Rr`, `di_Rr` | Respiration rate (1/d; `R_resp`). | Together with growth it sets the net growth rate, and with it winter persistence of each group. |
| `cy_wp`, `gr_wp`, `di_wp` | Vertical velocity (m/d; `w_p`): negative sinks, positive rises. | Cyanobacteria are buoyant and diatoms and greens sink, which affects where each group sits in the water column. |
| `zr_rot`, `zr_cla`, `zr_cop` | Maximum grazing rate of rotifers, cladocerans and copepods (`Rgrz_zoo`). | Grazing is the main loss term for phytoplankton and is needed to hold the groups close to observed biomass. |
| `zm_rot`, `zm_cla`, `zm_cop` | Mortality rate of each zooplankton group (`Rmort_zoo`). | Controls how large the zooplankton stock can grow, and so how much grazing pressure there is. |
| `z_Kgrz` | Prey concentration at which grazing is half its maximum (`Kgrz_zoo`). | Determines whether grazing is steady or only strong during blooms. |
| `z_Cmin` | Prey concentration below which grazing stops (`Cmin_grz_zoo`). | Gives a prey refuge so that groups at low biomass are not driven to zero. |
| `z_assim` | Fraction of grazed carbon assimilated (`fassim_zoo`). | Splits grazed material between zooplankton growth and recycling, which feeds back on nutrients. |
| `p_green`, `p_diatom`, `p_cyano` | Zooplankton preference for each phytoplankton group (`pref_*`). | Cyanobacteria are poor food and diatoms and greens are better, so the preferences shape which group is grazed down. |
| `z_IPC`, `z_INC` | Internal phosphorus and nitrogen to carbon ratio of zooplankton (`IPC_zoo`, `INC_zoo`). | Set how much nutrient is held in and released by zooplankton, so they affect TP, FRP and total N. |
| `nfix` | Switch: cyanobacteria fix nitrogen (`simNFixation`). | Tests whether N fixation is needed to sustain cyanobacteria when N is low. |
| `cy_motile` | Switch: cyanobacteria use the motile (buoyancy-regulating) option (`settling4`). | Tests whether buoyancy control explains summer cyanobacteria dominance. |
| `cop_zdiet` | Switch: copepods also eat rotifers, cladocerans and diatoms (`copepod_eats_zoop`). | Tests the effect of omnivory and top-down control within the zooplankton. |

### Score

The score adds four parts (all are about 1 when the model is within a stated tolerance):

1. **Chlorophyll-a:** log bias of the mean, the 90th percentile and the monthly pattern against the surface observations in the window.
2. **Nutrients:** mean absolute scaled log bias of TP, FRP, total nitrogen, nitrate and ammonium.
3. **Succession:** penalties when the diatom share in September to November and May to July averages below 0.5, the cyanobacteria share in January to March is below 0.5, the green share is below 0.35 over the year, and a large penalty for any group below 5% of biomass.
4. **Zooplankton:** penalty outside 3 to 30 mmol C/m³ in total, and for any group below 0.05.

The weighting is a judgement, not a result, so the ranking among near-equal scores should not be over-read.

- **The zooplankton refuge `Cmin_grz_zoo`** is the most powerful single control. At the low end the grazing removes nearly all the algae (mean chlorophyll about 1 mg/m³); at the high end chlorophyll-a rises by about 7 mg/m³.
- **Copepod and rotifer grazing rates** at the high end remove the cyanobacteria (even at a preference of 0.02) and so wreck the succession.
- **`IPC_zoo`** at the high end raises TP about seven-fold, and at the low end TP falls, so it must be tuned together with grazing.
- **Diatom respiration** at the high end wipes out the diatoms.
- **Green growth, light and nutrient half-saturation** decide whether the greens survive, but none of them alone brings the cyanobacteria to the target summer share.

- **Zooplankton almost disappear in winter.** In every configuration biomass is close to zero from about June to September and comes back in October to December, peaking at 10 to 30 mmol C/m³ in summer. Real zooplankton in a lake of this temperature persist through winter at low levels, so the winter collapse is probably a model artefact (temperature limits on growth and the minimum biomass floor), and it lets the diatoms bloom unchecked each spring.
- **Rotifers or cladocerans dominate; copepods are marginal** in all but one configuration (g010_042, where copepods reach 20 mmol C/m³ in spring). The search had no reason to favour copepods because no data say which group should dominate.
- **Grazers are 20 to 60% of phytoplankton biomass in summer** and well under 5% in winter (@fig-zoo-ratio), which is strong top-down pressure. There are no zooplankton data to say whether that is realistic for Rotorua.

### What was and was not varied

The search varied, for each of the three zooplankton groups, the maximum grazing rate and the mortality rate; it varied the half-saturation (`Kgrz_zoo`), the refuge (`Cmin_grz_zoo`) and the assimilation efficiency in common for all three; it varied how strongly all three prefer greens, diatoms and cyanobacteria (one preference each, shared by the three groups); and whether copepods eat the other zooplankton. Not varied: which groups are present (all three are always on, so there is no run with, for example, rotifers only or cladocerans only), a separate food preference for each group, the zooplankton temperature limits (`Tstd_zoo`, `Topt_zoo`, `Tmax_zoo`), respiration, the split of excretion and faeces, the oxygen threshold, and predation on the zooplankton. The model has no fish or other predator, so predation pressure on the zooplankton enters only through the linear mortality rate, and that is the only lever on top-down control of the grazers. The winter collapse and the unconstrained copepod diet suggest that the community composition and the temperature limits are the next things to test.

## Fit to the observed group data, 2003 to 2011 {#sec-obsfit}

The BOPRC phytoplankton counts (50 samples at sites 2 and 5, October 2003 to January 2011, mostly sampled in January, April, July and October) give the first observed group shares. They were used as follows.

- **Window:** one run from 2003-07 to 2011-06 per configuration. Calibration scores use 2003-07 to 2007-06 (17 sample dates) and hold-out scores use 2007-07 to 2011-06 (14 sample dates); both are recorded for every run, and only the calibration score steered the search.
- **Observed curves:** a first-harmonic seasonal curve per group, refit on the calibration samples only (so the hold-out years do not leak into the target), with a bootstrap spread as the tolerance (at least 0.07 in share). The model is scored on how far its monthly mean shares fall from these curves, in units of that tolerance (`z3` for the three-group scheme, `z4` for the four-group scheme with the cyanobacteria split).
- **Four model structures**, all with zooplankton on and the 15 to 21 parameters of the earlier best configuration free: `s3` (cyanobacteria, greens, diatoms); `s4c` (cyanobacteria split into a nitrogen-fixing and a non-fixing group); `s4g` (an added group of other greens, the AED cryptophyte column with a lower light requirement); `s5` (both).
- **Budget:** 240 runs (about 60 per structure). The first three rounds were scored against a diatom curve that was wrongly set to zero (a missing-value bug in my script). The model runs themselves were fine, so all 144 saved runs were re-scored offline and the search was resumed with two more rounds under the corrected score.
- **Selection:** structures are compared on the terms they share (`z3`, chlorophyll-a, nutrients and zooplankton, summed as the "common score"), because the search score adds a four-group term for the split structures.

**What the comparison shows**

- **The seasonal pattern can be matched.** In all four structures the model's monthly shares sit mostly inside the observed 95% band: diatoms dominate July to October, cyanobacteria peak in summer (January to March, about 20 to 50% in the model depending on the structure, against about 37% observed), and greens and other taxa are highest in summer to autumn. The earlier assumption that cyanobacteria should reach 50% in summer was too high, since the samples show them at about 25 to 35% of biovolume.
- **Hold-out years are about as well fitted as the calibration years** for the three-group, cyano-split and other-greens structures (share error on the sample dates about 0.20 to 0.23 against 0.22 to 0.28), so the seasonal order found in 2003 to 2007 holds in 2007 to 2011. Mean chlorophyll-a is within about 25% of the observed mean in both periods (9.0 to 12.3 against 11.3 mg/m³ in the calibration years, 8.9 to 11.0 against 12.0 in the hold-out years).
- **No structure is clearly better.** The common scores of the best runs are 4.1 (three groups), 4.3 (cyano split), 4.6 (other greens) and 4.5 (both), and the differences are small next to the run-to-run spread. With 60 runs per structure and 15 to 21 free parameters this is inside the noise of the search, so these results do not support the added complexity and also do not rule it out.
- **Other greens help the summer greens, but not the hold-out.** The `s4g` run reproduces the observed summer rise in greens (peak near 0.7 in December) that the three-group and cyano-split runs miss (about 0.35), but it overestimates greens by 0.21 in the hold-out years. The five-group run has the largest hold-out errors (greens +0.28, diatoms -0.34 on the sample dates).
- **A persistent diatom excess in summer.** Every structure keeps diatoms at about 25% of biomass from December to May, against about 5 to 15% observed.
- **Cyanobacteria split:** the split run overestimates the summer cyanobacteria (about 50%, at the top of the observed band), and the four-group score term (`z4` of 1.7 to 2.0) shows the nitrogen-fixing and non-fixing groups are not separated cleanly by the model (the observed peaks are about two months apart).

## Calibration with PEST++ and a nutrient-extended search {#sec-pest}

The random search tunes only the phytoplankton and zooplankton settings. Three further attempts tested whether a formal calibration, or freeing the nutrient processes, gives a better match to the nutrients as well as the succession. All three use the three-group structure (`s3`) with the zooplankton on, calibrate on 2003-07 to 2007-06 and hold out 2007-07 to 2011-06.

- **PEST++ run 1 (phytoplankton and zooplankton only).** `pestpp-ies` through aemetools, 39 free parameters (growth, light and nutrient half-saturation, respiration and settling for the three groups, and seven grazing settings for each of three zooplankton groups), 40 realisations, 5 iterations, 5 agents (333 min). Targets were the surface (0 to 5 m) total chlorophyll-a, the three group chlorophyll-a series from the BOPRC counts (25 samples per group in the calibration years, log1p), and FRP, TP, nitrate, ammonium and TN. The zooplankton parameters were switched on through the AEME configuration and calibrated through the same parameter table as the rest.
- **PEST++ run 2 (narrowed priors, nutrients freed).** The same set-up, started from the best nutrient-weighted member of the random search (`r5_s3_11`). The prior ranges of the phytoplankton and zooplankton settings were narrowed to the span of the 12 best search members (padded by 1.3; ±40% for settings the search never varied), and nine nutrient processes were freed within a factor of 4 (sediment ammonium, nitrate and FRP fluxes, nitrification, denitrification, DOM mineralisation, PON and POP hydrolysis, PO4 sorption). That gave 57 free parameters, with larger weights on TP, TN, FRP, nitrate and ammonium (357 min).
- **Nutrient-extended search.** The cross-entropy search of @sec-search, restarted from the 8 best earlier members with nine multipliers (0.25 to 4) on the same nutrient processes as extra search dimensions. 60 rounds of 12 runs (720 runs, 3 workers). It is scored by the same metrics as the earlier searches, so succession (`z3`), chlorophyll-a, nutrients and zooplankton all count.

The search score adds the seasonal succession shares, a chlorophyll-a high-percentile and seasonality term, and a zooplankton biomass range. - **The nutrient-extended search improved on all of the other approaches.** Its best member (`r45_s3_02`) scores 3.38 on the calibration years and 3.37 on the hold-out years, against 4.06 for the earlier best, with a nutrient penalty of 0.70 against 1.17 (re-scored; 1.32 in the original search) and 0.61 on hold-out. - **Succession did not improve.** `z3` is 1.65 against 1.57 for the earlier best, with biases of +0.11 (cyanobacteria), -0.17 (greens) and +0.06 (diatoms) on the sample dates, so the greens are still underestimated. The nutrient gain was not paid for in the shares, but it did not help them either.
- **What the nutrient parameters did.** The best member raises the sediment ammonium flux and denitrification by about 2.9 times and the nitrate flux by 1.1, and lowers the sediment FRP flux (0.30), nitrification (0.40), PON hydrolysis (0.41), POP hydrolysis (0.63) and PO4 sorption (0.56). - **Remaining gap.** The search plateaued: the last 14 rounds improved the score by about 0.04. Simulated ammonium rose to 0.025 g/m³ (0.004 to 0.017 in the other members), and I have not compared nitrate, ammonium, TP or FRP with the observed means. ## CSPS-based scoring: nutrient and alum dynamics {#sec-csps}

The CSPS framework (`calibration/csps_metrics.R`) scores a model by *signatures*: summary statistics computed in the same way on the observations and on the model, on the days that were sampled, with the interannual spread of the observed signature as the tolerance. Until now it covered mixing, anoxia, internal loading, chlorophyll-a and trophic state. Two sets were added, switched on with `extended = TRUE` so the sensitivity analysis and the step 1 to 3 calibrations are unchanged.

- **Nutrient dynamics** (`nut_*`, 23 signatures). For TP, TN, FRP, nitrate and ammonium in the surface 2 m: median, 90th percentile, coefficient of variation and the ratio of summer (December to April) to winter (June to August) medians, which catches a wrong seasonal cycle such as a winter nitrate pile-up that is not drawn down in summer. Also the TN:TP mass ratio, FRP as a fraction of TP and the dissolved inorganic N to FRP ratio.
- **Alum dynamics** (`alum_*`, 7 signatures). The response to the alum programme: surface TP, surface chlorophyll-a and summer bottom-water FRP after dosing (from 2008) over before dosing (before June 2006), the trend in annual mean TP after dosing, the change in surface pH, and the aluminium in the water (needs the 2011 to 2015 Al record, read from the GLM output with `csps_pair_al()`). The observed ratios are 0.44 for TP, 0.88 for chlorophyll-a and 0.19 for bottom FRP, so the observed TP fell by 56% and the summer bottom FRP by 81%.

The tolerances and observed values are in `calibration/csps_targets_ext.csv` (65 signatures, built by `csps_build_targets_ext.R`). Several tolerances are wide because the observed series are short (FRP has only 5 usable years; nitrate and ammonium are mostly near the detection limit).

**In the phytoplankton scoring** (`calibration/phyto_groups/csps_phyto.R`), each run is now also scored on the CSPS signatures of its calibration and hold-out windows. A term is the mean of min(|z|, 3) over its signatures, where z is the model minus observed signature, in tolerance units, on the paired days of that window: `csps_chl` (7 chlorophyll-a signatures), `csps_nut` (23), `csps_load` (9 bottom against surface signatures), `csps_sys` (trophic state) and `csps_alum` (the alum response across 2003 to 2011). The new `score2` keeps the succession, zooplankton and extinction terms of the earlier score and replaces the chlorophyll-a and nutrient terms by the CSPS terms. The earlier `score` is still recorded, so earlier runs stay comparable, and the nutrient-extended search can be steered by `score2` by setting `INV_SCORE=score2`; that search has not been re-run with it.

## Interpretation and limits {#sec-limits}

- **Zooplankton are a closure device here, not a measured pool.** There are no zooplankton data, and the phosphorus and nitrogen content that make the budget work are far from textbook values (a C:P of about 130 for the best run, close to the algae themselves rather than a zooplankton value), because the module's stoichiometry does not conserve mass in this build. A fix in the source (removing the divisions by 31 and 14 if the phytoplankton internal stores are already in mmol) would change these settings, and should be checked with the AED developers before the zooplankton parameters are taken as physical.
- **Group shares in the first search were not constrained by data.** Only total chlorophyll-a was used, and the shares were set to the ordering described in the brief. The later search (@sec-obsfit) uses the BOPRC counts, which are 31 quarterly dates from 2003 to 2011.
- **Calm-water cyanobacteria blooms are missing.** Whatever the buoyancy setting, the surface layer is too well mixed for floating to matter. A bloom that follows calm days would need either a stability-dependent growth or loss term, a lower mixing of the top metres (a model change in GLM), or a vertical resolution at the surface that this configuration does not have.
- **Total nitrogen stays 20 to 50% low**, and nitrate is 30 to 60% high.
- **The window is short** (2 years from two strong cyanobacteria summers), so a configuration may be tuned to them. The six-year check helps but is not independent of the search.
- **The first search was scored on succession targets I assumed** (cyanobacteria at least 50% in summer, greens at least 35% all year). The observed data (@sec-obsfit) put cyanobacteria at about 37% in summer and greens at about 60% in summer to autumn, so the configurations of the first search should be treated as a first pass, not a calibration.
- **Model shares are shares of carbon biomass and the observations are shares of biovolume.** Generic cell volumes are within a factor of two of Rotorua populations at best, and diatoms hold less carbon per unit volume than other groups, which may account for part of the diatom excess.
- **The observed series is short and quarterly** (31 dates), so the curves and the hold-out check have wide uncertainty, and year-to-year differences are as large as the seasonal ones for diatoms and nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria.

## Suggested next steps {#sec-next}

1. **Take the best configurations into a full ES-MDA refit** with the zooplankton on and the group-share targets as soft constraints, starting from member 46, because the nutrient budget and the sediment and alum parameters were calibrated without grazing.
2. **Ask the AED developers about the `/31` and `/14` factors** in the grazing budget, and rebuild a binary with them corrected if they are confirmed as unit conversions that no longer apply.
3. **Try a calm-weather cyanobacteria mechanism** (a wind- or stability-dependent growth multiplier or a buoyancy that depends on light and nutrient state) and test it on the 2-year window with the same score.
4. **Run a second search on zooplankton community and predation** (held until this page has been reviewed). Add as free parameters or switches: which of the three groups are present (including rotifers only and cladocerans only), a separate food preference for each group, and the zooplankton temperature limits (`Tstd_zoo`, `Topt_zoo`, `Tmax_zoo`) so they can persist through winter. Also vary respiration and the excretion/faeces split, and treat the mortality rate as the stand-in for predation by fish since the model has no predator. Score zooplankton on a winter floor (not near zero from June to September) as well as the existing summer range, and keep the same nutrient, chlorophyll-a and succession terms.
5. **Extend the observation-based search** (@sec-obsfit) before choosing a structure: several hundred more runs per structure, fewer free parameters (fix the well-constrained zooplankton settings), an extra term for the summer diatom excess, and a repeat of the comparison with the hold-out and calibration periods swapped, to see whether the ranking of the structures holds.
6. **Make PEST++ see the succession.** `calibration/phyto_groups/pest/calibrate_curve.R` replaces the 25 raw group samples by the observed seasonal curve (monthly shares scaled by the observed monthly chlorophyll-a), so the succession is part of the PEST++ objective. It has not been run (about 5.5 h). Alternatively add the search score terms as a penalty.
7. **Add zooplankton or group-level data** if any exist (even a few years of cell counts or pigment data would constrain the group shares and the zooplankton biomass).
